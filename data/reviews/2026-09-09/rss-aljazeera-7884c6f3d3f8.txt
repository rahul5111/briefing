═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Nepal is demanding climate justice and $20m in compensation following catastrophic floods on August 26 that killed more than 1,300 people and left over 5,000 missing. The disaster was triggered when a mountain glacier collapsed, sending a torrent of water and mud into Nepal’s Rasuwa and Nuwakot regions, as well as China’s Tibet region. 

Foreign Minister Shisir Khanal stated that Nepal is paying the ultimate price for a global crisis it did not create, emphasizing that support should be viewed as climate justice rather than charity. President Ramchandra Paudel echoed this appeal in an address to the nation on Monday, the same day Nepal observed a national day of mourning for the victims. Nepal’s Ministry of Finance and Ministry of Environment submitted a joint letter to the United Nations requesting $20m in damages from the UN Loss and Damage Fund, which currently faces a $2.8bn funding gap. 

Nepal contributes less than 0.1 percent of global greenhouse gas emissions, yet its glaciers have lost nearly one-third of their volume in about three decades. Preliminary estimates from Nepal’s National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority put property, housing, and infrastructure losses at $2.56bn. Securing compensation remains difficult, as rich nations resist setting a precedent for future climate liabilities, though a July 2025 International Court of Justice advisory opinion states that nations breaching climate obligations can be required to make financial reparations. Prime Minister Balendra Shah is scheduled to address the United Nations General Assembly on September 24, where the appeal for climate justice is expected to be a central focus.
