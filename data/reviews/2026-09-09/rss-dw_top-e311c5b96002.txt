═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

India is hosting the 18th BRICS summit in Delhi this weekend under the slogan Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability, which reinterprets the original acronym for the five founding members of Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa to include new members Egypt, Ethiopia, Indonesia, Iran, and the United Arab Emirates. The bloc now represents nearly half the world's population and about 40 percent of global GDP. Ahead of the gathering, MIT BRICS Lab director Mihaela Papa noted that India's geopolitical weight and relationships across competing camps make it an important bridge-builder. Russian President Vladimir Putin is expected to attend, while Chinese President Xi Jinping is widely anticipated but not yet formally confirmed by Beijing. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian will attend, whereas UAE President Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan is expected to send representatives, and Saudi Arabia's membership status remains somewhat ambiguous. For host India, the summit serves as a platform for bilateral meetings, including a scheduled Friday meeting between Putin and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, as well as an opportunity for Modi and Xi to speak face-to-face amid strained relations. Despite the scale of the bloc, Yale University lecturer Sushant Singh stated that concrete outcomes will likely be very limited because BRICS is not a priority for India, which is uncomfortable with any anti-western turn and prefers to focus on practical cooperation such as trade ties with the West. Papa expects progress this weekend specifically in trade, monetary cooperation involving alternative financial architecture for currency exchange, and agriculture, noting that India is keen for a coordinated approach at the World Trade Organization. However, Singh maintains that internal contradictions, including a growing number of autocracies and tense bilateral relations among members, will continue to make consensus difficult.
