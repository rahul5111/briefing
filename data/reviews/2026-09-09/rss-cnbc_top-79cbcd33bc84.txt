═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The Treasury Department announced Wednesday that it will buy back up to $6 billion of government debt in an operation scheduled for Thursday between a 20-minute window concluding at 2 p.m. ET. The announcement triples the normal $2 billion buyback operation for already-issued securities, following a statement on August 19 by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent that the department would at least double the normal amount. Treasury also stated that future operations will be at least $4 billion. 

The measure targets 10- and 20-year notes to keep government debt markets liquid and attempts to put a lid on Treasury yields that hit highs not seen since before the 2008 global financial crisis. Following the announcement, market reaction was negative with Treasury yields rising further. The benchmark 10-year issue hit 4.841% around 11:30 a.m. ET, the 20-year climbed to 5.314%, and the 30-year bond rose 5 basis points to 5.307%. 

Higher yields have coincided with government debt passing $40 trillion, inflation fears from tariffs and the Iran war, and crude oil topping $100 a barrel. Publicly held debt sits at $31.8 trillion, up 8.2%, with Treasury issuance jumping 11.8% from 2025. 

The move drew criticism for breaking predictable Treasury processes and failing to match speculation that buybacks could reach up to $10 billion. "Hank Paulson's bazooka this is not," said bond fund manager Mark Spindel, chief investment officer at Potomac River Capital. "And it took an act of Congress in that crisis." Stanley Druckenmiller, head of Duquesne Family Office, criticized the defense of prices in a Wall Street Journal op-ed, writing, "Once markets believe Treasury is defending a price, every rise in yields becomes a test of official resolve, and the operations must grow to survive the tests."
