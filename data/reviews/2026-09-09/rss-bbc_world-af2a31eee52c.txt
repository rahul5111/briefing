═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

US forces struck five Iranian oil tankers on Tuesday and sank one, following two separate Iranian attempts to target an American warship. US Central Command stated that four of the targeted vessels were in the Gulf of Oman and linked to the Iranian Revolutionary Guards Corps, while a fifth was struck near Kharg Island, a major terminal handling ninety percent of Iran's crude oil exports. 

In retaliation, Iran launched twenty missiles at a US base in Jordan, where the Jordanian Armed Forces reported shooting down eighteen while two landed in unpopulated areas. The Revolutionary Guards also stated they attacked two US vessels, eight oil tankers, and ten non-compliant vessels in the Strait of Hormuz. No American troops were harmed during the attacks, which followed an earlier incident where Iran claimed to have seized a malfunctioning uncrewed US submarine. US Navy Captain Tim Hawkins noted the recovered underwater drone was an older model carrying no classified equipment.

The latest exchanges follow more than six months of escalation after initial US and Israel attacks on Iran on February 28. The US military justified Tuesday's maritime strikes by identifying the targeted tankers as part of a multi-billion-dollar shadow network funding the IRGC and regional proxies. Addressing reporters in Colombia, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said, Iran continues to try to hit US naval ships. And, for every time they do that or try to do that, they're going to lose tankers. And I think you'll see that again today.

Regional instability widened on Tuesday as Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement launched drone and missile attacks against energy and civilian infrastructure in Saudi Arabia, injuring seventy-three people and causing temporary halts at oil facilities. The growing conflict pushed oil markets higher in Asia trading, with benchmark Brent crude rising 1.5 percent to $99.35 a barrel and US-traded oil increasing 1.3 percent to $94.23.
