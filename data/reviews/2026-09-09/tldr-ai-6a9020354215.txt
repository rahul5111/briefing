═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Magic announced that its pretraining recipe is now more than ten times more compute-efficient than leading open-weight base models, matching the DeepSeek V4 Pro Base using roughly fifty times fewer floating-point operations. According to the company, this initial efficiency equals about half of GPT-3's pretraining compute, or roughly $0.5 million on GB200 hardware. After scaling up ten times further at a cost of around $4 million, the company reported outperforming all publicly available open base models on perplexity evaluations. By comparison, Magic stated that under DeepSeek V4 Pro's recipe, training a model of equivalent capability would cost more than $100 million. To measure these capabilities without prompt sensitivity, the team tracked bits-per-byte loss on held-out data and evaluated models across 167 domains. The company's future roadmap focuses on scaling long-horizon reinforcement learning, developing alignment techniques with robust theoretical properties, and releasing its upcoming models.
