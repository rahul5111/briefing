═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

San Jose startup Kepler Computing has emerged from seven years in stealth mode with $468 million in funding and up to $245 million committed by the US Department of Commerce in July. Founded in 2018 by physicists and computer scientists, the company has developed a 3D stacking approach and proprietary material for high-bandwidth memory and high-speed cache memory that bypasses expensive extreme ultraviolet lithography. 

The startup’s technology increases density without EUV and works within existing semiconductor fabrication plants, allowing a fab to be converted in eight months instead of the typical 24 months. Kepler has raised capital from backers including GlobalFoundries, Intel Capital, AMD Ventures, Baillie Gifford, and Bill Gates through Gates Frontier. It has partnered with GlobalFoundries, which invested $50 million and hosts testing at facilities in Singapore and Burlington, Vermont. 

Kepler plans to ship its first HBM chip samples later this year, ramp up production in Singapore next year, and begin US chip production in 2028, having run its technology on roughly 2,000 wafers to date. 

For a long time our thinking was that we’d work on SRAM first and then follow up with DRAM and HBM, says Debo Olaosebikan, Kepler cofounder and chief executive officer. But with the launch of ChatGPT and the explosion of demand for an HBM alternative, we started working on our HBM road map. Now we’re making SRAM and HBM in parallel.

The approach aims to ease the global memory-chip shortage by allowing manufacturers to increase memory supply within existing facilities, avoiding the $20 billion to $40 billion cost of building new ones, provided the startup can successfully scale its technology to millions of devices.
