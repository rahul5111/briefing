═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

An Advance Passenger Information System database containing over 220 million traveler and crew records was exposed online due to a chain of security misconfigurations, according to researchers at Kinryū Labs. The researchers discovered the exposed Elasticsearch cluster on June 3 while surveying databases as part of research into ransomware activity. The cluster, which was hosted in Viettel-assigned IP space in Hanoi, held approximately 107 GB of data across 29 indices. The records included 210,318,069 passenger entries and 10,465,631 crew entries spanning from January 2017 to April 2026. The exposed information featured names, dates of birth, sex, nationalities, passport numbers, document expiration dates, issuing countries, flight numbers, airlines, departure and destination airports, seat assignments, baggage references, and flight times. Sample records reviewed by BleepingComputer included travelers from South Korea, China, Canada, and New Zealand, among other nationalities. Kinryū Labs accessed the database by chaining two misconfigurations, overcoming an initial HTTP 401 unauthorized response through a cloud-based path that accepted default credentials. The internet intelligence platform FOFA first recorded the host and port in October 2022, though the exact duration of the exposure remains unknown. Kinryū Labs reported the vulnerability to Vietnamese authorities, airlines, and national computer emergency response teams beginning June 3, and access to the database was remediated on June 8 with the help of Singapore Airlines' security team. Vietnamese authorities did not respond to requests for comment, and it remains unclear whether malicious actors accessed or copied the data before it was secured, as researchers found no server logs, ransom notes, or unfamiliar indices.
