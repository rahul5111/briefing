═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration announced new agency priorities last week aimed at cutting bureaucracy and prioritizing fishing communities. The reforms serve as an operational roadmap to fulfill President Donald Trump’s Restoring American Seafood Competitiveness executive order, which was signed in April 2025. The changes follow letters sent to regional fisheries councils earlier in the summer directing them to dismantle restrictions in favor of more permissive fishing procedures. The agency plans to open marine national monuments to commercial fishing operations, scale back critical habitat designations to focus on the downstream economics of conservation, and weaken decades of peer-reviewed science. Andrea Treece, senior attorney in the Oceans Program at Earthjustice, criticized the direction in an email to Inside Climate News, stating, “If you asked AI to come up with a plan to hollow out marine ecosystems and impoverish coastal communities, it would look a lot like the one NOAA just put out. The agency is taking a sharp turn away from science.” Treece added that it is inexcusable to make it easier to destroy marine habitats and commit endangered species to extinction for short-term profits.
