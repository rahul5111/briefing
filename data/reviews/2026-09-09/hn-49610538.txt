═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

LibreOffice version 26.8, released on August 26th, has become the software’s most popular update, with its installer downloaded more than one million times in a single week, excluding updates through Linux distribution repositories. The surge in popularity follows the software's explicit statement that it does not include generative AI features due to privacy concerns. A day after celebrating the download milestone, The Document Foundation, which manages LibreOffice, published a post titled Yes, no AI is now a feature, written by Italo Vignoli. The foundation stated it does not reject artificial intelligence out of hand, but declared that the technology does not yet meet an exhaustive list of required principles for default inclusion. Those principles include user-controlled execution, no unauthorized content leaving the computer, zero telemetry, no dependency on a single vendor, no compromises on file formats, and entirely optional use. The Document Foundation currently recommends community plugins for users wanting to integrate AI into LibreOffice. According to the foundation, its stance has almost nothing to do with the technology itself, but rather addresses how competitors use built-in AI assistants to justify price increases and keep documents within their own infrastructure to support subscription models.
