═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Researchers led by Columbia assistant professor of applied physics Michele Simoncelli have published a new physics-aware benchmark in Nature Communications that evaluates machine learning models predicting the thermal and mechanical properties of materials. Developed alongside University of Cambridge colleagues Balázs Póta, Paramvir Ahlawat, and Gábor Csányi, the benchmark addresses a major flaw in machine-learning interatomic potentials, which can run one thousand times faster than traditional approaches by bypassing explicit solutions to the Schrödinger equation. While many models show similar accuracy in predicting energy and crystal stability, the researchers found they often distort the small, fast quantum-mechanical thermal vibrations of atoms, sometimes predicting macroscopic thermal conductivity accurately only because microscopic errors accidentally cancel out. As first author Balázs Póta explained, models had previously been compared mainly on energy performance, which missed potential errors in forces that determine atomic dynamics. The benchmarking effort, which began about two years ago, compared several models against traditional quantum-mechanical calculations for a set of more than one hundred crystalline materials. The benchmark has already been adopted by research groups and companies including Meta, Microsoft, Radical AI, and Orbital Materials, and has been incorporated into the Matbench Discovery leaderboard. This adoption matters because thermal management is critical for microchips, batteries, quantum computers, jet engines, spacecraft heat shields, and nuclear reactors, and models that get underlying physics wrong can discard good material options and promote bad ones.
