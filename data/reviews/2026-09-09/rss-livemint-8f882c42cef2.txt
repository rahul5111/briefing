═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A viral ChatGPT trend prompting users to upload selfies to transform them into 1980s retro-style photographs has raised privacy questions regarding how personal images and associated data are handled. According to WIRED Middle East, uploading a facial photograph places a copy of personal data that can help identify an individual onto an AI service, complete with metadata including when the picture was taken, the device used, and its location. When uploaded, the photo becomes part of the user interaction and is stored with conversation data. Standard ChatGPT conversations are saved until deleted by the user, and data may remain on OpenAI systems for up to 30 days after deletion. Certain information can be retained longer for legal or security reasons, or if it is de-identified. While OpenAI states that its business products and API do not use customer data for model training by default, data handling rules for personal accounts differ. Services can also collect basic metadata such as IP addresses, device types, operating systems, and general geographic locations. Experts advise users to avoid uploading photos of others, particularly children, without permission, and recommend disabling model training, using temporary modes, and deleting unnecessary chats and files to enhance privacy.
