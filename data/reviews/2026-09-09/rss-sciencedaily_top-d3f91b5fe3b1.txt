═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

New research published on September 8, 2026, by Griffith University shows that semaglutide, marketed as Ozempic and Wegovy, is linked to a 21% lower risk of psychiatric hospitalization in people with bipolar disorder. Led by Professor Mark Taylor from Griffith's School of Medicine and Dentistry, the study analyzed nationwide Swedish data covering nearly 15,000 individuals with bipolar disorder over a 15-year period from 2009 to 2024. People taking semaglutide had significantly lower rates of psychiatric hospitalization compared to periods when they were not taking the medication. Professor Taylor stated that the findings add to growing evidence that GLP-1 receptor agonists may have benefits beyond diabetes and obesity treatment and could represent a promising new avenue for bipolar research. The researchers suggest that GLP-1 signaling may help protect the brain by reducing inflammation, cellular stress, and other biological processes associated with bipolar disorder, potentially contributing to greater mood stability and a lower risk of relapse. However, other medications in the same class, specifically liraglutide and dulaglutide, were not associated with a reduced risk of psychiatric hospitalization, indicating the potential mental health benefit does not extend across the entire drug class. The findings were published in Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica, and researchers hope the results will now be examined in a randomized controlled trial to provide stronger evidence regarding psychiatric outcomes.
