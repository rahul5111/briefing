═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Battery installations in the US reached a record 20.2 gigawatt-hours of new capacity in the second quarter of 2026, according to a report published by Benchmark Mineral Intelligence and the Solar Energy Industries Association. That capacity is enough to supply the daily electricity needs of about 700,000 homes. The surge puts the country on a trajectory to install 71 gigawatt-hours of batteries in 2026, representing a 20% increase over last year, driven by cheaper batteries and the need for storage as renewables like solar and wind are added to the grid. Massive utility-scale systems drove the record quarter, including seven new gigascale installations with a capacity of over one gigawatt-hour each. Shan Tomouk, energy storage and energy lead for Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, stated that it really came down to a handful of big projects. Behind-the-meter batteries also grew, with data centers leading the commercial sector by making up about three-quarters of new batteries. However, residential batteries saw a sharp slowdown, with home installations projected to drop by 16% in 2026 compared to last year. Tomouk noted that this drop happened largely because a tax credit subsidizing home battery systems ended in 2025, though home installations should recover by the end of the decade, and nonresidential tax credits have largely survived. Isshu Kikuma, an energy storage analyst at BloombergNEF, described the sector as one of the strong spots in US energy right now. Nearly all the systems coming online use cells made in China. Tariffs and tax credits taking effect this year that require projects to limit reliance on Chinese imports are pushing the industry toward domestic production, though US factories likely will not meet demand until at least 2030, which could cause prices to tick up.
