Aporva Baxi, co-founder and executive creative director of the global brand agency DixonBaxi, warns that modern design is becoming dangerously narrow and uniform due to the accumulation of safe, reasonable decisions and the rise of democratized creative tools. While current design work is frequently competent and professional, Baxi argues that this gravitational pull toward the middle ground drains originality and replaces genuine emotional impact with numbness disguised as professionalism. To combat this flattening effect, Baxi shares how DixonBaxi stepped back from five safe creative directions during a project for the music technology company Roli to discover a simpler, more human symbol. Furthermore, the agency combats creative stagnation through an internal initiative called SuperFutures, which gives every staff member two to four weeks to build a project of their own choosing with no client or brief. Baxi states that originality and distinctiveness require disrupting familiar recipes, embracing intuition before intention, and holding onto a sense of wonder rather than settling for work that is merely good.
