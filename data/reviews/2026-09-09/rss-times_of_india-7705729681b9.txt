═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A satellite imagery analysis reviewed by CNN reveals a sharp surge in construction activity in 2026 at Iran’s Pickaxe Mountain site, a suspected nuclear facility buried beneath a granite mountain near the Natanz complex about 140 miles south of Tehran. The analysis, conducted by the Center for Strategic and International Studies and based on six years of imagery, shows the site has shifted from active excavation toward internal construction, exterior reinforcement, road paving, and hardening tunnel portal accesses. Analysts indicated the facility is likely designed as a safe haven or housing for Iran's nuclear programme, serving as a potential centrifuge assembly plant, enrichment facility, or location for uranium metal conversion, though it is not yet capable of enrichment work. US President Donald Trump has repeatedly threatened to strike the heavily fortified location, stating last week that they may hit Pickaxe very soon and noting that they know everyone moving at the site. The US military maintains operational plans for strikes against Pickaxe Mountain using some of the largest bombs in its arsenal, though the granite mountain presents a difficult target. To address deeply buried sites, the US Defence Threat Reduction Agency signed an emergency $1.2 million contract on February 27 for a live-fire test at White Sands Missile Range in New Mexico, and the military is developing a next-generation penetrator bomb. These developments unfold amid an escalating six-month war between the US and Iran that has involved shipping attacks near the Strait of Hormuz, ballistic missile strikes in Jordan, and rising energy prices pushing Brent crude above $100 a barrel.
