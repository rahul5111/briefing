═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

LIV Golf filed for bankruptcy protection in the US on Tuesday in an attempt to restructure for a new era next year, leaving it owing at least $45m to 14 current and former players. Court documents show the breakaway league estimates its assets at $100m to $500m and its liabilities at between $500m and $1bn, following an outlay of $5bn that split professional golf after its 2021 launch. Star players including Bryson DeChambeau and Jon Rahm can now opt to leave rather than being obliged to see out their contracts in LIV 2.0, which aims to launch next year with a scaled-back business model featuring tournaments worth around £7m, expanded fields from 57 to 75 players, Monday qualifying events, and halfway cuts. In response to the filing, the DP World Tour stated it has had a high volume of inbound interest from LIV players and is exploring ways to accommodate those who leave, provided they are free from third-party contractual restrictions and abide by membership regulations. A spokesperson for the DP World Tour said given the high volume of inbound interest and enquiries received in recent weeks, they are exploring the terms on which they are willing to accommodate players, and their tournament committee has approved the process. While the DP World Tour offers a potential pathway back, returning to the mainstream tours remains complicated. Brooks Koepka previously quit LIV in December 2025 and returned to the PGA Tour in 2026 after making a $5m charitable donation, forgoing FedExCup Bonus scheme payments, and accepting ineligibility for the player equity programme until 2030. However, PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp indicated last month there are no current plans to run that returning member programme again. Other players like Patrick Reed have had to earn their place back through the DP World Tour's Race to Dubai top 10. Rahm said this week he was prepared to fulfil his contract with LIV 1.0 with no mention of any commitment to the intended new iteration, and is expected to depart alongside other players seeking a way back to the mainstream circuits.
