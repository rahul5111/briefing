═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Corps stated Wednesday that it struck two American vessels and eight oil tankers in the Gulf in retaliation for the United States destroying five Iranian tankers. The IRGC claimed the targeted vessels attempted to cross an area of the Hormuz Strait designated as forbidden and unsafe, according to Tasnim News Agency. U.S. Central Command reported that the U.S. military destroyed five Iranian crude oil tankers on Tuesday after the IRGC fired ballistic missiles at an American warship twice over the previous two days. The targeted U.S. warship evaded the attacks and no American personnel were harmed. Centcom stated that U.S. forces struck four tankers in the Gulf of Oman, specifically the M/T Kaviz, M/T Charminar, M/T Horizon 1, and M/T Riesco, along with the M/T Derya near Iran's Kharg Island. American forces directed the crews to abandon ship before rendering the vessels inoperable. This exchange follows the U.S. military's destruction of three Iranian tankers on Saturday in response to the Guard firing ballistic missiles at an aircraft carrier and a guided-missile destroyer. The escalating conflict in the critical oil corridor has pushed Brent crude to near $100 a barrel. In a separate incident on Tuesday, Centcom addressed an unmanned submersible that Iran claimed to have seized in the Strait of Hormuz. U.S. Navy Captain Tim Hawkins stated that an underwater drone operated by U.S. forces had malfunctioned more than a day prior while surveying regional waters. Hawkins dismissed the captured craft as an older, defective model that carried no classified sonar or radar equipment and collected no sensitive data. Iranian media outlets identified the sub as a Dive-LD autonomous undersea vehicle developed by Anduril, which costs $2.5 million each according to a 2024 DefenseScoop report. An Anduril spokesperson noted that the Dive-LD is an attritable autonomous system designed to operate in dangerous environments where loss is expected.
