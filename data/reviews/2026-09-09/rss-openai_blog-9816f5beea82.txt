═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

1Password has integrated Codex across its software delivery lifecycle, resulting in a measured engineering productivity improvement of 20.9% for its core user cohort and a 10.9% reduction in median pull request cycle time. 

The deployment yielded several operational gains. For a complex production issue spanning more than 10 microservices, investigation time dropped from about two hours to between 5 and 20 minutes. For a modeled cohort of 50 Codex users, 1Password estimates an annual engineering capacity value of approximately $784,000, representing a 553% return on investment. The company projects that scaling to 100 consistent users could raise the modeled annual capacity value to approximately $3.1 million. 

Nancy Wang, CTO at 1Password, noted the impact on feature development speed, stating that Codex allows teams to go straight from an idea to a working prototype in production. Previously, projects required breaking work down into separate sprints and assigning them to scrum teams. 

The tooling touches multiple phases of development, including planning, multi-stack implementation using Rust and TypeScript, pull request reviews, and testing. To maintain security, 1Password uses secret references instead of plaintext credentials in repositories, ensuring sensitive data never enters the model context. 

Following these engineering results, leadership is expanding Codex access to non-technical functions like finance and marketing to enable broader internal tool building.
