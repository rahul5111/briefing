═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The September 6 collapse of a five-storey paying guest accommodation in south Delhi's Satya Niketan, which killed seven people, has exposed widespread safety risks in student housing across the capital. According to the police FIR, building owner Hariram Gupta and his brother constructed four unauthorized floors on the 40-year-old structure to increase rental income, generating more than Rs 4 lakh monthly. Investigators found that a load-bearing wall was allegedly removed while two ground-floor shops were being merged, and the building had been leased to PG operator Hostel Daze in August 2025. Hariram Gupta was apprehended following raids across Gurgaon using technical surveillance and a look-out circular. 

The tragedy has deeply rattled students and young professionals who rely on PGs near Delhi University and employment hubs. Sneha Mishra, a resident from Deoghar, Jharkhand, told reporters that the incident has shattered her sense of security, saying, what if the building we trust with our lives is not safe? Other residents highlighted how tight budgets force them to compromise on safety, noting that management often fails to show regulatory documents or address basic structural concerns. In response to the collapse, Delhi Urban Development Minister Ashish Sood announced a comprehensive draft policy to regulate PGs. The proposed framework mandates municipal and police verification, operating licenses, CCTV cameras with 30-day storage, night guards for PGs housing 15 or more students, and a Rent Regulation Committee to set area-specific rent bands.
