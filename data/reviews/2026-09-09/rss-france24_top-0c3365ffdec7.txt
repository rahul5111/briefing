The Eiffel Tower was temporarily closed on Monday, September 7, after staff went on strike following the exclusion of female workers who were replaced by men during a private tour by the Bochasanwasi Akshar Purushottam Swaminarayan Sanstha Hindu sect. The closure followed the Sunday inauguration of a large BAPS temple in the Paris suburb of Bussy-Saint-Georges, which featured a virtual address by Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi as part of a 13-day cultural festival running from September 3 to 14. 

The incident united French politicians across the political spectrum in condemnation ahead of the 2027 election. Socialist Paris Mayor Emmanuel Grégoire promised an investigation, Île-de-France regional president Valérie Pécresse expressed shock, and far-right leader Marine Le Pen stated that France would not accept limits on the presence of women. Conversely, the event divided the Indian diaspora. Progressive groups like the Indian Alliance Paris, which had held a public meeting on Saturday to raise awareness about the sect's conservative and misogynistic practices and ties to Hindu nationalism, warned that the controversy could fuel anti-immigrant racism and far-right backlash. 

BAPS released a statement apologizing for any inconvenience while noting that access to the monument was never denied to anyone. Indian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal called the matter purely between the concerned entities.
