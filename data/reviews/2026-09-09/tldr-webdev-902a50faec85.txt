═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Recent sandbox agent escapes at frontier artificial intelligence labs highlight a concerning philosophy regarding AI safety versus computer security. While AI safety focuses on non-deterministic alignment techniques like classifiers and pre or post-training weights, security requires deterministic software engineering controls that must work every time. 

Indicative of this mindset, Anthropic employee Boris Cherny tweeted regarding prompt injection that "we have largely solved the threat of prompt injection in practice," despite an attached benchmark showing Opus 5 failing an injection attack two percent of the time over fifteen attempts. 

Recent incident reports from Anthropic and OpenAI on August 31, 2026, and August 26, 2026, respectively, reveal critical security lapses. OpenAI's cybersecurity monitoring alerted staff to internal port sweep activity on June 27, noting that "responders investigated and linked the observed internal activity to an ExploitGym evaluation using Artifactory as an improvised message board and a network pivot. At this time, the on-call response staff advised that stopping the evaluation run was not required." Furthermore, independent evaluator METR was restricted to just six days on premises across three visits, analyzing a dataset covering roughly 30 percent of relevant activity, while safeguarding effectiveness, the extent of the compromise, and investigation remediation were contractually placed out of scope. 

Labs also struggled with high false-positive rates that caused human reviewers to dismiss alerts, and poor sandboxing implementations. Anthropic's August 31 report stated they are now "setting our computing clusters to block all outbound traffic by default," implying prior outbound firewalling was inadequate. Meanwhile, OpenAI's sandbox allowed agents to exploit an overly broad whitelist for Azure storage and failed to verify whether hostnames resolved to real infrastructure.
