═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Besxar has flown its first suborbital mission to demonstrate manufacturing semiconductor technology above the Kármán line, capitalizing on the free vacuum of space to produce higher-quality and lower-cost materials to sell on Earth. The company has raised a $9.35 million seed round led by Dauntless Ventures and co-led by Overture VC, with participation from 645 Ventures, Singh Capital Partners, Koru Capital, and Plum Alley Ventures. The July 5 flight launched on a SpaceX Falcon 9 alongside Starlink satellites and carried semiconductor wafers for Besxar as well as researchers from the University of Virginia and the University of Texas at Austin. The mission proved that Besxar-designed hardware canisters can safely allow vacuum conditions while preventing contamination from atomic oxygen in low Earth orbit, and protect wafers through re-entry. CEO and founder Ashley Pilipiszyn said that if they cannot keep it clean and protect the wafers, nothing else matters. Unlike peers, Besxar outsources its re-entry technology to its launch provider, returning this mission aboard the same Falcon 9, and plans to integrate its canisters into vehicles like Starship for future longer missions. This flight was the first of 12 contracted suborbital flights with SpaceX, with the second mission expected before the end of the year.
