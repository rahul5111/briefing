═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Bulgarian authorities are investigating a fire that broke out Friday night at a munitions storage facility operated by arms maker EMCO near the village of Tsareva Livada. The incident follows a similar occurrence at another EMCO site in August and comes as the company supplies munitions to Ukraine. The full extent of the damage remains unknown, but employees were evacuated, the area was cordoned off, and no casualties have been reported. Local police stated there is currently no danger to the population, and EMCO has ruled out human error. On Saturday, Bulgarian Interior Minister Ivan Demerdzhiev announced an investigation to review previous fires at warehouses belonging to EMCO and other companies to check for a potential connection or pattern. EMCO production facilities manager Slavcho Dimiev confirmed the site stores ammunition, including a small quantity of medium-caliber rounds, and told reporters that additional security measures should be considered. Dimiev said, if it is concluded that arms facilities have been targeted, it is an attack on the national interest. NATO member Bulgaria has supported Ukraine since Russia invaded on February 24, 2022, and EMCO has faced repeated sabotage. The company is run by Emilian Gebrev, who survived an assassination attempt that authorities attribute to Russian operatives. In 2024, Bulgaria issued arrest warrants for six Russian nationals linked to the destruction of EMCO depots storing munitions for export to Ukraine and Georgia between 2011 and 2020. Prosecutors previously connected those incidents to the attempt on Gebrev and 2014 depot explosions in the Czech Republic. Friday's fire mirrors other recent attacks on European arms makers supplying Ukraine, including an August 13 explosion at the KNDS Ammo Italy facility outside Rome and September 3 incendiary devices thrown near the Munich headquarters of Rohde and Schwarz. Additionally, US and German intelligence uncovered a 2024 Russian plot to assassinate Armin Papperger, CEO of Germany's Rheinmetall. Russia has denied involvement in these instances, calling the accusations European warmongering.
