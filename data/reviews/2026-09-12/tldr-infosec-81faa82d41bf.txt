Google announced on Wednesday that it will invest at least 13 billion euros, or 15.1 billion dollars, into AI infrastructure in Finland through 2028. The capital will fund data centers and supporting energy projects, marking the tech giant's largest single investment in Europe. As part of the buildout, Google signed a 22-year life extension power purchase agreement with Finnish energy company Fortum, whose stock rose 11% following the announcement. Google also stated it will work with Fortum to explore business models for potential new nuclear reactors at the Loviisa site in Southern Finland. Finland has increasingly attracted hyperscalers due to its available land and power supplies, which remain constrained across the rest of Europe. Ruth Porat, president and chief investment officer of Alphabet and Google, said in a statement that the project pairs technical infrastructure expansion with new energy capacity and grid enhancements. Prime Minister Petteri Orpo noted that the collaboration will deliver lasting economic and innovative benefits for the country.
