═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Several major companies are cutting their artificial intelligence spending by transitioning from closed, proprietary models to cheaper open-weight alternatives run on inference services. Uber blew through its annual AI budget in the first three months of the year, prompting engineering teams to optimize spending through open-weight models, optimized model selection, and prompt caching. As a result, Uber cut the cost per AI request by 34 percent and the cost per AI session by 52 percent, keeping its overall AI costs flat since March despite significantly higher usage. Pinterest reported even steeper reductions, achieving cost per transaction at less than eight percent of the cost of comparable closed proprietary models by utilizing compact fit-for-purpose models and open-source models post-trained in its own secure cloud environment. Pinterest CEO William Ready stated, When we leverage open source models, such as with Pinterest Assistant, we are seeing superior performance for our use cases when compared to closed third-party models because we are able to post-train open models on our highly unique data. Similarly, AT&T reduced its AI bill by 56 percent after swapping workloads over to open-source models for less intense tasks like generating summaries of previously submitted code, while recording only a two percent decrease in output quality. According to data from Ramp, AI spending among top businesses declined by 10 percent in August as companies increasingly adopt these cost-optimization strategies.
