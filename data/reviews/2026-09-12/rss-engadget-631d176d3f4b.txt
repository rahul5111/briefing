═══ 00_KEY_POINTS ═══

1. Google Maps determines venue busyness by tracking aggregate device density from opted-in users.
2. Google analyzes historical data from previous weeks to calculate average visit durations and wait times.
3. Google Maps displays a graph indicating when businesses may be slow or have long queues.
4. Google also calculates busyness for entire outdoor areas and high-traffic zones using location data.
5. Google ensures all collected data remains private and never reveals individual user identities.
6. Users can disable the Google Timeline feature to opt out of the company data collection process.
7. Knowing crowd levels helps users plan their visits and decide when or where to go.

═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Google Maps determines how busy a venue or area is by analyzing an aggregate of anonymous data supplied by random users who have opted into the Google Timeline feature. By tracking device density at specific establishments like restaurants, hotels, stores, and coffee shops, Google compares current numbers against typical traffic benchmarks to calculate popular times, usual wait times, and how long people stay based on visitor patterns from the previous few weeks. For entire areas, such as outdoor events, tourist towns, or festival locations, Google combines visitor data from various locations within that locale, excluding private residences, to provide busy area charts when sufficient data is available. According to Google, the collected data always remains private and never reveals individual identities or the total number of people at a location. Users can opt out of contributing to this data collection at any time by turning off Google Timeline.
