═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay on Saturday proposing a voluntary slowdown in the development of advanced artificial intelligence capabilities. The plan brought together industry figures, with OpenAI chief Sam Altman and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk throwing their support behind the proposal. 

Amodei’s three-step plan begins with granting third-party evaluators employee-level access to verify safety practices and report incidents, a step Anthropic has already unilaterally committed to. The second step encourages leading AI companies in democratic nations to establish common safety standards, while the third calls for coordination between democratic and authoritarian governments. 

To be clear, pacing does not mean halting model training or technical progress, but ensuring companies take adequate time to align and safeguard their models, and for third party evaluators to confirm this, Amodei wrote. 

The proposal follows a public resignation this week by Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon, who expressed concern that top labs are gambling with human lives. Altman responded on X that he agreed with the need to pace advanced capabilities and announced that OpenAI will also commit to independent evaluators with employee-like access. Musk also posted his support on X, writing that Dario is right. 

The alignment of tech leaders comes amid growing pressure in Washington, where lawmakers are demanding new safeguards following unauthorized cyberattacks, and local officials are confronting community pushback over the power and water demands of AI data centers. Amodei noted that while slowing development was floated as early as 2023, it was previously unnecessary because models lacked real-world action capabilities and significant deception or cyberattack skills. He believes slowing down could buy an extra year or two to advance model alignment and reduce catastrophic risks.
