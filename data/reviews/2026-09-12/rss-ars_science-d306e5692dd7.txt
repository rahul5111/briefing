═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration announced new agency priorities last week aimed at cutting bureaucracy, modernizing science, and prioritizing fishing communities. The reforms serve as an operational roadmap to fulfill President Donald Trump's Restoring American Seafood Competitiveness executive order, signed in April 2025. The changes follow letters sent to regional fisheries councils earlier in the summer directing them to dismantle restrictions in favor of more permissive fishing procedures. The agency's plans include opening marine national monuments to commercial fishing, scaling back critical habitat designations to focus on downstream conservation economics, and weakening decades of peer-reviewed science. Critics warn the actions will erode fish stocks, threaten endangered species, and surrender marine habitats to destructive trawlers. Andrea Treece, senior attorney in the Oceans Program at Earthjustice, stated that if artificial intelligence were asked to create a plan to hollow out marine ecosystems and impoverish coastal communities, it would resemble NOAA's new directives. Treece added that it is inexcusable for the administration to facilitate the destruction of marine habitats and commit endangered species to extinction for short-term profits.
