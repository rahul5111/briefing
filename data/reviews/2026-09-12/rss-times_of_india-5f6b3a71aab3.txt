Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Chinese President Xi Jinping held a bilateral meeting lasting more than 50 minutes in New Delhi on Saturday on the sidelines of the Brics Summit 2026. Welcoming Xi, PM Modi thanked China for its support and cooperation during India's Brics chairmanship and stated that the two sides now had an opportunity to discuss bilateral ties. In his remarks, PM Modi said it gives me great pleasure to extend a warm welcome to you and expressed his gratitude for China's positive remarks and cooperation during India's chairmanship. 

The meeting follows years of tensions along the disputed India-China border as both nations seek to stabilise bilateral relations and maintain peace and tranquillity. Xi told PM Modi that China views the relationship from a strategic and long-term perspective. As the world's two most populous nations and key members of the Global South, Xi stated that the two countries shoulder significant responsibilities amid a complex international landscape. Xi emphasized that both sides should learn from each other's strengths and work towards shared development. 

Shortly before the bilateral meeting, leaders at the Brics Summit adopted the New Delhi Declaration 2026. The declaration expressed deep concern over escalating tensions in West Asia, called for maximum restraint, urged the protection of civilians and civilian infrastructure, and stressed the need for dialogue and diplomacy to achieve lasting peace. The document also addressed global trade, supply chains, energy security, and the safety of peaceful nuclear facilities, while calling for a Gaza ceasefire and unhindered humanitarian access. 

During the summit sessions, PM Modi proposed 10 measures for global governance reform to be developed into a Brics Reform Roadmap by the next summit, and called for transforming the Global South from a rule-taker into a rule-shaper in international decision-making.
