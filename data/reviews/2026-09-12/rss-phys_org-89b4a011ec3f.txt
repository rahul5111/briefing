═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency successfully conducted the first-ever laser ranging experiment with an asteroid during a flyby of Torifune on Sunday, July 5. The milestone marks the first exploration target of the Hayabusa2 spacecraft's Extended Mission. The probe used its LIDAR laser altimeter instrument to fire the laser twice during the flyby, occurring at 6:29 p.m. from distances of about 20 kilometers and 15 kilometers, respectively. The spacecraft traveled at a speed of 5.3 kilometers per second while targeting the asteroid with a narrow beam that struck surface areas measuring 30 meters and 23 meters. The LALT instrument was originally developed from the altimeter used by JAXA's Kaguya lunar orbiter and previously gathered topographical data on asteroid Ryugu. Laser ranging adds scale to spacecraft images and helps determine the size, density, porosity, and surface reflectance of astronomical objects. When combined with tracking data, the technique accurately determines a celestial body's position and trajectory to improve orbital predictions. The capability advances future missions involving near-Earth asteroids and supports planetary defense by allowing scientists to predict potential collision risks. The mission team is currently analyzing data to determine the exact surface location where the laser struck.
