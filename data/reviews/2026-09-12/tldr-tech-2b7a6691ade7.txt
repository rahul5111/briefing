═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

In Europe, where Google Search has held around ninety percent of the online search market share for over a decade, the European Commission is enforcing data sharing under Article 6(11) of the Digital Markets Act passed in 2022. The regulation requires gatekeepers like Google to provide competing search engine providers with access to ranking, query, click, and view data on fair, reasonable, and non-discriminatory terms, with personal data appropriately anonymized.

Google started offering access to an anonymized dataset in March 2024, but competitors experienced no significant uptake because Google's design choices rendered the data largely useless for search engine optimization. In response, the European Commission opened specification proceedings in January 2026 to resolve the issue, concluding last month with a published decision detailing how Google must comply. 

The anonymization framework balances strict privacy standards under the General Data Protection Regulation with practical utility for competitors. The standard dictates that personal data must be irreversibly altered so that information no longer relates to an identifiable natural person. Per draft guidelines from the European Data Protection Board and the European Commission, privacy protections specifically cover end users submitting queries, while technical and non-technical measures combine to mitigate residual re-identification risks from insiders or product teams attempting unauthorized re-identification.

Simultaneously, the data must retain high utility across three core categories where data asymmetry heavily impacts Google's competitors: crawling and indexing web pages, query understanding for ambiguous terms, and ranking and quality algorithms. Competitors primarily need this long-tail data to answer novel and ambiguous queries and identify less-visited websites that users find useful.
