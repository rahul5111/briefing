═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Saudi Arabia temporarily shut down its 1,200-kilometer East-West pipeline on Saturday following a drone attack, cutting off a vital route that has moved 4 million to 5 million barrels of oil per day over the past six months while the Strait of Hormuz has remained largely closed by war. Satellite images showed black smoke rising south of Medina, and the Saudi Foreign Ministry reported injuries and damage currently being assessed. Both Baghdad and Riyadh stated the drone attack originated in Iraq, prompting Iraq to dismiss a military commander on Saturday. US President Donald Trump, visiting Ireland, stated that Iran was probably to blame. In a separate incident on Saturday, Saudi Civil Defence reported that a Houthi projectile injured two people and damaged several buildings, including a mosque, in the Jazan region. Meanwhile, four Yemeni government sources stated that the Houthis seized the strategic island of Perim on Friday in the middle of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait at the mouth of the Red Sea. Three sources told Reuters that Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman phoned Trump on Thursday to request US military help against the Houthis, but was told Washington would not intervene directly for now though it would provide intelligence. Trump confirmed he spoke with the crown prince and added that the Houthis had also contacted his administration to ask that Washington avoid direct involvement. The pipeline closure and Houthi advances threaten to further disrupt global energy supplies, coming as US diesel prices surged past a record $6 a gallon amid widening conflict.
