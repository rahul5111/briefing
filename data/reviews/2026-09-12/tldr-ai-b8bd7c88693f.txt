═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Researchers have proposed a metric called Natural Language-rooted node-Separated depth, or NLS depth, to track the degree to which artificial intelligence architectures allow for latent reasoning and communication that bypasses human-interpretable monitoring. The measure builds on opaque serial depth, a concept originally defined in a 2026 paper by Brown-Cohen and colleagues at Google DeepMind that measures the longest path in a computational graph not passing through an interpretable bottleneck.

To operationalize this for modern systems, the new proposal defines interpretable bottlenecks as natural-language-rooted nodes. For a set of computational nodes to qualify, they must meet four criteria. First, they require natural-language initialization from a pre-training prior to produce human-understandable media such as text, code, JSON, or images. Second, they must have no expansion of the output space, ruling out techniques like abstract chain-of-thought or token superpositions. Third, the model cannot be modified to interpret the tokens as a different type of data. Fourth, tokens must not be treated as continuous intermediate states that are backpropagated through during training. 

The authors argue that tracking NLS depth complements existing monitorability assessments because it is crisp, easy to quantify, and can be adjudicated before training begins based solely on the architecture, training recipe, and deployment configuration. While insensitive to certain post-training choices, NLS depth effectively separates architectures in the current natural language chain-of-thought paradigm from concerning alternatives like scaled-up full-bandwidth transformers. The briefing suggests that AI companies transparently share information about architectural degrees of latent reasoning and flag whether high-NLS-depth models are used in distillation procedures.
