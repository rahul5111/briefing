NASA is pushing for open science and data sharing across the Artemis Accords, which currently have 71 signatory countries. To advance this tenet, NASA hosted a two-part virtual workshop series that began on July 28 and concluded on September 8. The workshops followed initial discussions led by the Indian Space Research Organisation in May. 

The first session covered open science principles and implementation practices, while the second focused on tools for open science, providing a working model for signatories to build their own data-sharing frameworks. During the sessions, NASA presented its Planetary Data System as a real-world example of how it structures, curates, and shares openly available lunar data, visualization tools, and information model standards.

The Artemis Accords were established in 2020 by NASA, the U.S. State Department, and seven founding nations to set practical principles for safe and transparent civil space exploration of the Moon, Mars, and beyond. 

As Jacob Bleacher, chief exploration scientist at NASA, stated, "We are making data, tools, and results freely available, and inviting the Artemis Accords partners to innovate with us and share their data as well, accelerating our understanding of lunar processes and laying the groundwork for human space exploration for the Moon, Mars and beyond."
