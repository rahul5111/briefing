═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

OpenAI is calling for mandatory, capability-based national AI safety regulation from Congress, warning that a closing policy window threatens to let AI capabilities outpace governing institutions. To build momentum until federal action occurs, the organization announced its support for four California bills headed to Governor Newsom: SB 813 for independent safety assessment infrastructure, AB 1405 for AI-auditor standards, SB 1119 for youth protections involving companion chatbots, and AB 1864 for safeguards against AI-enabled biological threats. 

Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki recently highlighted that the rapid rise of machine intelligence calls for extreme caution, while Greg Brockman described a defenders window, representing a limited period when frontier AI can help secure critical systems before offensive capabilities become widespread. OpenAI states that confidence in safety must set the pace of progress, and when development poses unacceptable safety risks, the company will slow or stop systems it cannot sufficiently safeguard. 

The proposed national framework should target well-resourced laboratories developing the most capable systems while exempting startups and small developers, and it should address risks without weakening open models or driving innovation overseas. OpenAI argues that strong safeguards are required for widespread public trust and lasting American leadership.
