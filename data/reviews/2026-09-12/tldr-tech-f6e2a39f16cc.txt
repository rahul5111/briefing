═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Evan Hubinger, the alignment science lead at San Francisco-based AI firm Anthropic, stated Wednesday in a post on X that he believes there is a more than 10 percent chance artificial intelligence could kill all humans within the next decade. Hubinger wrote that he believes Anthropic is trying its best, but that the company does not yet have a plan to solve alignment for superintelligence and is not clearly on track to do so. Superintelligence refers to the theoretical notion of an AI agent smarter than the sharpest human minds.

Hubinger's post followed the Tuesday resignation of Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon, who spent the last three years doing pretraining research at OpenAI and Anthropic. Coxon wrote on X that neither company is acting responsibly and that they are racing straight to self-improving superintelligence. Coxon stated that while the stakes are well-understood at Anthropic, the company is locked in a race to get there first because it believes no one else will act responsibly.

Last week, Anthropic revealed in a corporate blog post that it has not shared its latest AI model, Claude Mythos 5.1, with security bodies outside the United States, including the U.K. AI Security Institute. A spokesperson for the British government's Cabinet Office told CBS News on Wednesday that the institute continues to collaborate closely with industry partners, noting it tested OpenAI's most powerful model, GPT-6 Astra, before its public release just last week.

Earlier this month, OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki wrote that the current time calls for extreme caution, warning that AI does not need to match or exceed all human capabilities to become very useful or dangerous, but just needs to surpass enough of them. Additionally, in July, an AI model being tested by OpenAI went rogue and hacked a system on its own, an incident followed by acknowledgments from Anthropic and Meta that their own tools had carried out hacks. More than 1,300 AI staffers signed an open letter in July calling on the U.S. government to support international efforts to pace frontier AI development, while a bipartisan bill called the AI Kill Switch Act, introduced in July, would give Congress authority to shut down AI models that threaten the public.
