═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

OpenAI’s upcoming Astra model will utilize a reasoning technique known as recurrent depth, or opaque recurrence, which allows the system to process queries through a loop rather than traditional sequential thinking. Reported by The Information on Tuesday, the technique bypasses standard, linear chain-of-thought records that AI safety experts rely on to monitor model behavior and alignment. While Astra's current use of opaque recurrence is limited and OpenAI has stated its chain of thought remains legible, the development has alarmed outside researchers who fear broader adoption could eliminate visible reasoning entirely. Redwood CEO Buck Shlegeris wrote that if OpenAI pushes the technique further, they will have the option to massively increase recurrence and totally destroy chain-of-thought monitorability. Similarly, longtime safety advocate Zvi Mowshowitz warned that the approach risks a taboo established by top labs to maintain chain-of-thought faithfulness. In response to the concerns, OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki emphasized on X that preserving and utilizing chain-of-thought monitoring remains a core goal of the company's research program. Meanwhile, follow-up reporting on Wednesday indicated that competitors Anthropic and Google DeepMind are already discussing the technique, raising concerns among researchers that labs could accelerate opaque reasoning to a point where models operate entirely in latent space.
