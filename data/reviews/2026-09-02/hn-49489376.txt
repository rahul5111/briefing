═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A developer has launched Laser Graffiti, a browser-based application that allows users to turn a standard laser pointer into a digital paintbrush without needing any software installation. To use the setup, operators connect a projector as a second screen, point a webcam at the projection area, and open the control and projector windows in a browser. A single click calibrates the system by flashing markers into the corners of the projection so the camera can compute the mapping. Users then wave the laser for four seconds to let the detector learn its brightness and color. Every frame, the camera tracks the laser dot and maps it onto the projector, which renders the stroke in real time. Users can also access an on-wall menu by holding the laser in a designated corner, or play tic-tac-toe against the computer. The project requires a laptop running Chrome, an HDMI projector, a webcam, and a laser pointer, with green lasers recommended because they show up best on camera. The software is a from-scratch browser reimplementation inspired by the Graffiti Research Lab Vienna's Michael Zeltner and Florian Hufsky, who built a hardware laser marker in 2007 to tag buildings in Vienna. That earlier work drew inspiration from the original Graffiti Research Lab in New York and its L.A.S.E.R. Tag project, which similarly combined a camera, projector, and laser pointer for large-scale outdoor projections.
