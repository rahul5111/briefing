═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A new analysis of the original data files from the 2002 Psychological Science study on procrastination by Dan Ariely and Klaus Wertenbroch concludes that the data for Study 2 were tampered with. The original paper found that participants performed better on proofreading tasks when given evenly spaced deadlines compared to self-set or last-day deadlines. It has been cited over 2,100 times.

The reassessment stems from data files received in 2006 by replication researcher Kyle Hyndman, which listed Dan Ariely as the last user to save them. Following a recent independent analysis of these files, researchers identified four major red flags. 

First, the reported effect size of the evenly spaced deadlines was implausibly large at Cohen's d equals 2.5, yielding a correlation of r equals 0.79 with condition performance, despite proofreading error detection typically being a noisy measure. Second, the data contained duplicate observations in the last-day deadline condition, where 18 of 20 participants had a corrections twin with identical scores across all three tasks and ID numbers precisely 10 positions apart. Third, subjective ratings and performance metrics that strongly correlated in a recent replication showed virtually no correlation in the original dataset. For instance, partial correlations between task liking and interest ranged from positive 0.63 to positive 0.92 in the replication, but ranged from negative 0.29 to positive 0.18 in the original data. Fourth, while 85 percent of participants in the replication rounded their self-reported minutes spent on tasks, only 11.7 percent did so in the original data.

Upon sharing these findings, Ariely and Wertenbroch requested that Psychological Science retract the original 2002 article.
