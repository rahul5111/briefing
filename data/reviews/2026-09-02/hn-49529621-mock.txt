═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A dark web service named Nexus launched on Monday, August 31, on the Russian cybercrime forum Exploit, offering digital scans of more than 153 million driver licenses from the United States and Canada. The proprietor included a Virginia driver license as a free sample in the initial sales thread. Researchers and associates tested the data and matched image timestamps to specific travel dates when individuals surrendered their physical IDs to rental car companies and businesses utilizing identity verification software. 

The suspected source of the breach is IDScan dot net, a Louisiana-based identity verification company whose systems scan IDs using infrared and ultraviolet light and process verifications for major brands such as Hertz and Planet Thirteen dispensaries. The New Orleans field office of the Federal Bureau of Investigation launched an official inquiry into the source of the images. Jillian Kossman, a marketing and operations leader at IDScan dot net, stated that the company was investigating the matter. Shortly after the initial story was published, the Nexus website vanished from the dark web and displayed a message stating the service is no longer available.
