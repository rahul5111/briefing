═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A tech hobbyist repurposed three existing home security cameras and an old laptop to build an automatic, 24/7 bird identification system using the open-source software BirdNet-Go running locally in Docker. The setup uses RTSP streams from the cameras and processes audio through a local AI inference engine without cloud dependencies or subscription fees. The platform recently added support for Google Perch v2, expanding species detection capabilities to 14,795 species compared to the 6,000 offered by BirdNET 2.4. Over a twelve-month period, the system recorded 418,726 detections across 271 unique species with an average confidence rating of 60.9 percent, identifying the House Finch as the most common visitor in Southern California with 118,667 detections. The project also integrates with Home Assistant via MQTT, connects to a home Discord server, and works with front-end interfaces like Saezuri and the iOS app BirdNET-Go Companion built by Robert Oesterlin. During operation, the system inadvertently picked up non-bird audio, successfully detecting a neighbor passing by the driveway and flatulence. The creator noted that the project turned standard security cameras into a daily resource for tracking local biodiversity without requiring special hardware.
