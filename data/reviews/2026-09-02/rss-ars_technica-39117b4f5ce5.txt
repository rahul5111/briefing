NASA has decided to use a simpler spacesuit for its initial missions to the lunar surface in an effort to accelerate its program to land humans at the South Pole of the Moon as early as 2028. The agency announced the decision during an internal meeting this week. At the direction of Artemis Program Manager Jeremy Parsons, NASA will work with Axiom Space to develop a Sortie Suit variant of the planned spacesuit for the lunar surface. Sources indicated that the Sortie Suit will be used for the initial landing missions flown on landers developed by both SpaceX and Blue Origin. The goals of the initiative include lowering the mass of the spacesuit, reducing its complexity, and simplifying interfaces between the spacesuits and the lunar landers.
