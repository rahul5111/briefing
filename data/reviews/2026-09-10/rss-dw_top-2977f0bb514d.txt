═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Around 12,000 app users in Britain sued the dating app Grindr after it allegedly shared highly sensitive personal information, including HIV status, with advertisers. The lawsuit, which was filed in 2024, was settled earlier this week. Grindr agreed to pay claimants a total of 26 million pounds, which is approximately 30 million euros or 35 million dollars. The company stressed that the settlement is not an admission of liability, continues to dispute the allegations, and noted there has been no legal judgement. 

The case highlights broader concerns about data privacy and how information entrusted to apps is handled by cloud services, analysis tools, and advertising networks. Jan Penfrat, a digital policy expert for European Digital Rights, stated that many big tech companies want to make it as difficult as possible for users and regulatory authorities to understand what data is gathered on devices and for what purpose. 

While the European Union enforces strict regulations like the General Data Protection Regulation, the Digital Markets Act, and the Digital Services Act, major tech firms continue to face financial penalties that remain small relative to their overall profits. In 2025, the EU Commission fined Apple 500 million euros, Meta 200 million euros, and Google 890 million euros. Penfrat noted that these sums are amounts companies like Google or Amazon make in just a few days, allowing them to simply include fines in their budgets. Effective oversight is further complicated by Europe's reliance on cloud services, operating systems, and platforms provided largely by US companies, alongside a lack of political will in Brussels to properly equip European data protection authorities and take firmer action.
