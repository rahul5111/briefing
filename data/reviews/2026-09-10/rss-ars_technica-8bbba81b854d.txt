═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

LinkedIn has successfully dismissed two class-action lawsuits regarding its practice of scanning users browser extensions. US District Judge Vince Chhabria, presiding in the US District Court for the Northern District of California, granted the Microsoft subsidiary's motion to dismiss on Tuesday. The judge ruled that the plaintiffs, California residents Nicholas Farrell and Jeff Ganan, failed to adequately establish legal standing because neither asserted they had browser extensions installed that actually conveyed private information to LinkedIn. 

Chhabria granted the plaintiffs leave to amend their complaints, though he expressed skepticism about their prospects. In his ruling, Chhabria wrote that given LinkedIn's arguments that users voluntarily download browser extensions, which intentionally expose data to websites by their nature, it seems unlikely the plaintiffs will ever be able to allege a privacy violation or prevail. 

The lawsuits were originally filed separately in April. J.R. Howell, the attorney representing Ganan, stated that he is currently evaluating whether to refile the claims in a California state court, which operates under different standing requirements, or to appeal the federal court ruling in the US Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.
