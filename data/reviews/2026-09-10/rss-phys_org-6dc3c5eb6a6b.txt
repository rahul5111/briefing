═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

An international team of researchers, including CIIMAR, has developed the first high-density genotyping platform for two economically vital clam species, the Japanese clam and the common clam. Published in the journal Aquaculture, the study introduces an SNP chip comprising approximately 63,000 genetic markers to help produce clams more resistant to disease and climate change while conserving genetic diversity. More than 45,000 markers have been validated for the Japanese clam and 15,000 for the common clam. Additionally, the Aquatic Animal Health group identified more than 13,000 markers related to resistance or susceptibility in the common clam to the parasite Perkinsus olsenii, published in Evolutionary Applications. The platform also enables the identification of kinship, monitoring of genetic diversity, and tracking of inbreeding levels. According to the researchers, this tool will in the future enable us to support coastal communities through the implementation of new genetic improvement programs for both species of clam, with a significant impact on the blue economy sector. The project involved researchers from the University of Padua, the University of Santiago de Compostela, the Roslin Institute at the University of Edinburgh, the Institut Universitaire Européen de la Mer, and aquaculture genetics companies Benchmark Genetics and Thermo Fisher Scientific. The openly available platform aims to accelerate selective breeding and support marine conservation amid ongoing production declines caused by habitat degradation, climate change, and disease.
