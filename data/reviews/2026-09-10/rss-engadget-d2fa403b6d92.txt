═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Anthropic has flagged and stopped multiple working scientists who were attempting to use its Claude AI models to further biological weapon research. Released in a report shared on Thursday, the findings detail five case studies of potential biological misuse alongside safety measures and company responses. In one instance from May, Anthropic's biological safety classifier flagged a request for Claude to author a grant for gain-of-function research on the chikungunya virus, a pathogen with no licensed treatment that causes debilitating symptoms. The proposal sought to genetically alter the virus to increase its transmissibility and immune evasion, and originated from a military research institute. Other flagged incidents involved gain-of-function research into bird flu and a separate case where a researcher used Claude to develop an atlas of venom toxin peptides and a generative pipeline to optimize toxin characteristics. Additional case studies in the full report cover models being used for surveillance, software exploits, propaganda, and weapons systems. Jacob Klein, Anthropic's head of threat intelligence, told The New York Times that the situations are deeply nuanced, stating that you do not see someone acting like a comic book villain wanting to build a weapon to kill everybody. Detecting misuse is complicated because legitimate vaccine research can closely resemble bioweapon creation, prompting Anthropic to err on the side of caution due to potential consequences. The company banned the accounts of anyone misusing the models and is using the investigation's findings to improve safeguards. Anthropic is not naming the individuals or institutions involved, noting that the implicated parties are working scientists whose intent to cause harm is not asserted, and that naming them could expose them to danger.
