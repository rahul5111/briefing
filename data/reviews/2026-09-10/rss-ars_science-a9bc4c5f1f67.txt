═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

On Tuesday, Google announced AlphaGenome Atlas, a new resource designed to predict the consequences of every possible single-base variant in the human genome. Because the human genome is approximately 3 billion bases long, evaluating the three alternative DNA bases that do not appear in the reference genome requires running a total of 9 billion bases through the AlphaGenome software. The system is specifically engineered to identify the potential functions of non-coding DNA, which makes up the vast majority of the human genome without encoding proteins itself. While this non-coding region does not build proteins, portions of it are essential for controlling the activity of protein-coding regions by instructing cells where and when to make messenger RNAs and how to process them, though much of it appears to consist of remnants from viruses and other molecular parasites. Having all this analysis performed by a single software package and being able to pinpoint functional portions of DNA is useful, but the true value of AlphaGenome will remain unclear until biologists begin heavy adoption to see what it offers beyond its original training data.
