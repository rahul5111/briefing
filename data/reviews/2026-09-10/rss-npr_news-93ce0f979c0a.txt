═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A new federal lawsuit filed on Thursday by Minneapolis resident Michael Khalili and the nonprofit Common Cause challenges what it describes as a nationwide policy of intimidation and retaliation against citizens legally observing federal immigration enforcement. The 94-page complaint, filed in Washington, D.C. against the Department of Homeland Security, the Department of Justice, the FBI and other agencies, accuses the federal government of violating the First Amendment and privacy rights of observers across states including Maryland, Minnesota, New Jersey, Illinois, Maine and Tennessee. The lawsuit seeks national relief and asks a judge to bar federal agencies from intimidating or retaliating against observers. The legal action outlines encounters faced by seven Common Cause members and other activists who were photographed, followed, pepper-sprayed, threatened with police visits, or subjected to facial recognition databases, administrative subpoenas, and the revocation of trusted traveler statuses like Global Entry. The complaint highlights the case of Khalili, a 47-year-old father of two who stopped observing ICE following pressure from his family and tense exchanges with federal officers. Khalili's inclusion in the suit notes that law enforcement queried his state motor vehicle records 55 times since March 2026, compared to just twice before he began observing, and that his Global Entry status was revoked one day after an encounter with ICE officers before being reinstated following a journalist's inquiry. The lawsuit argues these actions stem from top-down Trump administration directives, including National Security Presidential Memorandum 7 and associated DOJ memos that link impeding immigration enforcement to domestic terrorism, as well as the repeal of guardrails on facial recognition and license plate readers. Danielle Lang, vice president of voting rights and rule of law at the Campaign Legal Center, which is litigating the case, stated that the events described are not one-off activities but rather a top-down policy of retaliation that needs to be addressed. Maryam Jazini Dorcheh, Common Cause vice president for litigation, said that the right of citizens to criticize and hold the government accountable is under attack and that criminalizing peaceful observation hinders their mission. DHS and DOJ did not provide an immediate response to the allegations, and the FBI declined to comment.
