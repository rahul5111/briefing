═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A team of six mathematicians from Denmark, Canada, and Japan has produced a new computer-assisted proof of the centuries-old four-color theorem, which states that any contiguous map can be colored using only four colors without adjacent regions sharing a color. Initiated on a Danish beach in 2015 by Ken-ichi Kawarabayashi and Mikkel Thorup, and later joined by Carsten Thomassen, Bojan Mohar, Yuta Inoue, and Atsuyuki Miyashita, the project took nearly a decade of work. The researchers posted their findings online in March 2026 and will present them in November at the annual Foundations of Computer Science conference. While previous proofs from 1976 and 1997 required sequential steps proportional to $n^2$ for a graph with $n$ vertices, the new proof identifies an unavoidable set of 8,202 configurations in flat areas of graphs that can be reduced in parallel, dropping the coloring process to $n(\log n)$ steps. Beyond efficiency, the work reveals previously unknown structural properties of planar graphs by using a different concept of reducibility. As Georges Gonthier of Inria in Paris noted regarding the team's intricate approach, it is really cool to see a real result for once given the problem's history of false starts and dashed hopes.
