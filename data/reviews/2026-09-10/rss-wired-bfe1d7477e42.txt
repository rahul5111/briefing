This week on the WIRED podcast Uncanny Valley, AI researcher Jacob Coxon’s resignation from Anthropic went viral after he published a thread on X warning that leading AI companies are behaving irresponsibly. Coxon claimed that, quote, "The people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade," a timeline reinforced by an Anthropic senior safety executive who publicly agreed with the sentiment. WIRED senior correspondent Will Knight noted that while Anthropic has historically built its safety pitch around existential AI risk, the public departure signals growing alarm over rapid developments. These include OpenAI solving a Clay mathematics puzzle within days and labs accelerating recursive self-improvement, where AI is used to enhance its own code. Knight expressed skepticism regarding existential doom percentages, pointing out that recent agent misbehavior often stems from systems being unintelligent rather than hyper-competent, though he remains concerned about corporate concentration of power and vulnerabilities arising from rushed cyber-capable model releases.
