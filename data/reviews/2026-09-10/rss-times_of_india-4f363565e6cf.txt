A disagreement over the song Vande Mataram has exposed a deeper power struggle between the National Conference and the Congress in Jammu and Kashmir, highlighting ongoing tensions over who sets the political terms of their alliance. The dispute began Wednesday at the inaugural International Film Festival in Srinagar, where NC leaders Omar Abdullah and Farooq Abdullah stood through the full three-minute, ten-second, six-stanza rendition of Vande Mataram in accordance with new central protocol. Congress leader Venugopal subsequently called on allies to honour the wisdom of freedom fighters, prompting NC spokesperson Tanvir Sadiq to clarify that the government was merely fulfilling a statutory obligation regardless of party ideology. 

This latest friction follows a series of uncoordinated moves and disagreements between the partners since the 2024 assembly elections restored their relationship. In July 2025, Congress announced unilateral protests and wrote to Prime Minister Narendra Modi seeking the restoration of Jammu and Kashmir statehood, irritating NC leaders who viewed the move as uncoordinated and potentially counterproductive. In October 2025, the allies deadlocked over four Rajya Sabha seats, with the dominant National Conference ultimately parting ways on seat-sharing after failing to agree on allocations. Addressing the Rajya Sabha deadlock, Omar Abdullah stated, We felt that the best chance of winning that seat was with the Congress, and they felt otherwise. So be it. 

The National Conference's sensitivity to control is rooted in historical friction, stretching back to the 1984 dismissal of the NC government and the contentious 1987 alliance that won 66 of 76 assembly seats but was marred by allegations of electoral rigging and subsequent political disillusionment. While the two parties later managed a coalition government from 2009 to 2014 under Omar Abdullah as chief minister, the current power asymmetry continues to drive friction over statehood campaigns, Article 370 positions, and legislative coordination.
