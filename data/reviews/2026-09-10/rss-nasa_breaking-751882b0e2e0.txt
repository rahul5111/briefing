NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman chaired the first meeting of the Presidential Commission on the United States Space Academy on September 9, 2026, following President Donald Trump’s August 28 executive order establishing the institution. Following the meeting, NASA issued a Request for Information on Thursday seeking state hosts and sponsors for the academy. 

During a discussion at NASA Headquarters in Washington, Isaacman and Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson met with key officials including U.S. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth, National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett, Air Force Secretary Troy Meink, and U.S. Chief Technology Officer Ethan Klein to discuss the academy's governance, curriculum, service commitments, and partnerships. 

State submissions in response to the request are due by 6 p.m. EDT on Monday, October 26, limited to one entry per state via each governor’s designee. The commission operates under a 120-day mandate to deliver recommendations to President Trump for building America's technical talent and leadership pipeline in space. The timeline outlines a groundbreaking no later than 2027, temporarily hosting the first 300 students in 2028, and opening a permanent operational location by 2031.

“Our first Commission meeting made clear how much talent and commitment we have behind President Trump’s vision for the U.S. Space Academy,” said Isaacman.
