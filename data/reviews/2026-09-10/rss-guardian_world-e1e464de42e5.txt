═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A bitter succession dispute has erupted in the Ugandan kingdom of Tooro following the death of King Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV, who died on August 27 in the United States while receiving cancer treatment. King Oyo, who ascended the throne in 1995 at the age of three as the world's youngest reigning monarch, was unmarried and not publicly known to have children. On Wednesday, the royal clan succession committee chose Prince Edward Rukidi Kijanangoma, a news anchor and editor at the public broadcaster UBC, as crown prince. However, hours later, Oyo's sister Princess Ruth Nsemere Komuntale and Queen Mother Best Kemigisa Akiiki rejected the selection, citing a will left by the late king directing that an unpublicized biological son succeed him. Tooro prime minister Calvin Armstrong Rwomiire Akiiki first revealed the existence of the heir on August 27. The Queen Mother denounced the committee's choice and stated that the royal family had been placed under house arrest after troops removed royal guards and surrounded the palace. Another contender, London amateur football goalkeeper Prince George Desmond-Kamurasi, declined the throne due to other responsibilities. Commentators and family members have raised questions regarding the validity of the secret heir, noting traditional requirements that a successor be born to an officially married queen and introduced to elders. Prime Minister Calvin Armstrong Rwomiire Akiiki called for restraint, stating on social media that the process should take place calmly and credibly, not through competing announcements, confrontation, or attempts to establish facts on the ground while our king still awaits burial. King Oyo is scheduled to be buried on Saturday at the Karambi royal tombs in Fort Portal, western Uganda. Tooro is one of five ancient kingdoms recognized by the Ugandan government, where monarchs wield vast social influence but hold no political authority.
