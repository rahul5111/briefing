═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Authors awaiting payouts from Anthropic's $1.5 billion copyright settlement are pushing back after discovering that publishers and literary agencies are attempting to claim shares of the money. Under the settlement, which received final approval in July after a class action suit over pirated training material, the authors of nearly 500,000 titles are slated to receive $3,000 for each pirated work. In-print books traditionally split the payment 50-50 between author and publisher, while authors of self-published or out-of-print works are meant to receive the full amount. However, writers have reported receiving emails indicating that publishers are seeking full payments instead of halves, or claiming rights to books that reverted years ago. For instance, author April Henry posted that HarperCollins claimed a book whose rights reverted at least 17 years ago. Victoria Strauss of the blog Writers Beware and Authors Guild CEO Mary Rasenberger both attribute the disputes to poor record-keeping and a confusing settlement process rather than malicious intent. Beyond publishers, writers have also complained that literary agencies are attempting to claim percentages of the settlement funds. Author Courtney Milan responded on Bluesky by writing, Apparently some agents are trying to claim percentages on the Anthropic settlement, and I do not REMOTELY think they should do this, what the fuck, stop that shit! Milan and the Authors Guild have shared details on how authors can dispute allocations, noting that to claim 100 percent of a payment, the rights reversion must have occurred before the August 10, 2022 download date in the settlement.
