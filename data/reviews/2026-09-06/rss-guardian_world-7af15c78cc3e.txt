═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Voters in Guinea-Bissau have approved a new constitution that significantly expands presidential powers ahead of elections scheduled for December. According to provisional results, 70 percent of voters backed the sweeping changes during Sunday’s referendum, which saw a turnout of over 55 percent, amounting to an estimated 544,060 people. This marks the country's first constitutional amendment since revisions ended the one-party socialist state over three decades ago. The new charter reduces parliamentary seats from 102 to 65 and cuts electoral constituencies from 29 to 12. It also requires presidential candidates to have lived continuously in the country for at least five years prior to the vote, a provision believed to target diaspora-based politicians. The referendum paves the way for the December elections, following a turbulent period that included a military intervention after last November's disputed presidential vote. Nelson Moreira, second vice-president of the transitional council, said the outgoing 1993 constitution had frequently been cited as the cause of instability in Guinea. However, the leading opposition party, the African Party for the Independence of Guinea and Cape Verde, which was barred from fielding a presidential candidate in the upcoming election, called the referendum a democratic farce and urged a boycott. Its leader, Domingos Simões Pereira, said that what would happen is the continuation of the coup d’état carried out on 26 November. Gen Horta Inta-A Na Man, head of the transitional ruling council, defended the changes, stating that what they want after the referendum is to allow whoever wins the elections to see out their mandate without hindrance.
