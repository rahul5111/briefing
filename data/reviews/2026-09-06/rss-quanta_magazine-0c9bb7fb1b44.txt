In December 2025, five mathematicians at ETH Zurich solved a decades-old open problem in percolation theory by proving the supercritical half of the sharpness conjecture for all infinite transitive graphs. The research team included postdocs Sahar Diskin and Philip Easo, graduate student Ritvik Ramanan Radhakrishnan, Benny Sudakov, and Vincent Tassion. 

Percolation studies flow in networks, originally inspired by Rosalind Franklin's 1940s research into coal porosity at the British Coal Utilization Research Association. In the 1950s, Simon Broadbent and John Hammersley modeled this using lattices and coin flips to represent open or blocked pathways. When the probability of open pathways passes a critical threshold, a phase transition occurs and the network is suddenly dominated by large seas of fluid. While sharpness—proving that pools grow rapidly and predictably past this point—was established for standard grids in the 1980s, proving it for the vastly broader class of infinite transitive graphs remained elusive after foundational work in the late 1990s by Itai Benjamini and Oded Schramm. The subcritical half was solved in 2007, but the supercritical half remained an open fortress.

The ETH Zurich team initially set out to study how critical probability scales with edge numbers in the fall of 2025. Nearing the end of the semester, Easo suggested pivoting to sharpness. Tassion then realized their strategy could potentially apply to all infinite transitive graphs. Working through the night toward Christmas, the group devised a simple proof demonstrating that above the critical probability, the shoreline of any supposed large isolated pool creates contradictions making it nearly impossible for the pool to remain disconnected from infinite seas.
