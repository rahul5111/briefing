═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Researchers have introduced BenchMIRT, a new method for auditing large language model benchmarks at the level of individual prompts. Built on multidimensional item response theory adapted from psychometrics, the method analyzes how models perform on specific questions to separate underlying capabilities that contribute to a benchmark score. BenchMIRT was trained on benchmarking results from 100 open-weight LLMs released by March 2025, covering 16 benchmarks and over 34,000 questions. Without being told which benchmarks measured which capabilities, the system independently recovered two dominant dimensions: safety and general reasoning. The analysis revealed that several evaluations measure more than their intended focus. The BBQ social bias benchmark and the WMDP dangerous knowledge benchmark both aligned more strongly with general reasoning than with safety. In addition, BenchMIRT found that HarmBench safety prompts tracked closely with safety, whereas its copyright questions associated more strongly with general reasoning. By evaluating question-level data, BenchMIRT showed that keeping only 50 percent of a benchmark's questions often matched the full benchmark's measure of capabilities, and keeping 10 percent generally preserved a similar picture of model strength. Furthermore, the method correctly predicted whether a model would answer a held-out question correctly 79 percent of the time, compared to 70 percent for a simpler overall score approach. Limitations include that the analysis does not capture models released after March 2025 and that discovered dimensions depend on the selected benchmark set.
