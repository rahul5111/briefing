═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Researchers analyzing how artificial intelligence coding agents select third-party tools have published findings from 16,893 experimental runs across 75 simulated repositories spanning 10 programming languages. The study tested agents including Cursor, Codex, and Claude Code using real-world tasks prompted by four distinct user profiles ranging from junior engineers to enterprise staff. An orchestrator powered by Gemini 3.7 Flash acted as a simulated human in the loop, while another instance of Gemini 3.7 Flash judged the valid sessions. From the initial runs, researchers retained 5,292 valid sessions across 51 codebases and 18 sectors. The data revealed distinct behaviors among the coding assistants. Cursor based its decisions on the web in two-thirds of sessions. Codex used web search in 94 percent of sessions, employing operators like site: to target trusted domains. Claude Code relied primarily on its training priors, searching the web in approximately 30 percent of cases, though that figure rose to about 80 percent in newer sectors such as sandboxes. All three agents picked the exact same tool in only 42 percent of the cells. Furthermore, Claude Code chose to build in-house in 19 percent of sessions, nearly twice as often as Codex and Cursor at 10 percent. The experiments also highlighted discrepancies between brand mentions and actual selections. PayPal was cited 139 times without being picked, while Stripe won 124 of those specific sessions. LangChain received 194 mentions but was chosen only 4 times, and Supabase accumulated 242 mentions while being largely dominated by Neon. Market dominance varied by sector, with Stripe winning in nine out of ten cases, Neon taking 66 percent of database selections, and Amazon S3 leading file storage at 45 percent. All traces and analysis results have been made publicly available.
