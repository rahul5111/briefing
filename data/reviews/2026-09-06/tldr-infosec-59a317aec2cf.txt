═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

OpenAI is incorporating a new reasoning technique known as recurrent depth, or opaque recurrence, into its upcoming Astra model, according to a report published on Tuesday by The Information. Unlike conventional reasoning models that rely on step-by-step sequential thinking, Astra processes queries multiple times in a loop, utilizing a less linear approach that leaves fewer legible traces. While standard chain-of-thought records have previously served as a crucial tool for monitoring AI misbehavior and misalignment, the new method side-steps conventional logs and makes the model's internal process significantly harder to track.

The development has raised alarm among AI safety experts. Redwood CEO Buck Shlegeris wrote in a post that if OpenAI pushes the technique further, the company will have the option to massively increase recurrence and totally destroy chain-of-thought monitorability. Longtime advocate Zvi Mowshowitz warned that the technique risks breaking a vital taboo established by leading labs, while Redwood chief scientist Ryan Greenblatt expressed concern that opaque reasoning could scale faster than conventional methods and eventually remove all reasoning from visible channels entirely.

Despite these concerns, Astra's current use of opaque recurrence is reportedly limited, and the model's chain of thought is still expected to remain legible. OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki pushed back against suggestions that the company would shift to neuralese, emphasizing on X that OpenAI has worked to preserve and utilize chain-of-thought monitoring since its very first reasoning models and that it remains a core goal of the current research program. Meanwhile, a follow-up report by The Information on Wednesday morning indicated that both Anthropic and Google DeepMind are already discussing the technique.
