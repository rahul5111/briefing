═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Global superpowers and technology firms are scrambling to secure real-world battlefield data from Ukraine to train next-generation military AI models. Last month, Ukraine signed an agreement to share data collected from drones, cameras, and sensors with the UK, making Britain the first foreign state granted access to Ukraine's "Avengers AI" model used to identify adversaries and accelerate attacks. The first project stemming from this deal will test buried fibre-optic cables as sensors at an unnamed UK defence site. Peter Lee at the University of Portsmouth described the data as gold dust, stating, "This is the gold dust that allows the UK to enter the autonomous age of drones."

Ukraine collects extensive combat data through its government department Brave1 and the A1 organisation, which was launched with UK support in March. In addition to the UK deal, Ukraine signed a data-sharing agreement with Palantir months prior, while US companies Clearview AI and Scale AI have supplied facial recognition and satellite analysis technology used to find Russian spies and track troop movements. Joe Burton at Lancaster University noted that access to credible battlefield data provides a major commercial advantage over lab-generated simulations. Burton also suggested that Ukraine's broader partnerships reflect a strategy to hedge its bets amid potential shifts in US support under a Trump presidency, while experts believe China is likely securing similar data from the Russian side of the front line.
