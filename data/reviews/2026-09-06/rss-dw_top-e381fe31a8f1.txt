═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Following Saxony-Anhalt's recent election where the far-right Alternative for Germany won a decisive lead, social media platforms were flooded with false and misleading claims alleging vote-rigging and irregularities, which fact-checkers have linked to Russian disinformation efforts. 

A staged video viewed 10,000 times on X falsely claimed to show election workers destroying AfD ballots. The footage showed a man drawing black X marks on ballots, all containing the same name and missing the regulatory hole punch for visually impaired voters. The Russian disinformation watchdog group the Gnida Project identified the clip as part of the Storm-1516 campaign controlled by Russia's military intelligence agency, the GRU.

A separate Russian disinfo initiative dubbed Matryoshka, or Operation Overload, utilized spoofed videos featuring logos from reputable outlets like DW, ARD, WIRED, and BBC. These posts falsely alleged that the CDU party was engaging in fraud by registering fake ballots using Nazi soldier names or deceased residents. Disinformation reporter Lars Wienand confirmed his name and image were misused in one fabricated report, stating he never published such findings. Julia Smirnova, senior researcher at the Center for Monitoring, Analysis and Strategy, noted that the reach of these posts is mostly inauthentic. DW Fact Check has previously identified more than 180 fake posts from this operation on X, Bluesky, and TikTok.

Additionally, a right-wing journalist's post with over 160,000 views falsely claimed a scandal where Halle began counting absentee ballots before the 6 p.m. legal deadline. The claim relied on a German newspaper ZEIT liveblog screenshot that incorrectly stated counting had begun at 3:15 p.m. The reporter later clarified they were only describing preparatory tasks, and ZEIT issued a correction and transparency note. Saxony-Anhalt regulations permit opening ballots in advance to facilitate later counting, though official tallies cannot begin until voting closes.
