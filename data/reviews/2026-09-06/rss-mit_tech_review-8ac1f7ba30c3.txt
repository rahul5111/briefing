═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

As agentic AI transitions from experimentation to enterprise deployment, approximately 80 percent of Fortune 500 companies have adopted the technology, though scaling remains uneven as many organizations remain stuck in isolated pilots. According to Arun Chandra, chief operating officer at NiCE, organizations must move beyond experimentation by aligning agentic AI with specific business strategies to increase revenue or reduce costs, and they must redesign workflows instead of applying AI to outdated processes. Chandra notes that organizations should avoid applying AI to inefficient systems, stating that the last thing you want to do is to apply AI on an outdated or an inefficient workflow. To achieve scale, organizations must treat agentic AI as a cohesive system by providing agents with necessary data, knowledge, context, and back-end system connections. Chandra emphasizes that the efficacy of these AI agents is purely a function of the context, the knowledge, and the data they can ingest and use. Scaling also requires managing governance, privacy, security, and change management as agents take on consequential work. Chandra argues that AI agents should ultimately be held to the same standards as human workers, with enterprises viewing their workforce as a combination of humans and AI agents. Rather than trying to boil the ocean, organizations transitioning from pilots to scale should focus on a connected strategy built around high-value use cases, workflow changes, and measurable outcomes.
