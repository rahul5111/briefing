═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Historically, sharing media between Android and Apple devices resulted in degraded image quality due to differing messaging systems, with Apple using SMS and Android using RCS. The release of iOS 18 introduced RCS support on iPhones, resolving this gap and enabling higher image quality. 

To ensure quality when sending from an iPhone, users can verify RCS is enabled by navigating to Settings, then Apps, then Messages, and checking that RCS Messaging is toggled green, as it is turned on by default in iOS 18. On the Android side, Google Messages compresses photos by default. Senders must press the HD or HD+ button and select "HD+ Original Quality" instead of "HD Optimized for Chat" to avoid compression.

For larger files and videos, users must navigate the RCS attachment size limit of 100MB. Alternative options include email, alternative messaging services, Google Drive, or Google Photos by selecting "Send in Google Photos" within the app. Additionally, Android Quick Share now supports AirDrop on Pixel phones from the 8a model onward and various Samsung Galaxy S devices. Android phones incompatible with AirDrop can instead select "Use QR Code" within the Quick Share menu, allowing an iPhone user to scan the code to receive the file.

IPhone users can verify this feature is working by opening their settings. They need to go to apps and then messages to make sure the Rich Communication Services toggle is green. This feature is actually turned on by default in the new software.

Users should choose the high definition plus original quality option instead of the optimized version to keep their files completely intact.

An IPhone user can then scan that code as a solid last resort.
