═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Muslim pitmasters across Texas are putting a halal spin on traditional barbecue, offering beef, turkey, and lamb raised and butchered according to Islamic guidelines at establishments like Goldee's BBQ in Fort Worth, Levant Mediterranean BBQ in Houston and Frisco, Yearby's Barbecue and Waterice in Plano, and Kafi BBQ in Irving. At least a dozen barbecue spots across the state now serve halal meats, driven by a new crop of pitmasters blending traditional Texas smoking techniques with family heritages and international spices, such as tandoori smoked turkey, 13-spice Wagyu brisket, and Iraqi-inspired spicy beef sausage. The rise of halal barbecue comes at a time when some Republican leaders in Texas have targeted the state's growing Muslim communities, with some GOP lawmakers even discussing a ban on halal food. Despite this political hostility, halal barbecue joints are thriving and drawing diverse crowds of diners from various backgrounds. Salahodeen Abdul-Kafi, owner of Kafi BBQ who moved to Texas six years ago after working in Silicon Valley, noted that the success of these businesses directly contradicts claims that Muslims do not integrate into society. As food writer Farhan Mustafa stated, halal food is now part of the American culinary mainstream, describing it as a multibillion-dollar industry. Mustafa noted, "No matter how much they try to erase us, our food is here to stay."
