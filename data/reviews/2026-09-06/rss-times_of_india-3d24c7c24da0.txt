═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

India has maintained a 7.8% real GDP growth rate in Q1 FY27 despite global headwinds from the US-Iran war and 50% US reciprocal tariffs introduced under the Trump administration in late 2025. The resilience was anchored by domestic consumption, supported by tax-free income up to Rs 12 lakh starting April 2025 and subsequent GST rate cuts. 

To counter energy vulnerabilities linked to Middle East supply routes, the government diversified procurement, boosted Russian crude imports, and increased US LPG and LNG supplies, while invoking the Essential Commodities Act. Although India's crude oil import bill hit a record $49.7 billion in Q1—up about 61% year-on-year—the Indian basket crude price fell from an April peak of $114.5 per barrel to $83.2 in June and $82.0 in July. 

To stabilize the external sector, the Reserve Bank of India absorbed currency-hedging costs for NRI deposits under a special swap scheme. This attracted $136.38 billion, with FCNR(B) deposits contributing $127.23 billion, lifting forex reserves to a record $740.80 billion by late August, equivalent to about 11 months of import cover. 

Retail CPI inflation reached a 19-month high of 4.45% in July but remained within the RBI target range of 2% to 6%. Governor Sanjay Malhotra projected full-year 2026-27 inflation at 5.0%, with Q2 at 4.7%, Q3 at 5.9%, and Q4 at 5.5%. 

Economists caution that the recovery remains narrow and two-speed, driven largely by urban and asset-linked services rather than broad-based mass consumption. DK Srivastava of EY India noted that annual GDP growth is expected to stay above 7% despite ongoing regional conflicts and trade uncertainties.
