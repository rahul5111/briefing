═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Initial projections show the far-right Alternative for Germany is heading for a victory in the eastern state of Saxony-Anhalt, set to win a record 44% of the vote. That puts the party far ahead of its mainstream rivals, with the conservative CDU trailing at 17.8%. No far-right party has won control of a German state since World War Two, and governing at the state level would grant the AfD powers over policing, local education, and culture. The state has a population of 2.1 million and voter turnout was estimated at a high 78%. Ulrich Siegmund, the charismatic state leader of the AfD, called the result sensational and told supporters, "This isn't just about Saxony-Anhalt. It's a signal for the whole of Germany - a self-confident signal." Whether the party can secure enough seats to govern depends on the performance of smaller parties, specifically the populist left-wing BSW. If the BSW clears the 5% threshold to enter parliament, the AfD is currently heading for 39 seats in the 83-seat state parliament. Other political parties currently maintain a firewall refusing to form coalitions with the AfD, though the BSW has offered to cooperate on some issues. The pro-Russia and anti-migration party's projected result exceeded earlier opinion polls. CDU state leader Sven Schulze conceded defeat, while national SPD leader Lars Klingbeil described the outcome as truly dramatic. The result also drew international attention, with US President Donald Trump posting the projection on Truth Social, prompting Russian presidential envoy Kirill Dmitriev to praise a historic win for the pragmatic AfD. Charlotte Knobloch, the 93-year-old Holocaust survivor and head of the Jewish community in Munich and Bavaria, responded to the news by saying it frightened her and that she finds it hard to recognise her homeland anymore.
