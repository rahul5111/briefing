═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The UK government has introduced late amendments to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill to grant ministers powers to block critical-sector organizations from using technology suppliers deemed high risk. Introduced to Parliament in November 2025, the bill has passed the House of Commons, moved to the House of Lords as HL Bill 32, and is nearing Royal Assent to become the Cyber Security and Resilience Act. The legislative response followed a report by The Telegraph on August 22, 2026, revealing that Iran-linked adversaries targeted and forced a small-scale UK energy facility offline for four days. Government ministers tabled the supply chain amendments on August 24, 2026. Darren Guccione, CEO and co-founder at Keeper Security, stated that the incident and purported nation-state involvement have sharpened appetite for the bill's power to designate critical suppliers. Shankar Haridas, UK business head at ManageEngine, noted that the bill classifies cyber threats against hospitals or water supplies as public safety threats rather than mere IT problems. Jamie Akhtar, CEO and co-founder at CyberSmart, explained that defenses of critical infrastructure organizations can be undermined if attackers exploit smaller suppliers down the chain. Keeper Security research indicates that 34 percent of UK organizations report incidents involving third-party vendors or suppliers.
