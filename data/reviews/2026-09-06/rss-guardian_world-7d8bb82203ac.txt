═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Jamaican delegates led by culture minister Olivia Grange and members of the National Council on Reparations arrived in the UK over the weekend to hand a landmark petition to King Charles on Monday. First announced in June last year, the petition asks the monarch to use his authority to request legal advice from the London-based privy council on whether the forced transport of Africans to Jamaica was lawful, if it constituted a crime against humanity, and whether Britain is obligated to provide restitution. Grange stated that between the 15th and 19th centuries, more than 12.5 million Africans were kidnapped, forcibly transported to the Americas, and sold into slavery, leaving former colonies at a severe economic disadvantage that impedes their ability to tackle modern issues like the climate crisis. As Grange told the Guardian regarding inherited systemic problems, we still have a lot of work to do, and you can imagine if we did not have those inherited problems, the legacy that came out of slavery, how much better we would have been able to do so. The visit precedes a Caribbean reparations conference in Barbados later this month, advocacy at the UN general assembly in late September, and the Commonwealth heads of government meeting in Antigua in November. A palace spokesperson noted that King Charles has repeatedly expressed a commitment to promoting greater understanding of slavery and addressing historic wrongs.
