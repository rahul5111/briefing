═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The shift toward continuous AI inference requires enterprises to rearchitect data centers as integrated systems rather than optimizing memory, storage, compute, and networking in silos. According to Jim McGregor, founder and principal analyst at Tirias Research, AI is not a single workload but billions of distinct workloads, making data movement and system-level coordination critical. As modern techniques like retrieval-augmented generation demand immediate access to massive databases, data movement has emerged as the primary bottleneck, elevating memory and storage from background infrastructure to strategic assets. McGregor notes that the biggest task is efficiently moving data from one place to another to ensure it can be used effectively, stating that organizations must architect compute, memory, storage, and networking together to maintain efficiency. Because inference workloads are continuous, geographically distributed, and highly sensitive to response time, legacy IT assumptions no longer suffice. Enterprises must adopt modular architectures, define specific workloads rather than relying on generic AI readiness, balance performance with efficiency and cost, and continuously reassess procurement strategies. This integrated approach matters because delays in real-time sectors like healthcare, finance, and robotics directly affect human outcomes, operating costs, and business reputation.
