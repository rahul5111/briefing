During a special live episode of the podcast The Joy of Why recorded at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Philadelphia on July 26, 2026, hosts Janna Levin and Steven Strogatz discussed the growing role of artificial intelligence in mathematics with panelists Akshay Venkatesh of the Institute for Advanced Study, Ravi Vakil of Stanford University, and Alex Kontorovich of Rutgers University. 

The conversation focused on recent milestones, including AlphaProof achieving a silver medal at the 2024 International Mathematical Olympiads, followed by a DeepThink model securing a gold medal in 2025. Vakil noted that while these competitions are valuable for encouraging millions of students to hone their thinking, the models' achievements do not represent superhuman intelligence. Kontorovich highlighted recent progress on Erdős problems, pointing to an autonomous solution by GPT 5.6 of a counterexample to the Erdős unit distance conjecture, which subsequently inspired human mathematicians to solve the sum-product problem a week later. 

The panel addressed concerns about the sustainability of peer review amid a rising volume of automated outputs. Venkatesh warned that community systems like academic journals will face immense strain, while Vakil noted that mathematicians are already bracing for an influx of AI-generated work. Kontorovich described the current landscape as a potential golden age of learning, though he cautioned that theorems primarily hold value for human mathematicians and corporate marketing rather than intrinsic universal value.
