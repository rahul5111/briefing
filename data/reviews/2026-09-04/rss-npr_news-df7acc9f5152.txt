═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The Trump administration plans to begin excavation work for a proposed 250-foot arch near Washington, D.C.'s National Mall within the next two weeks, moving forward without final approval from the National Capital Planning Commission or federally mandated reviews for aviation safety and historic preservation. Interior Secretary Doug Burgum announced the upcoming prep work on social media Thursday. The White House told NPR that work will begin that does not require approval and that it will continue seeking final sign-offs. The arch, officially termed the Great Triumphal Arch and Military Observation Deck, is slated for Memorial Circle, a traffic island between Arlington National Cemetery and the Lincoln Memorial. An architectural historian and three Vietnam War veterans, represented by the nonprofit Public Citizen, sued earlier this year to stop the project, arguing it desecrates the cemetery and requires congressional approval. On Friday, the plaintiffs asked a judge for a 14-day restraining order to block construction steps at Memorial Circle. Attorney Nicolas Sansone stated that any preparatory work is premature and lacks a legal basis. On Thursday, the administration filed a motion to dismiss the lawsuit, citing a recent Supreme Court ruling regarding the White House ballroom that found the challenging historic preservation group lacked standing. Sansone argued that his clients have a deeper personal connection to the site than mere offense. The National Park Service released a report confirming the white-and-gold structure, topped with three gilded statues, would disrupt the historical significance of dozens of nearby sites, though the agency maintained its location is necessary.
