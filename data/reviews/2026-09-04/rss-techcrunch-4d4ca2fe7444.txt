═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A federal court in Delaware has issued a split preliminary injunction in a trademark dispute between Elon Musk’s X and the startup Operation Bluebird. U.S. District Court Judge Colm F. Connolly ruled that the startup must stop using its original name, Twitter.now, siding with X on the core Twitter trademark. However, the judge denied X’s request to block the startup from using the word “tweet” and the Twitter bird logo, finding that X had likely abandoned those two related marks with no intention of resuming their use. Following the ruling, the Virginia-based startup rebranded its website as Tweet.app and launched to the public. The effort is led by founder Michael Peroff and Stephen Coates, a former trademark lawyer at Twitter. The startup is charging users $20 to reserve a handle and join the network, and told TechCrunch that more than 172,000 people requested a handle before launch. The court's decision is not final, and the case will proceed to determine whether X ultimately retains rights to the disputed marks. Describing the outcome in an email announcement to TechCrunch, Coates wrote, they kept the word, they let go of the bird, and they let go of the tweet. A tweet was never a corporation, it is one person saying something, that word survived three years of a company trying to replace it, because the public declined to stop using it, we think that tells you who it belongs to.
