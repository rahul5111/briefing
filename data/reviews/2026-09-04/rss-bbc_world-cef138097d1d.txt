═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Europe is facing an escalating campaign of sabotage targeting military and defence facilities, with officials pointing to Russia as the chief suspect. Germany's interior minister explicitly stated that Russia is responsible after explosive-packed drones were discovered at Leipzig airport on August 4. This incident was followed by a wave of suspicious fires and attacks across the continent through August. 

On August 10, a major fire broke out at a warehouse belonging to Bulgarian defence firm EMCO in Tryavna. On August 13, an explosion occurred at a KNDS Ammo facility outside Rome, Italy. Two days later, a fire broke out at the Milrem Robotics factory in Tallinn, Estonia, leading to two arrests. In Slovakia, police announced on August 25 that they had averted an arson attack on a Ukrainian-owned drone manufacturer and seized a large amount of incendiary mixture, arresting three suspects. Poland reported multiple incidents, including a fire at a helicopter component company in Lublin on August 30 and an attack on drone producer WB Group caught on CCTV. In September, two Bulgarian nationals were arrested in Munich after throwing incendiary devices at defence firm Rhode & Schwartz. 

Polish interior minister Marcin Kierwiński stated that there are many signals that Russia is changing its strategy and intensifying terrorist and sabotage activities. While Russian President Vladimir Putin has demanded proof and officials called the accusations absurd, experts suggest the campaign is designed for coercive signalling to pressure countries into halting support for Ukraine, or to test NATO for vulnerabilities and prepare for future aggression. The vast majority of these operations are carried out by disposable Russian proxies recruited online from former Soviet states, though some incidents suggest the use of professional operatives. German federal police have listed more than 165 suspected sabotage cases nationwide this year.
