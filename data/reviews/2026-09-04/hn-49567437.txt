═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A developer has introduced an open-source electronic ink bike computer featuring a four-point-seven-inch e-paper panel designed for readability in direct sunlight. The single-board hardware integrates GPS, capacitive touch, a front light, a battery, and a USB-C port, alongside an SD card slot intended for offline maps, routes, ride files, and logs. Connectivity is handled via Bluetooth five to support heart-rate, power, and cadence sensors, while firmware updates can be installed directly from a desktop Chromium browser over USB. 

The device comes with several technical tradeoffs, including a measured battery baseline of roughly seven point four hours of usable life from a one thousand five hundred milliamp-hour build with the front light off. It lacks a barometric altimeter, meaning climbing data and total ascent are estimated from elevation baked into map tiles rather than measured. Navigation is further limited by a basic GPS receiver that lacks modern multi-band capabilities, and the absence of a magnetometer prevents the map from orienting via compass while the user is stopped. Physical interaction relies primarily on the touch panel rather than hardware buttons, presenting potential challenges when using winter gloves or riding in the rain. Additionally, the bare board ships without a seal or an IP rating, requiring a custom case or bag for wet-weather use.
