═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez told parliament on Thursday that the Spanish government has seen no evidence whatsoever that Morocco planned or facilitated a mass border breach into the north African territory of Ceuta in late July. Addressing lawmakers the morning after about 50,000 people marched in Madrid to protest his handling of the crisis, Sánchez stated that neither the diplomatic service, the European Commission, nor any international body had provided solid evidence of Moroccan government involvement. He added that authorities received no warnings anticipating the scale or probability of the event, which involved an estimated 70,000 people crossing the border, though he conceded Moroccan forces allowed crossings to occur during a 10-hour period on July 30. As many as 141 people are thought to have died during the influx, which triggered a humanitarian emergency and a political crisis. Sánchez noted that Spain summoned Morocco's ambassador on August 21 following unacceptable statements by two Moroccan ministers regarding Ceuta and Melilla, including sovereignty claims reiterated by Moroccan justice minister Abdellatif Ouahbi. While about 90% of those who crossed returned to Morocco within 24 hours, several thousand people, including at least 500 minors, remain in Ceuta in makeshift shelters or on the street. Rejecting calls from the far-right Vox party to summarily return the remaining migrants, Sánchez cited legal obligations, stating that migrants have a right to be treated as individuals and cannot simply be dragged across the frontier. Sánchez also appealed for European solidarity during his address, arguing the principle should apply to Ceuta as much as to Greenland and Lampedusa.
