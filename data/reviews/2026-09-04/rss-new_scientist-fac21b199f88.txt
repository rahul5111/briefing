═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Aedes aegypti mosquitoes and their larvae have been discovered breeding at residential properties in east London, marking the first time the species has been found breeding in the UK. The UK Health Security Agency announced the detection on 3 September, noting it is the fourth time the species has been spotted in the country in recent years. The insects were reported under the government Mosquito Watch scheme. Officials destroyed the larvae and stated that the area will be monitored closely to eradicate any further specimens. 

The UKHSA insists the threat to public health remains very low, with no evidence that the London mosquitoes have transmitted infections. César López-Camacho at the University of Oxford described the discovery as an early warning rather than an immediate public-health threat. Jolyon Medlock of the UKHSA explained that the mosquitoes likely arrived as accidental importations, hitchhiking in vehicles, suitcases, or imported plants. 

While the UK just experienced its hottest summer on record, Medlock noted that the British climate remains too cold overall for the mosquitoes to establish long-term breeding populations for now. However, mosquito-borne viruses are becoming more common across Europe as the continent warms, with French authorities reporting local dengue, West Nile, and chikungunya cases in late August, and West Nile virus having been detected in UK mosquitoes in Nottinghamshire last year. 

Authorities advise residents to eliminate standing water around homes—such as in plant water trays, rain barrels, pet bowls, and flower vases—to prevent invasive species from gaining a foothold. Anyone spotting a mosquito in the UK can report it via the Mosquito Watch scheme.
