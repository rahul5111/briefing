═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A Maltese jury on Wednesday acquitted 44-year-old businessman Yorgen Fenech of ordering the 2017 murder of investigative journalist Daphne Caruana Galizia. Eight of the nine jurors rejected the prosecution's argument that Fenech contracted the October 16, 2017 assassination, which was carried out using a car bomb. The verdict provoked angry protests outside the Valletta courthouse, where demonstrators shouted mafia and murderer as Fenech departed. The victim's son, Matthew Caruana Galizia, called the decision obscene, stating on Facebook that it flies in the face of all evidence, of all logic, of the truth. Fenech had spent more than five years in detention following his November 20, 2019 arrest aboard his yacht Gio before being released on bail in early 2025. The prosecution case relied heavily on confessed middleman Melvin Theuma, who received a pardon to testify that Fenech ordered the killing over Caruana Galizia's investigations into his company 17 Black and the ElectroGas consortium. Defense lawyers questioned Theuma's credibility and pointed to unpursued evidence regarding former prime minister Joseph Muscat's chief of staff, Keith Schembri, who has denied involvement and was not charged. Judge Edwina Grima has ordered trial evidence passed to the police chief to determine if other suspects will face criminal proceedings.
