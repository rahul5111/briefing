═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

On September 3, 2026, website owner Neil Fraser announced that his internet presence of nearly twenty-five years will be wiped out following a decision to terminate the entire third level of the dot-name hierarchy. Fraser registered neil.fraser.name before YouTube, Facebook, and smartphones existed, and later registered beverly.fraser.name for his daughter. 

On April 15, 2026, registry operator Verisign proposed destroying the third level of dot-name to simplify its administration. Despite Fraser's historical distrust of Verisign—which acquired original operator Global Name Registry years ago—ICANN approved the action on July 28, 2026. Fraser learned of the decision via an email from his registrar. 

Unlike dubious third-level domains sold by independent operators, dot-name was established exclusively as a third-level operation with full whois records, comparable to national extensions. 

The termination means Fraser's website, email address, and connected IoT devices will cease functioning in February, despite his domain being registered and paid for until 2040. Furthermore, once the third-level domains are terminated, the vacant second-level domains are expected to become available for public registration. If another party registers fraser.name, they could recreate neil.fraser.name, potentially hijacking hundreds of online accounts, committing code using his authentication, and seizing control of his IoT devices. 

Fraser is one of 22,000 individuals who will lose their domains under this administrative change.
