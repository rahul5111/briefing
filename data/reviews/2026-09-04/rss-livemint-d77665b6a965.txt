═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A judge declared a mistrial on Friday in the case of Lindsay Clancy, a 36-year-old Massachusetts woman who acknowledged strangling her three young children, Cora, Dawson, and Callan, in January 2023. After seven days of deliberations, the jury of nine women and three men was deadlocked, with eleven jurors favoring a verdict of not guilty by reason of insanity and one holdout opposing it. Judge William Sullivan declared the mistrial after the jury failed to reach the unanimous decision required in criminal proceedings in the United States. Clancy claimed she should be excused from criminal responsibility because she was suffering from postpartum psychosis, a condition her lawyers said causes her to hear a voice ordering her to kill her children. Prosecutors rejected that argument, maintaining she carefully planned the murders and understood the consequences of her actions. During the televised trial, which featured more than 80 witnesses, defense attorney Kevin Reddington argued Clancy received lousy medical care despite making numerous pleas for help before the killings at the family home in Duxbury. Following the murders, Clancy attempted suicide by cutting her neck and wrists and jumping from a second-story window, leaving her paralyzed from the waist down and confined to a wheelchair. Reddington expressed frustration with the outcome, telling reporters, "They were robbed by one man for whatever his agenda was. I hope that guy can sleep well at night." Plymouth County District Attorney Tim Cruz said he would decide shortly whether to try the case again before a new trial, while Clancy will remain at a psychiatric institution pending that decision. If she had been found guilty of first-degree murder, the former nurse faced life in prison without the possibility of parole.
