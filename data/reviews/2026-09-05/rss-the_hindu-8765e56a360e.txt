═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A truck accident near Shillong on the morning of August 24 has triggered renewed scrutiny over illegal coal mining and transportation in Meghalaya, where hazardous rat-hole mining has been banned by the National Green Tribunal since April 2014. In its 40th Interim Report submitted to the High Court of Meghalaya, the Justice retired B.P. Katakey Committee requested a detailed inquiry to determine if the coal carried by the overturned vehicle was legally auctioned or illegally mined. The truck, traveling from Nongstoin in the West Khasi Hills district to Guwahati in Assam, lacked the mandatory Mineral Transport Challan issued by the Directorate of Mineral Resources. Although the truck operator produced documents claiming the load belonged to a successful bidder for 8,338.374 metric tonnes of pre-ban auctioned coal, and presented a photocopy of a challan meant for a different vehicle that allegedly broke down, neither original challan could be produced. The committee found this to be a violation of the Standard Operating Procedure for transporting auctioned coal and directed the police to submit a status report within a fortnight of September 4. Furthermore, the panel questioned why authorities have not confiscated 62 vehicles and 92 equipment items seized in illegal coal trade cases registered between 2023 and August 2026. Police records show that 127 cases of illegal extraction and 64 cases of illegal transportation were registered across the state during this period, while action against landowners and mine owners has been limited to only four cases in the East Jaintia Hills district.
