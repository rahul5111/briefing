═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Group-IB researchers have uncovered the Outsider Phishing Kit, a sophisticated Phishing-as-a-Service platform operated by a threat actor known as ChenLun. The kit features Adversary-in-the-Middle capabilities that intercept authentication flows and bypass multi-factor authentication. Between December 2025 and May 2026, researchers identified more than 100,000 phishing pages targeting over 54 countries using 267 templates. Campaigns are typically delivered via SMS using a dedicated Telegram ecosystem.

In response to the threat, Google filed a civil lawsuit against the Outsider group on June 12. On June 13, the FBI cyber division announced a coordinated partnership with Google and Lumen’s Black Lotus Labs to dismantle the infrastructure through an effort designated Operation Ghost Hook. Despite these actions, the threat persists. Group-IB identified more than 700 new phishing pages created within a single month following the lawsuit, demonstrating that affiliates continue to actively utilize the kit. 

Operators of the platform employ a WebSocket connection for live keylogging and to dynamically manipulate multi-factor authentication challenges. The phishing pages utilize a consistent naming convention with alphabetical prefixes that function as status codes to trace victims through the attack flow, lowering the technical barrier for affiliates. JavaScript components such as common.js manage campaign configurations and branding, while main.js utilizes AES-CTR encryption via the Web Crypto API to transmit harvested credit card data, personal identity details, and security credentials directly to the operator command panel in real time.
