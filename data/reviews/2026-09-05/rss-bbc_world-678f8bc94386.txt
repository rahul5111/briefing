═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Rescuers in Nepal are intensifying efforts to reach scores of workers trapped in tunnels nine days after a devastating flash flood hit the area on the border with Tibet in China. Hopes rose on Friday when two people, Sanjaya Shah and Kabir Maharjan, were pulled alive from the Trishuli 3A hydropower site tunnel, where rescuers heard voices and called out before locating the injured men. Maharjan is in critical condition, and Shah is undergoing medical tests at the Army Hospital in Kathmandu. The body of a third unidentified person was subsequently found in the same tunnel, and officials believe another 39 people remain inside. The August 26 flash flood, believed triggered by a glacial collapse, has left 1,311 confirmed dead and 5,330 still missing across Nepal and Tibet, with hundreds of foreign citizens from at least 34 countries among the missing. In Nepal, rescue operations are underway at seven hydropower tunnels where more than 150 people are feared trapped, alongside 19 other areas. Col Jhabindra Reshmi reported that his teams have rescued more than 400 Nepali nationals and 270 foreign citizens, though rescue efforts have been severely hampered by mud, rocks, and the loss of heavy machinery washed away by floodwaters. Engineers have been pumping air into the tunnels while crews dig access holes. Uttam Bhlon Lama, vice president at Independent Power Producers of Nepal, told the BBC that the rescue of the two men would motivate rescuers, adding that the flood level is still very high. Nepalese authorities have warned that ongoing rainfall may cause additional flooding in the region.
