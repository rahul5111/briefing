═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Casio is expanding its G-Shock lineup with a new series modeled after the Patek Philippe Nautilus, drawing early nicknames like the CasioNaut and CasioTilus on social media. The upcoming collection features prominent shoulders on either side of the case, echoing the famous Nautilus design originally created by Gérald Genta in 1976. 

The upcoming watches are split into two main lines. The GAC-2500 series consists of a resin analog lineup built from the design DNA of the original 1983 DW-5000C G-Shock, with leaked resin colorways expected in white, black, green, and yellow. The stainless-steel versions will be labeled the GMC-2500 series and offer a choice of colors alongside bracelet or strap options. 

Breaking from typical G-Shock tradition, the watches are fully analog without a digital display, featuring a two-eye dial layout with a small seconds subdial on the left and a date window on the right. A Shock Resist mark sits on the tail of the seconds hand. The pieces incorporate Casio's Magnetic Holding Structure, which uses magnetic fields instead of a fixed shaft to hold the hands in place, allowing them to absorb severe impacts without breaking.

The metal GMC-2500 dimensions measure 46.7 by 43.8 by 12.9 millimeters, while the GAC-2500 measures 46.7 by 44.5 by 12.7 millimeters. The resin models are estimated to cost 20,900 Japanese yen, or about $134 each, while the metal versions are expected to retail for approximately $320. All models across the series are rumored for a November release.
