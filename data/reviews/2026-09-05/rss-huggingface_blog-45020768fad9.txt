═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Task-specific fine-tuning of the 350-million parameter LiquidAI LFM2.5 model using 100 steps of Group Relative Policy Optimization significantly improves its schema compliance and structured output reliability. Evaluated on the open-source IFStruct benchmark dataset available on Hugging Face as LiquidAI/ifstruct-v1.0, the base model achieved an overall pass rate of 22.6 percent across 2,000 samples, closely matching the 21.1 percent score reported in the IFStruct release blog. 

The fine-tuning pipeline used about 500 samples from nvidia/Nemotron-RL-instruction_following-structured_outputs, augmented with format instructions and top-level-array tasks. Training attached a LoRA adapter targeting approximately 6 million parameters, or 1.66 percent of the model, using three reward functions scored on a zero to one scale for JSON formatting, field count, and schema validation with weights set to 1.0, 0.5, and 2.0 respectively. The GRPO configuration ran with a learning rate of 5e-5, a batch size of 4, gradient accumulation steps of 8, and 8 generations per prompt group.

After converting the merged checkpoint into a BF16 GGUF format and serving it locally via llama.cpp, the fine-evaluated model scored an overall pass rate of 29.7 percent on the 2,000 IFStruct samples. Specifically, the JSON pass rate jumped by nearly 14 points from 18.0 percent to 31.9 percent, while YAML performance remained stable. This performance closes much of the gap to larger models, bringing the small architecture close to the Qwen3.5-2B score of 33.15 percent.
