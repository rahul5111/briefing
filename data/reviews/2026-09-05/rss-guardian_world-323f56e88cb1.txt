═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Three survivors and relatives of victims of the March 21, 1960, Sharpeville massacre filed a lawsuit against the South African government on Thursday demanding compensation. The historic protest, organized by the Pan Africanist Congress, gathered thousands of residents outside the Sharpeville police station to oppose apartheid-era pass laws. Police opened fire without warning, killing at least 91 people and injuring 238.

The claimants are seeking approximately 500,000 rand, equivalent to 23,000 pounds, per person. The legal team is challenging a 1961 retrospective law passed by the white minority regime that indemnified the government for actions taken during the suppression of the protest, a statute that remains in force. A successful outcome could enable dozens more to file claims. 

Abraham Mofokeng, an 86-year-old survivor who still has a bullet lodged in his spine, is one of the test claimants alongside two individuals whose fathers were killed when they were children. Mofokeng previously received a one-off payment of 30,000 rand after a 2003 Truth and Reconciliation Commission recommendation, falling short of the commission's proposed annual reparations. “We continue to suffer,” Mofokeng said. “Especially now … I am struggling and my health is not up to standard.”

The litigation is part of a broader wave of legal actions seeking justice for apartheid-era crimes amid widespread disillusionment with ANC-led governments over persistent racial inequality and poverty. Spokespeople for South Africa’s justice department did not respond to a request for comment.
