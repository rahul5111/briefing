═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Educators and experts in the frontend web development space are stepping back or pivoting their focus as artificial intelligence changes the industry. Figures like Axel Rauschmayer, Salma Alam-Naylor, and Josh W. Comeau are dialing back their efforts, while Kent C. Dodds, Addy Osmani, Rachel Nabors, and Lydia Hallie have shifted their topics away from traditional frontend development. 

The shift is driven by the capability of AI tools like Claude Sonnet to handle complex frontend analysis, such as diagnosing Chrome traces with high Style Calculation costs. Rather than manual troubleshooting, developers can now rely on AI agents to identify performance bottlenecks, including complex selectors, broad invalidation scopes, and property propagation issues.

This technological evolution is altering the landscape in several ways. Frontend code is generally considered less risky to hand off to an agent compared to database migrations, leading to more unsupervised deployment. Additionally, developer experience is taking a back seat to agent experience. Companies like Cursor and Viget have migrated codebases to React simply because AI agents are more heavily trained on it and know it better. Furthermore, web standards focused solely on ergonomics, such as new CSS shorthands or JavaScript syntax, are expected to decline in perceived importance relative to performance capabilities.

To adapt to this environment, frontend education may need to focus on teaching agents architectural big-picture concepts, such as choosing multi-page application frameworks over single-page applications. Another potential direction is optimizing websites for agents by emphasizing server-rendered content, accessibility, and page speed. Finally, as massive amounts of AI-generated code are produced, consulting opportunities may emerge to fix slow, non-compliant, and poorly structured digital outputs.
