═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

An unprecedented El Niño gathering strength in the Pacific Ocean could bring continuously wet and stormy conditions to the UK until spring, upending standard winter weather forecasts. El Niño occurs when weakened easterly trade winds allow warm water to wash back across the Pacific, heating the atmosphere and weakening winds further. While normal-intensity El Niños typically cause wet weather in late autumn and early winter followed by a colder, drier late winter, the current exceptionally large event may alter that pattern. Adam Scaife, who leads long-range forecasting at the UK Met Office, says very strong El Niños show a different response over the Atlantic. We found that the big events show a slightly different response over the Atlantic, Scaife says, noting that instead of cold easterly winds prevailing in late winter, a very strong event may lead to prevailing westerlies that bring mild and wet conditions. This pattern occurred during the powerful 1997-1998 and 2015-2016 El Niño events, the latter resulting in one of the UKs mildest and wettest winters on record. A prolonged wet and stormy winter could saturate soils and increase the risk of severe flooding while also replenishing reservoirs and groundwater after a dry summer. However, Scaife warns that separating the effects of a strong El Niño from a standard one is difficult due to limited historical data, and significant uncertainty remains regarding late winter weather. The World Meteorological Organisation warned this week that the El Niño is still intensifying in the tropical Pacific and is set to be the largest on record, with the pattern likely to peak in November and persist until at least February 2027.
