═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The developer behind the PJON network protocol has released TERMy, a new terminal assistant built on top of the NPC-Forge framework that operates entirely without large language models, machine learning, or embeddings. Developed over two months starting in early July amid rising AI costs, the tool runs locally on a CPU, including on a Raspberry Pi Zero, or client-side in a browser tab, and returns responses in milliseconds. TERMy uses a lightweight natural language understanding pipeline written in approximately one thousand lines of Python to translate natural language into Linux shell commands. The pipeline processes prompts through five stages: noise removal by stripping interjections and expletives, sentiment analysis, exact matching, template matching, and probabilistic matching. The probabilistic stage utilizes Inverse Document Frequency to identify rare words, a Bag of Words approach to handle word inversions, and IDF-weighted Levenshtein distance to account for typos. Unlike unpredictable language models, TERMy features hardcoded permission gating within its dataset to enforce safety for potentially destructive commands. The creator built the assistant to eliminate monthly subscription costs for trivial operations like activating virtual environments, questioning whether trillions of parameters are truly necessary for such tasks. The source code is available on GitHub under the NPC-Forge repository, and video demonstrations showcasing its operation, typo tolerance, and Copilot integration have been published online.
