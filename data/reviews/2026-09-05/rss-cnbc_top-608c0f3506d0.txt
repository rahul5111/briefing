═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Ten days ahead of a Federal Reserve meeting scheduled for September 15-16 where a rate hike will likely be considered, the Trump administration has launched a broad public pressure campaign to stop the increase and prompt rate cuts. Over the past week, President Donald Trump, Vice President JD Vance, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, and senior economic counselor Peter Navarro have all publicly urged the central bank against raising rates. On Friday, President Trump escalated the pressure by threatening on Truth Social to halt trade with countries that run trade surpluses with the United States unless the Fed lowers rates, marking the first time he has directly linked tariffs to Federal Reserve policy. Navarro warned in an interview with Steve Bannon that a rate hike would be careless and called members of the rate-setting Federal Open Market Committee clowns. Vice President Vance stated that the administration believes the Fed should be lowering interest rates, and Bessent argued in a CNBC interview that the Fed typically avoids raising rates during a supply shock. Markets are pricing in a roughly 60% probability for a rate hike at the upcoming meeting, which falls two months before November midterm elections where the administration faces voter dissatisfaction over high prices and interest rates. Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh has previously maintained that the president has had no impact on his decisions and emphasized that the central bank's focus must remain squarely on inflation, noting in a Jackson Hole speech that 54 percent of the components in the Fed's preferred PCE price measure had risen more than 3 percent over the previous 12 months.
