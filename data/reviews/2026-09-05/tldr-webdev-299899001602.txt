═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Venture capital has transformed into an unaccountable oligarchy driven by a handful of billionaire extremists, according to a tech founder who has raised tens of millions in startup funding. The traditional model of high-risk investing in new companies has been warped by massive "do everything" funds that combine private equity with VC, managing tens of billions of dollars. The top one percent of these firms collect two percent management fees on funds reaching fifty billion dollars, guaranteeing a billion dollars in annual income regardless of whether their investments succeed or fail. This shields them from market risk and separates them from traditional venture capital legal structures. This concentration of power has flipped the dynamic between founders and investors, turning founders into executors of VCs political plans while public pension funds and retirement accounts unknowingly absorb the actual portfolio risk. The firms cash out long before portfolio companies ever reach an IPO or turn a profit, utilizing self-dealing tactics such as selling investments to their own internal funds to book artificial profits. The political influence of these entities has scaled drastically, with Andreessen Horowitz and its co-founders Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz contributing $115.3 million in the current midterm cycle, nearly double their $63 million spend in 2024. According to Tech Influence Watch data tracked by Molly White, a16z accounts for over twenty percent of all political contributions from the crypto and AI companies it monitors, funneling money to candidates across both major parties. Former partner John O’Farrell recently criticized the firm for its political infiltration of artificial intelligence policy. Additionally, Andreessen Horowitz hired a partner with no background in tech, finance, or startups whose only qualification was having choked an unarmed neighbor, Jordan Neely, to death on a subway car.
