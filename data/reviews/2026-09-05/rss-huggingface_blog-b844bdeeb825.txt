═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

On 23 August, a video posted by Surya Narreddi showing language model-generated watercolour paintings written in JavaScript through the p5.brush library went viral, garnering over 1.5 million views. Following his work, an open reproduction project was launched using TRL and OpenEnv on Hugging Face, utilizing Qwen/Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as the base model. The entire pipeline, including the RL environment, scorer model, pairwise judge, and trained models, has been published in a Hugging Face collection.

The project explores whether reinforcement learning can be driven by aesthetic preference rather than verifiable correctness. The reward system combines two judges. HPSv3, an open 7-billion parameter preference model, scores images based on general human choices, while a pairwise judge, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct, compares candidate paintings against four references drawn from a curated pool of 178 model-generated images. The reference pool is divided into two tiers, love and okay, ensuring that early policies receive gradient signal. 

Training is performed through an environment that wraps the p5.brush library, restricting the model to a specific allowlist of ten methods out of 47 to maintain the watercolour style. The final pipeline executes via a Hugging Face Jobs command using specific training parameters, including a learning rate of 5e-5, a batch size of 1 with gradient accumulation steps of 8, and 110 steps across 240 episodes. The implementation demonstrates that smaller models can be successfully steered toward personal artistic preferences by curating the underlying reference dataset.

═══ 02_REDRAFT_HALLUCINATIONS ═══

On 23 August, a video posted by Surya Narreddi showing language model-generated watercolour paintings written in JavaScript through the p5.brush library went viral, garnering over 1.5 million views. The article describes an attempt to reproduce his idea using TRL and OpenEnv, with the reference pool dataset, RL environment, training scripts, and trained models all published openly on Hugging Face. The entire pipeline runs on Hugging Face end to end, with training on Jobs, the RL environment and scorer model as Spaces, the pairwise judge through Inference Providers, and every artifact gathered in one collection.

The project explores whether reinforcement learning can be driven by aesthetic preference rather than verifiable correctness. The reward system combines two judges. HPSv3, an open 7B preference model, scores images based on general human choices, while a pairwise judge, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct, compares candidate paintings against four references randomly selected from a pool of 178 model-generated images. The reference pool is divided into two tiers based on personal preferences, love and okay, with the pairwise judge drawing half from each tier so that a weak early policy still gets signal. 

Training is performed through an environment that wraps the p5.brush library, restricting the model to a strict allowlist of ten methods out of 47 to maintain the watercolour style. The pipeline runs using a command that specifies parameters such as the H200 flavor, a learning rate of 5e-5, 110 steps, 240 episodes, 8 num-generations, a per-device-batch-size of 1, and gradient-accumulation-steps of 8. The implementation demonstrates that smaller models can be successfully steered toward personal artistic preferences by curating the underlying reference dataset.
