═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Following an enforcement drive by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India that discovered expired infant food products at private companies, paediatricians and lactation consultants are warning new parents against using formula-based products. Experts emphasize that mother's milk is the sole ideal option, with alternatives including Nandini milk or milk from hospital-affiliated banks. 

Hajira Tarannum, an infant and young child feeding counsellor in paediatrics, noted that commercial formula foods are expensive, ranging from ₹700 to ₹1,800, and stated, “Most companies sell formula foods by stating that they are close to mother’s milk, but they are not.” Tarannum also warned that feeding regular cow milk causes diarrhea and gastrointestinal issues in infants, while recommending the blue packet of Nandini milk boiled, cooled, and given without added water if maternal feeding fails.

Dr. Niranjan, a neonatologist at Indira Gandhi Institute of Child Health, explained that the hospital operates a screened milk bank where donor mothers undergo testing for HIV, Hepatitis B and C, syphilis, and human T-lymphotropic virus, with stored milk remaining viable for six months. However, doctors do not actively encourage milk donation if it reduces the supply needed for a mother's own infant. 

Joyce Jayaseelan, a lactation consultant at Fortis Hospital, pointed to guidelines from the World Health Organisation and the Breastfeeding Promotion Network of India advising that no formula be given to infants under six months, as it can prevent babies from latching onto the breast. After six months, doctors recommend introducing water, mother's milk, and semi-solid foods based on dietary preferences.

However, doctors do not encourage mothers to donate milk to banks as it leads to less milk for their infants. 

Joyce Jayaseelan, a lactation consultant at Fortis Hospital, pointed to rules of the World Health Organisation and the Breastfeeding Promotion Network of India suggesting that no formula should be given to infants below six months, as it prevents babies from latching onto the breast. After six months, doctors advise mothers to provide mother's milk, water, and semi-solid foods depending on vegetarian or non-vegetarian preferences.
