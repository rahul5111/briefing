═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Two ongoing network intrusion and data-exfiltration campaigns targeting organizations in Latin America have been analyzed, revealing that attackers are using commercial large language models to orchestrate operations and overcome execution failures. The activity spans two distinct clusters. The first, tracked as CL-CRI-1131 and also known as Operation Escaneo, targeted a transportation organization, federal government ministries, and municipal water utilities in Mexico and Ecuador during compromises observed in April and June 2026. This campaign utilized living-off-the-land techniques, batch scripts, and self-hosted NextChat instances on infrastructure hosted at the IP addresses 178.128.87.160 and 62.171.185.97, utilizing the domain m-doxa-apodo.duckdns.org. The second cluster, tracked as CL-CRI-1163, targeted the Brazilian financial sector starting in February 2026 following a job-themed phishing campaign. Attackers in this cluster deployed custom remote access Trojans and a Go-based SOCKS5 proxy named SockTz, attempting to install versions 1 through 9 within a two-hour window from an open directory at 167.148.195.53. In both campaigns, iterative script naming conventions, descriptive adjectives like exploit_creative.py and exploit_careful.py, and trial-and-error troubleshooting indicated the use of artificial intelligence to generate workaround scripts and streamline execution. According to the analysis, this integration serves as a force multiplier for threat actors, but foundational operational security failures, such as exposed staging directories and unsecured NextChat interfaces, left their playbook and prompt history visible to researchers and provide defenders with a clear roadmap to track and disrupt the campaigns.
