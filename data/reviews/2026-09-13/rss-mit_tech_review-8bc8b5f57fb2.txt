═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The United States energy storage market is experiencing record growth, heavily supported by cheap Chinese batteries, even as Washington attempts to reduce its reliance on them. In late August, the Trump administration issued an executive order declaring a national emergency that effectively bans Chinese batteries from grid-scale energy storage systems, along with inverters and transformers. 

This follows earlier measures, including Section 301 tariffs that increased import taxes on batteries to 25% in January, up from 7.5%, and tax credit restrictions originating from the 2022 Inflation Reduction Act. Under upcoming legislation taking effect in 2026, 55% of the cost of materials for new energy storage projects must come from outside China and other restricted countries for projects to qualify for tax credits.

An outright ban was a bit of a surprise, and it does create a bit of concern for domestic players in the US, according to Shan Tomouk, energy storage and energy lead for Benchmark Mineral Intelligence. BloombergNEF analysis indicates the move will likely slow deployment in the near term and cause project delays. Isshu Kikuma, an energy storage analyst at BloombergNEF, notes that in the worst case, projects could face cancellation if they cannot find alternative, more expensive sources following Department of Energy guidance expected by the end of the year. 

While the order technically applies to existing plants, analysts consider it unlikely they will be taken offline, as doing so would remove most installed battery storage from the grid. Longer term, the US could reach enough domestic capacity by around 2030, with support from new factories by companies including LG Energy Solutions, Samsung SDI, Ford, and SK On, though domestic supply may not fully meet demand until later in the 2030s as the market absorbs these higher costs.
