═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio outlined a shift in the Trump administration's strategy for combating drug trafficking in Latin American waters during a trip to Quito, Ecuador, stating the focus is now on working with allied countries and aligning with their laws, though he insisted the military would still blow up ships if need be. The public acknowledgment follows a year of unilateral deadly strikes against alleged narco-terrorists operating boats in international waters that began in September 2025. Late Wednesday, the US Southern Command announced a strike in the Caribbean Sea that killed three people, bringing the total death toll to at least 230 people across 69 strikes. Unlike earlier unilateral actions, recent operations involve joint efforts where people on floating refueling stations are transferred to local authorities before the US military destroys the vessels. The administration is building agreements with regional allies including Colombia and Ecuador. Rubio told reporters in Quito that it is an incredible advance for countries to participate in efforts within their territorial waters, noting that the important point is that these trafficking routes are being disrupted and ended. He described the approach as a strike-by-strike or operation-by-operation determination depending on the threat and location of the boat. Rubio's remarks were delivered during a three-country South American tour aimed at boosting security cooperation with conservative leaders.
