═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

On New Year’s Day in 2022, the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory detected a massive burst of gamma rays designated GRB 220101A from a distant galaxy. After four years of analysis, an astronomical team led by Remo Ruffini at the International Center for Relativistic Astrophysics Network in Italy has proposed that the event involved a collapsing star triggering the formation of both a pulsar and a black hole in a complex multi-stage sequence. According to the researchers, the event began with the core collapse of a massive older carbon-oxygen star, which released an accelerating cloud of electrons and positrons in a hypothetical event called an HB supernova. This cloud allegedly collided with a nearby white dwarf, causing it to collapse into a neutron star and trigger a second supernova blast 3.5 seconds after the first. This second blast then struck a third nearby stellar object, another neutron star, which released a dramatic burst of energy before collapsing into a black hole. Meanwhile, fast-moving material falling onto the new neutron star caused it to spin faster and faster within milliseconds, turning it into a pulsar. Ruffini describes the event by saying it is the most complex system ever observed in astrophysics. However, other researchers urge caution. Ryan Ridden at the University of Canterbury in New Zealand notes that extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence, stating that the observations do not definitively support the novel model and could equally fit a scenario ending with a single black hole and no neutron star.
