═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The creator of the small puzzle app Dayzle spent CA$220 on a two-week Google Ads campaign for Android, set to an install goal, only to discover that roughly 60 percent of the billed installs were driven by what appears to be a bot farm. After removing a target cost-per-install of $1.50, the campaign spent CA$80 in a single day and reported 21 installs. Raw analytics revealed that 20 of those devices were running an old version of the app no longer served by the Play Store, yet all claimed Google Play was the installer. Each device opened the app once for zero seconds and never returned, spanning 28 phone models across 19 states. Over the entire two-week campaign, Google billed 56 installs, of which 33 matched the bot pattern, 7 originated from untargeted countries, and only 13 were genuine users. The developer found that Google's algorithm optimized for the install goal by feeding a feedback loop, as bot farms viewed the shortest ad video and installed a saved copy of the file rather than downloading from the store, tricking Google into sending more ads to the farm. In response, the developer filed an invalid-traffic form with Google and changed the campaign conversion goal from app opens to winning a puzzle, raising the effort required for a script to farm the app.
