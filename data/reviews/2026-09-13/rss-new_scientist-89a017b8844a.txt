═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

US psychologist Eli Susman has released a new book titled Micropractice: a science-backed approach for calm, clarity and joy in 30 seconds or less, which argues that mindfulness and lifestyle benefits can be achieved through exercises lasting 30 seconds or less. Research highlighted in the book demonstrates that consistency matters more than duration. In one past study, participants who performed a 20-second daily self-compassion meditation involving placing one hand on the belly and the other on the chest for a month showed considerably less stress and better mental health compared to a control group doing finger-tapping exercises. Susman writes that study after study pointed to the same overlooked pattern, noting that consistency, not duration, is what drives change. Similar effects were found for slow breathing exercises, where a large meta-analysis showed the size of effects did not change based on how long participants practiced. Additionally, a study of surgeons performing 90-second stretches every 40 to 60 minutes improved their mental focus and reduced physical pain. To help people build these routines, Susman outlines strategies including anchoring habits to specific regular cues, keeping track of progress, and allowing two emergency skip days each week to avoid the shame spiral that typically derails progress. In a forthcoming study, Susman found that teaching these tips via a 15-minute video vastly increased the likelihood that participants would continue the self-compassion meditation over the following six months.
