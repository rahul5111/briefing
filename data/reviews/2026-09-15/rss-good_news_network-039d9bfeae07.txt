A former Athens airport site is transforming into the Ellinikon Park, which will stretch over 400 acres along the sea in a southern suburb. Planners designed the space to serve as a shaded refuge for residents of Europe's hottest city. The project aims to keep the park 7.2°F cooler than surrounding asphalt and concrete areas. To achieve this thermoregulation, the park will feature 30,000 trees and 3 million smaller plants representing more than 520 species of flora. Rest areas will offer a shading ratio of more than 60%, while water features include running channels and misting systems. Athens previously held the World Meteorological Organization record for the highest temperature ever recorded in Europe at 48.0°C. In 2024, under the mayorship of Haris Doukas, the city began planting its first micro-forest alongside greening projects across six neighborhoods. The Ellinikon Park will utilize onsite water treatment facilities and a giant rainwater catchment system to support its flora. The park is planned to open to the public next year, with future renovations scheduled for 2030.
