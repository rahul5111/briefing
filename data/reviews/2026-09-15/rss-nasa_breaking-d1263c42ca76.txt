The Republic of Djibouti officially became the 72nd nation and eighth African country to sign the Artemis Accords during a ceremony held Monday at NASA Headquarters in Washington. Ambassador of Djibouti to the United States Mohamed Siad Douale signed the agreement on behalf of his country. The event was hosted by NASA Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson. Djibouti joins this coalition of like-minded nations at an important time as NASA works to return Americans to the lunar surface and establish an enduring presence there. Djibouti previously launched its national space program in 2020 and successfully launched its first satellites in 2023 and 2024 aboard American rockets from California. By joining the Artemis Accords, Djibouti seeks to deepen its partnership with the United States for peaceful space cooperation. According to Ambassador Douale, the nation looks forward to building partnerships in scientific research, satellite applications, education, and technical training while creating new opportunities for Djibouti students, researchers, engineers, and entrepreneurs. The Artemis Accords establish practical principles to guide space exploration cooperation among participating nations, covering activities such as peaceful exploration, rendering aid, enabling access to scientific data, and preserving historically significant sites. U.S. State Department Assistant Secretary for African Affairs Frank Garcia also participated in the signing event. Through this agreement, participating nations open the door to future opportunities for lunar exploration with NASA, shaping the golden age of space exploration and innovation.
