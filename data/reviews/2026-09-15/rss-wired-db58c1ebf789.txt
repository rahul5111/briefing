═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

On Monday, the Manhattan District Attorney's Office announced the seizure of 12 domains used for unlawfully sharing, publishing, and selling celebrity deepfake videos. Around 1,200 people, overwhelmingly women including social media influencers, actors, activists, athletes, musicians, and politicians, were depicted in nonconsensual sexual images and videos on the websites. These seizures mark perhaps one of the most significant and sweeping actions against websites hosting explicit deepfake videos since the technology first emerged at the end of 2017. District attorney Alvin Bragg stated in a release that these horrific violations of privacy follow victims into their careers and personal lives and take an immense toll on emotional and mental well-being. Researchers noted that on at least two of the seized websites, elected officials were given profiles containing their name and position, along with a grid of manipulated videos hosting their likeness. Leonie Oehmig, a researcher at the Institute for Strategic Dialogue, stated that the takedowns are a significant win in the fight against synthetic nonconsensual intimate imagery. Oehmig added that enforcement of state laws shows that harmful content can be tackled and that meaningful coordinated enforcement is achievable even where regulation remains fragmented across jurisdictions. Investigators from the Manhattan District Attorney's Office stated that their investigations into who is responsible for the websites and who uploaded damaging videos of victims are currently ongoing.
