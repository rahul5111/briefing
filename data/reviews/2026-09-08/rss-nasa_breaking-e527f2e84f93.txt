═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

More than 1,000 visitors explored NASA sea level science on June 6, 2026, during a free World Ocean Day event at the University of Georgia Aquarium on Skidaway Island. The event was supported by NASA’s Science Activation program through the Sea Level Education, Awareness, and Literacy project, known as SEAL. SEAL is a partnership between NASA and four NOAA Sea Grant programs that connects NASA sea level rise observations, models, and projections with educators and coastal communities. 

During the event, partners including Marine Extension and Georgia Sea Grant and the Coastal Equity and Resiliency Hub at Georgia Tech hosted hands-on activities. Visitors played a Tumbling Tower game representing community impacts from rising seas, made sea level friendship bracelets using colored glass beads to show local sea level rise, and experienced the debut of an augmented reality sandbox. The sandbox allowed guests to shape coastal landscapes and watch NASA-informed sea level rise projections transform their creations in real time while testing resilience strategies like adding dunes or relocating homes. Educators also received four different SEAL lesson plans designed for classrooms and informal learning settings.

World Ocean Day is an annual tradition at the UGA Aquarium, according to Shannon Matzke, Marine Educator and Public Program Coordinator at UGA Marine Extension and Georgia Sea Grant. With the support of NASA’s Science Activation program, this year's free event reached more people and different audiences than normal, making the day particularly impactful for visitors living in coastal communities already experiencing sea level rise effects. The SEAL project operates under NASA cooperative agreement award number NNH21ZDA001N-SCIACT.
