═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Electric vehicle batteries are retaining about 90 percent of their original usable capacity after 150,000 kilometers, according to a new study that addresses long-term durability concerns for drivers considering a switch from petrol models. 

The study was published by Aviloo, an Austria-based group that analyzes battery health, and is based on more than 500,000 tests carried out globally between 2022 and 2026 across 20 popular EV models, including Tesla’s Model Y and Volkswagen’s ID.4. Chinese models were not included due to a lack of data. 

The findings showed that the median state of health, representing the remaining percentage of a battery’s original usable capacity, stood between 87 percent and 94 percent after 150,000 km. At earlier milestones, the median state of health was between 91 and 97 percent after 50,000 km, and 88 and 95 percent after 100,000 km. These results outpace standard EV battery warranties, which typically cover eight years or 160,000 km and require manufacturers to provide a replacement if capacity falls below 70 percent. 

The study also found that the speed of battery degradation varied significantly within the same EV model depending on climate conditions, battery size, and driver habits such as parking. Marcus Berger, chief executive of Aviloo, noted the challenge this creates for valuation, stating that in contrast to a combustion engine car where age and mileage determine technical condition, the car might look the same with the same age and mileage, yet you do not know how it has been treated.
