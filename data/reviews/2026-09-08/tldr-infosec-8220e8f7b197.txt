═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Online maths learning platform Mathspace disclosed that unknown attackers stole personal data belonging to 1,079,819 students, staff, and parents after breaching its internal reporting system. Founded in Sydney in 2010, Mathspace is used by thousands of schools across Australia, New Zealand, the United States, and the United Kingdom, though only individuals in Australia and New Zealand were impacted by this incident. 

According to Mathspace CTO Alvin Savoy, threat actors gained access to the company's systems on August 10, confirmed the breach on September 3, and downloaded the data from the Australian reporting database on August 27. The attackers exploited a security vulnerability in a self-hosted installation of Metabase, an internal reporting software, which allowed them to obtain administrator access without a legitimate login. 

"On 3 September 2026, we confirmed that unauthorised parties had accessed an internal reporting system used by Mathspace and downloaded information on students, their parents or guardians, and school staff. Mathspace staff records were also affected," Savoy said in a Saturday blog post. 

The compromised data did not include passwords, authentication tokens, academic records, learning activities, or results. While the exposed data did not explicitly include records linking user accounts to their schools, Savoy noted that this may still be possible for schools with identifiable email domains. Mathspace warned affected individuals to watch for suspicious account-related activity, as attackers may target them using the stolen information. 

This incident is part of a broader campaign involving exploited Metabase SQL injection zero-day vulnerabilities that has recently impacted other companies, including Trezor, Framework, and Tally.
