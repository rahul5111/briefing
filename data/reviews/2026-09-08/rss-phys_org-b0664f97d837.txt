═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Theoretical physicists Valerio Faraoni and Massimiliano Rinaldi have published a paper on the arXiv preprint server suggesting that black holes expand along with the universe, challenging the traditional assumption that gravitationally bound objects are immune to cosmic expansion. Standard astrophysics models typically treat black holes as isolated, motionless objects in an empty void using the century-old Schwarzschild solution. Faraoni and Rinaldi used mathematical models to track a black hole's apparent horizon, the boundary where gravity traps light, to see how it interacts with an expanding background. Their models showed that a black hole eventually becomes comoving with the cosmos, expanding at the exact same pace as the universe. This theory could resolve a puzzle raised by the James Webb Space Telescope, which has found supermassive black holes inside infant galaxies that formed just a few hundred million years after the Big Bang. These objects exceed the size permitted by the Eddington limit, which dictates the physical speed limit for gas falling into a black hole. Under the new theory, the oversized nature of these early supermassive black holes could be driven by cosmic expansion rather than gas consumption alone. The theory might also explain discoveries from gravitational-wave detectors like LIGO and Virgo, which have recorded black hole collisions involving masses inside an upper mass gap where stellar evolution dictates such black holes should not form. While the hypothesis remains a theory for now, it is testable, and observational data from the James Webb Space Telescope and other observatories may soon provide evidence to prove it.
