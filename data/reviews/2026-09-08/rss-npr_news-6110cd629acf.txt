═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A new study published by the University of Illinois Chicago reveals that immigration crackdowns beginning in early 2025 drained over $1.26 billion from local businesses in Chicago's Cook County. Researchers tracked movement using anonymous cellphone GPS data and found that regular travel between immigrant and non-immigrant neighborhoods collapsed almost immediately after President Donald Trump took office on January 20, 2025, amid rumors and sweeps of ICE raids. The decreased consumer mobility cost retail shops and restaurants in non-immigrant areas approximately $1.26 billion and cost Illinois $107 million in lost tax revenue. Co-author Professor Matt Wilson told NPR that the city has yet to recover, stating they saw a 9 percent drop in retail and a 10 percent drop in restaurant visits that persisted for about a year with systematically changed consumer behavior. Nationally, the immigration detention population has reached a record high of approximately 65,000 people following hundreds of thousands of arrests. White House spokesperson Lauren Bis defended the enforcement strategy in a statement to NPR, saying that removing criminals makes communities safer and that nearly 70 percent of ICE arrests involve individuals charged or convicted of a crime. Conversely, ICE agency statistics indicate that roughly 70 percent of currently detained individuals have no criminal convictions. The report also highlights broader economic impacts, including an estimated $700 million in total economic damage in Minneapolis, where small businesses lost over $81 million in January alone, alongside a Brookings Institution estimate of a 1.7 percentage point decline in aggregate consumer spending in high-enforcement states.
