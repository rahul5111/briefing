═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Prime Minister Narendra Modi urged India's fintech sector to expand its focus beyond the Unified Payments Interface into credit, insurance, pensions, and savings during his address on Tuesday at the seventh edition of the Global Fintech Festival. Modi called for greater international integration of India's digital payments infrastructure, noting that UPI should extend its global presence beyond the several countries where it currently operates. Pointing to India's existing connection with Singapore, Modi said the country must build similar payment system connections in various markets, prioritizing nations with large Indian populations and significant trade volumes. Linking these payment systems can reduce transaction costs and accelerate remittances for Indians working overseas. UPI can provide savings in transaction costs and bring money faster to Indians, he said. Modi also advocated for India to develop independent standards and frameworks for financial technology rather than relying on systems designed elsewhere, noting that the country now has a choice to make its own rules. Highlighting emerging technologies like agentic AI, quantum computing, and tokenization, he emphasized the challenge of turning these opportunities into actual impact through cybersecurity, ethical data protection standards, and a strong regulatory framework. He also proposed a fintech consumer protection index to enable transparent company ratings. Additionally, Modi noted that India's economy grew 7.8 percent in the June quarter and pointed to an improvement in India's sovereign rating by a Japanese credit rating agency as indicators of global confidence.
