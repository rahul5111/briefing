═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Australian social media users over the age of 16 would be given the choice to opt out of algorithms under proposed Digital Duty of Care legislation announced by the government on Tuesday. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese told reporters in Canberra that the laws will require platforms to provide user empowerment tools offering genuine and enduring choice about feed content. Under the proposal, social media platforms must notify new and existing users, allowing them to choose between a default feed featuring personalized algorithmically recommended content or a feed limited to friends and creators they follow. The legislation would also require digital services including online games, apps, and artificial intelligence chatbots to protect children from design features that cause negative behavioral impacts, such as addictive elements and effects on self-esteem, while safeguarding minors from content promoting eating disorders, hostile ideas about women, pornography, crime, and dangerous stunts. Big tech companies failing to follow the laws could face penalties as high as 109.2 million Australian dollars, equivalent to $78.6 million US dollars. This follows Australia's introduction of world-first laws in December that ban children younger than 16 from holding accounts on major social media platforms like Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok.
