═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

To mark the 25th anniversary of Donnie Darko, the film will receive a limited theatrical run beginning on October 2, featuring a new 4K restoration. Released in October 2001, the debut feature by director and screenwriter Richard Kelly struggled at the box office due to its proximity to the tragic events of 9/11, but later gained a fervent cult following on the home video market. 

On December 15, Kelly will publish his 800-page debut novel, The Philosophy of Time Travel, which picks up where the movie left off and is intended to set the stage for a film sequel. Reflecting on the production, Kelly noted that Jason Schwartzman initially attached to play the lead role, which helped circulate the script before Drew Barrymore and Nancy Juvonen of Flower Films stepped in to produce. Barrymore agreed to play English teacher Mrs. Pomeroy provided she could shoot her scenes within a one-week window in August 2000 before filming Riding in Cars with Boys. After Schwartzman faced a scheduling conflict, Jake Gyllenhaal was cast alongside his real-life sister, Maggie Gyllenhaal, to anchor the fictional family. The film also featured Seth Rogen in his first film role, cast during a commercial actors strike in the summer of 2000. 

The original script's plan to feature the horror movie C.H.U.D. fell through due to high licensing costs demanded by the studio, leading producer executive Linda McDonough to secure permission from Sam Raimi for free use of The Evil Dead poster, clips, and music instead. 

Reflecting on the unpredictable nature of filmmaking, Kelly said, “My memories of this film run the entire spectrum from fear to love,” adding that, “It’s a miracle that any movie gets made.”
