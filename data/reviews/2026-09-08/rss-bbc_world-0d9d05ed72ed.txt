═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Prime Minister Mark Carney announced that Canada's pivot away from the United States as its primary trading partner will come at a cost, even as a new wave of Canadian retaliatory tariffs came into force. In a 15-minute video address on Tuesday, Carney stated that the counter-tariffs are necessary to protect workers and that his government will support affected employees for as long as it takes. 

Canada's levies apply to nearly C$28bn, or $20bn, of American products, ranging from steel and furniture to cotton T-shirts, with tariffs reaching as high as 50%. Specific measures include 50% tariffs on American milk, golf clubs, steel, aluminium, and jackets, while cheese, toilet paper, and air conditioners face 25% tariffs. Forklift trucks and industrial moulds are hit with 15% tariffs. Canada altered its original plan by removing dozens of seafood items following pushback from the fisheries industry. 

Negotiations between the two nations collapsed in late August, and no new talks have been scheduled, though both sides have expressed a desire to strike a deal. US trade representative Jamieson Greer stated that Washington would consider imposing tit-for-tat tariffs as early as Tuesday afternoon. President Donald Trump also threatened on Monday to halt all US business with Canada-based airplane maker Bombardier unless it moved its manufacturing south. 

The trade conflict impacts a bilateral trading relationship valued at nearly $900bn last year. Prior to the trade war, an average of 75% of Canadian exports went to the US, but July trade figures show that share has dropped to 66%. Meanwhile, Canada lost 41,000 jobs in August, coinciding with the implementation of new US tariffs and the breakdown of trade talks, following a period from April to July where the economy gained 181,000 jobs and second-quarter GDP grew 3.3%.
