═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Beatriz Yankelevich, a graduate student in MIT’s Engineering Quantum Systems Group, used the AI model GPT-5.6 Sol harnessed to Codex to streamline experimental workflows on superconducting qubit chips. The MIT group studies superconducting qubits, which are cooled to near absolute zero inside dilution refrigerators and controlled using microwave signals. Testing the AI on an uncalibrated six-qubit chip, Yankelevich provided Codex with specific skills to run, evaluate, and calibrate the hardware. When signals were clear, the AI autonomously completed standard measurement sequences by identifying transition frequencies, calibrating control pulses, and determining how long qubits retained quantum information. However, the model faced difficulties when experimental signals were weak or noisy, taking longer to find suitable parameters and occasionally requiring human guidance. Characterizing a standard chip typically takes a researcher several days, but the group now regularly uses agents to handle routine measurements. Yankelevich noted that she can have agents running measurements overnight or while working in the cleanroom, checking in from her phone to steer them if needed. This automation saves significant time by removing the need for constant supervision during preliminary measurements, allowing researchers to focus on experiment design, data analysis, and higher-level work.
