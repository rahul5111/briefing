═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The far-right extremist AfD has won the state election in Saxony-Anhalt, securing a landslide victory that leaves the governing CDU barely half as strong as before and positions the party to potentially put forward the state minister-president. Commerzbank economist Ralph Solveen noted to Reuters that an AfD-led government would be seen as a turning point, though he advised waiting, pointing out that major economic decisions regarding taxes, labor structures, and social security are made at the federal level rather than by the state. Economists and industry leaders have raised concerns over the potential economic fallout, particularly regarding the state's attractiveness to foreign talent. Michael Berlemann, director of the Hamburg Institute of International Economics, stated that while much of the AfD's promised migration policy cannot be implemented at the state level or would be overturned by courts, the indirect impacts remain problematic. Berlemann warned that the announced measures mean skilled workers needed in health care, hospitality, agriculture, and construction will avoid the state, exacerbating labor shortages and widening the economic gap. ING chief economist Carsten Brzeski added that symbolic headlines regarding forced deportations would act as a deterrent for foreign skilled workers, although he noted Saxony-Anhalt is not the entire German economy. In response to the outcome, business associations and economic institutes called for federal action. DIW President Marcel Fratzscher described the result as a vote of no confidence in the federal government and urged Berlin to implement far-reaching structural reforms. Tanja Gönner, managing director of the Federation of German Industries, similarly urged that the response must not be political paralysis, stating that the federal government must consistently implement reforms to boost investment, innovation, and growth.
