Iran launched a fresh attack targeting US navy ships on Monday, following similar ballistic missile strikes over the weekend aimed at a US aircraft carrier and a guided-missile destroyer. No US warships were hit in the incidents, which have not yet been acknowledged by the US military. In response to the weekend carrier targeting, the US struck three Iranian oil tankers that Washington stated were funding Iran's military and proxy groups. The repeated attacks have raised concerns among US officials that Iran is employing more advanced weapons and potentially receiving tracking assistance from China or Russia. Iranian state media claimed the country is using new missiles with advanced technology, which weapons analysts note could include electro-optical seekers that use images to adjust trajectories toward moving targets. John Caves, a senior research associate at the Wisconsin Project on Nuclear Arms Control, stated that Iran unveiled the Qassem Bassir medium-range ballistic missile with such seeker technology last year and that other Iranian missiles, including the Khalij Fars, also use it. The escalation comes as a US blockade of Iranian ports increases economic pressure on the country. A US navy official noted that 19 warships are currently supporting operations in the Middle East, and Washington has warned it will respond to attacks on its naval vessels as the US hopes sanctions will ultimately force Iranian leadership to back down.
