═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

On Tuesday, September 8, mathematicians at OpenAI announced that 10,000 autonomous AI agents running on an unreleased advanced model found a singularity in the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations, resolving one of the six remaining Millennium Prize Problems established in 2000 by the Clay Mathematics Institute with a $1 million award. The proof was formally verified using the Lean programming language. 

The breakthrough builds upon foundational strategies developed by Diego Córdoba of the Institute for Mathematical Sciences in Madrid and Luis Martínez-Zoroa of CUNEF University, who pioneered non-computer-based analytic techniques involving infinite cascades of layers. 

The announcement followed a competing claim made 12 hours earlier by Tristan Buckmaster of New York University and Levent Alpöge of Anthropic, who stated they used various AI models to resolve related problems, resulting in a Lean-verified proof for the Euler equations by August 22. Buckmaster noted that the first language model-generated proof he received was extremely difficult to read, writing that one of their upcoming papers "can only be described as AI slop." 

OpenAI reported that nearly 100 agents worked for about 50 hours on an Euler disproof, followed by roughly 10,000 agents working for 88 hours to produce the Navier-Stokes proof, with another 17 hours required for formalization. Sébastien Bubeck of OpenAI estimated the computational cost at several million dollars. While OpenAI conceded priority for the 3D Euler result to Buckmaster and Alpöge, Buckmaster suggested the OpenAI researchers or their agents may have accessed work he and Alpöge conducted using OpenAI's systems. 

Charles Fefferman of Princeton University praised the underlying research, stating, "The heroes of the story are Córdoba and Martínez-Zoroa." Buckmaster added in his statement, "Let me make plain what I have said to colleagues in private: in view of this body of work, I believe Luis Martínez-Zoroa deserves a Fields Medal."
