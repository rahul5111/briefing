═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Scientists have identified a new giant dinosaur species in northeastern Brazil named Dasosaurus tocantinensis. Published on September 8, 2026, in the Journal of Systematic Palaeontology with support from the São Paulo Research Foundation, the study reveals the animal measured about 20 meters long and lived roughly 120 million years ago during the transition from the Lower to the Upper Cretaceous. The fossils were uncovered at a road rail terminal construction site in Davinópolis, in the state of Maranhão, at a depth of about eight meters. Initially mistaken for prehistoric mammal remains by monitoring archaeologists, the bones were recognized as much older by Elver Luiz Mayer of the Federal University of the São Francisco Valley. The preserved skeleton includes tail vertebrae, a 1.5-meter femur, ribs, foot bones, and arm and leg bones. Max Langer of the University of São Paulo noted that it is considered a relatively complete specimen, stating that because it includes these major bones, "we believe there are more fossils from this same specimen yet to be excavated at the site." Dasosaurus tocantinensis is the largest known dinosaur for Maranhão. Its closest known relative lived in Spain, suggesting its ancestors crossed ancient land connections between Europe, Africa, and South America within the supercontinent Gondwana between 140 and 120 million years ago. Microscopic bone analysis also revealed growth patterns combining traits of older sauropods and titanosaurs, which may explain how certain sauropods reached enormous sizes. Researchers are currently negotiating with the construction company to return and excavate remaining fossils at the site.
