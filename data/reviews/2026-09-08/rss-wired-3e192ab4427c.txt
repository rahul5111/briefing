The Ig Nobel Prizes, hosted by the organization Improbable Research since 1991, held its annual ceremony in Zurich, Switzerland, recognizing 10 unusual scientific studies that first make people laugh and then make them think. The prizes covered a wide range of quirky findings across various categories. The biomechanics prize went to researchers who defined a kiss as a non-agonistic interaction involving directed intraspecific oral-oral contact, with some movement of the lips or oral structures and without the transfer of food. The economics prize was awarded for a study concluding that people from higher social classes have a greater tendency to prioritize self-interest. The chemistry prize recognized an analysis of the high-protein, energy-dense nutrient crystals produced by mother cockroaches of the species Diploptera punctata. The medicine prize went posthumously to scientist Unno Tokuji for his research recommending that people blow their nose one nostril at a time with a long, forceful exhalation. The biology prize involved researchers stepping on Bothrops jararaca snakes while wearing a boot, finding a 44 percent probability of a bite when stepping on the head, 20 percent on the body, and 11 percent on the tail. The physics prize focused on mathematical models for designing splash-free urinals using lower angles of impact. The technology prize recognized the use of dead mosquito proboscises as ultrathin nozzles for 3-D printers. The olfaction prize confirmed that parents rate the smell of young children more favorably than that of teenagers. The peace prize found through questionnaires that people generally prefer a friend who is kind to them but cruel to their enemies. Finally, the soil science prize used an underwear index, where about 1,000 participants buried cotton underwear to measure soil health based on how much the fabric decomposed from microbial activity.
