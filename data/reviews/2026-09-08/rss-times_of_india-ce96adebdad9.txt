═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Russian president Vladimir Putin and US president Donald Trump held a one-hour phone call on Tuesday to discuss recent visits to Moscow and Kyiv by US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. Kremlin foreign affairs adviser Yuri Ushakov described the conversation as constructive and frank, stating that Trump stressed his desire for the war in Ukraine to end as soon as possible. Ushakov said Putin briefed Trump on the battlefield situation, outlined what the US could do to help end the conflict, and assured Trump that Russia had no aggressive intentions toward Europe. In view of the speculation about Russia’s alleged hostile intentions toward European countries, Ushakov told reporters that Russia does not have any aggressive plans regarding Europe. 

The call followed weekend meetings where Witkoff and Kushner met separately with Putin and Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Putin viewed the outcome of the Moscow visit positively, while Zelenskyy stated that the American envoys presented some very good, worthwhile ideas for ending the war. Zelenskyy said he hopes to meet Trump around September 20 during the UN General Assembly in New York, with securing additional Patriot air defense systems among his priorities. 

UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting stated at a support meeting that Ukraine needs 300 Patriot missiles from partners stocks, 500 air-to-air interceptors, and $2 billion for additional drones. Additionally, the European Commission approved a 3.2 billion euro or $3.7 billion loan for Kyiv to purchase Patriot components. Zelenskyy indicated that negotiations between Ukraine, Russia, and the US could resume in the autumn and that Kyiv is prepared to seek compromises on issues including grain exports and energy. 

Despite diplomatic efforts, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Russia remains determined to accomplish its military goals and believes victory is very close on the battlefield, adding that Moscow welcomes Trump's diplomatic efforts. Ushakov noted that Putin briefed Trump on plans for further battlefield gains along the roughly 1,250-kilometre front line. However, the Institute for the Study of War reported Sunday that the Russian rate of advance has slowed significantly since 2025, stating that Russian forces will not seize Donetsk until 2032 at their current rate of advance, if they can seize it at all.
