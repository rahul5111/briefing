═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Google announced that its personal agent, Gemini Spark, can now manage Google Photos libraries, allowing users to execute tasks such as editing images, curating albums, automatically creating shared albums, turning concert flyer photos into calendar appointments, and running workflows. Google Photos lead Shimrit Ben-Yair shared the new capabilities in a post on X on Thursday evening, noting that they will roll out over the next few weeks to eligible Gemini AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the U.S. in English. Google did not state if or when the feature will expand to international markets. To use Gemini Spark with Google Photos, users must first connect Google Photos to Gemini, toggle on Spark in the top corner of the Gemini app, and enter a prompt. The integration represents an attempt by Google to make consumer AI appealing by automating tedious tasks and managing large photo libraries, arriving as the broader AI industry faces criticism for struggling to communicate the technology's benefits to consumers.
