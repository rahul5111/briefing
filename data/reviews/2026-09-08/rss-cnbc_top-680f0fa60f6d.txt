═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Canadian airplane manufacturer Bombardier responded to demands from President Donald Trump that the company build in America by highlighting its existing U.S. footprint. On Monday, Trump wrote on Truth Social that Bombardier must build in the United States to access the local market, stating that they must stop treating America like a piggybank and declaring no more selling Bombardier in the United States. In a statement to CNBC, Bombardier noted that it has direct employment in more than 20 states and sites in 10 states, including California, Texas, Arizona, and Kansas. The company also stated its aircraft are built with American-made components such as engines and avionics, it plans to grow its defense activities and service network, and it will inaugurate a new facility in Fort Wayne, Indiana, later this year. The company said it values its great partnership with American companies and its U.S. employees. Trump's demand arrived on the eve of Canada imposing retaliatory tariffs on about $20 billion of U.S. goods, mirroring the size of Trump's recent 50 percent import taxes on Canadian wine, cement, hockey sticks, and other products following a breakdown in trade talks. Hours after the Canadian duties took effect, Trump declared on Truth Social that products originating in Canada should be ineligible for government commercial procurement programs, writing that there will be no reciprocity and no access. Trump's comments on Bombardier drew rebuttals from Republicans in states where the company operates, including Senator Jerry Moran of Kansas, who stated he reached out to Trump to ensure he was aware that Bombardier supports a local workforce of more than 1,000 employees in Wichita and makes significant contributions to the state. U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick told CNBC that Canadians blew up a nearly complete trade deal for political reasons, while Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney stated his nation would resume trade talks when the Americans are ready.
