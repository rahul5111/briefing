═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

On Tuesday, the House of Representatives passed a stopgap measure to continue funding the US government through early December, averting a government shutdown before the midterm elections. The measure is widely expected to be signed by President Trump. 

Due to dissent within the House Republican caucus preventing the passage of their own funding measure, the House adopted a version of the spending bill previously passed by the Senate in early August. 

For scientists and their supporters, the adoption of the Senate bill is significant because it includes a provision blocking the Office of Management and Budget from implementing new rules. Those proposed rules would have given political appointees full control over funded science and allowed them to cancel any grant at any time. The proposed rule had faced widespread opposition from scientific and health-focused organizations. 

Because the stopgap measure only funds the government through early December, the protection for research grants is currently put on hold until that time.
