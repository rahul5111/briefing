═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Bhopal Police Commissioner Sanjay Kumar has ordered an inquiry into the police action against protesting MBBS intern doctors following a directive from Deputy Chief Minister and Health Minister Rajendra Shukla. On September 7, 2026, hundreds of interns from the government-run Gandhi Medical College protested on their campus to demand a stipend hike from the current 14,437 rupees to 30,000 rupees. As protestors planned a march toward the Chief Minister’s residence, police deployed water cannons and, according to protestor accounts, resorted to a baton charge near the campus, stopping the march less than two kilometres from the destination. At least four interns suffered serious injuries, including a broken arm and an eye injury. In response, interns launched an overnight sit-in at Kamla Park on September 7 and 8, boycotting outpatient department and routine operation theatre services while maintaining emergency care. Services were also disrupted at Gwalior’s Gajra Raja Medical College in solidarity. Siddhartha Kumar Rai, undergraduate student wing president of the Madhya Pradesh Junior Doctors Association, stated that the peaceful protestors, who handle heavy patient loads during 36-hour duties, were treated like criminals. GMC Dean and CEO Dr. Kavita N. Singh criticized the police action in a letter to Commissioner Kumar, calling the use of force without prior notice at the hospital campus completely inappropriate and demanding action against responsible officers. Deputy Commissioner of Police Vikas Sahwal has been appointed to conduct an impartial investigation. Meanwhile, Deputy Chief Minister Shukla held discussions with senior officials and GMC management, stating on X that the government is taking the young doctors expectations seriously and aims to reach a practical solution soon.
