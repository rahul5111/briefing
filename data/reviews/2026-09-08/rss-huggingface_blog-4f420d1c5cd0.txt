═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Multiverse Computing has released a new research paper titled Safety for Whom? Boundary-Aware Self-Distillation for Controlled LLM Safety Refusal, addressing the challenge of shaping fine-grained safety boundaries in large language models rather than relying on broad topic-level refusals. Standard safety guardrails often fail in real-world deployments where a model must distinguish between harmful and benign subsets within the same topic, such as answering factual election questions while refusing requests for targeted political manipulation. Using political persuasion as a testbed, the researchers identified three major weaknesses in standard self-generation safety pipelines: a coverage gap where single-shot generation silently drops 19.88% of prompts, totaling 8,009 items; downside reactions that cause false refusals on safe prompts; and metrics that obscure boundary over-refusal. To fix the coverage gap, an escalating retry strategy reduced residual failures to 0.20%, or 79 prompts, preserving 40,293 harmful training prompts. Furthermore, incorporating 11,955 verified surface-dangerous benign prompts and held-out harmful-benign boundary pairs allowed researchers to measure both sides of the boundary. Tested on Qwen3-8B, the escalated-coverage model raised in-distribution political refusal from 9.47% to 84.75% and dropped the mean unsafe-response rate across HarmBench, StrongREJECT, and WildJailbreak from 26.26% to 0.14%. However, the baseline configuration also caused over-refusal on XSTest to surge from 2.00% to 74.00%, turning the model into a blunt refusal machine. Adding benign boundary data successfully countered this effect, reducing over-refusal on the comply-worthy side of held-out pairs from 32.94% to 4.16% while keeping genuine refusal on the harmful side high, dropping only from 91.88% to 87.72%. The findings emphasize that safety tuning cannot be assessed by harmful-refusal rates alone, as data composition must be balanced and evaluated on both sides of the intended boundary.
