═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Recent threats from Argentinian President Javier Milei regarding the Falkland Islands demonstrate an urgent need to commit to raising military spending to 3% of GDP by 2030, according to Tan Dhesi, the Labour chair of the defence committee. Dhesi said the government must not defer funding decisions to next year, pointing to both the Argentinian threats and recent geopolitical tensions. The intervention follows confusion over government policy after Prime Minister Andy Burnham appeared to reassure Dhesi during prime minister’s questions that the 2030 target would be hit at an estimated additional cost of 10 billion pounds a year, only for Downing Street to subsequently retreat from a firm commitment. Chancellor John Healey previously declined to sign up to the 2030 target, and the government instead remains aligned with a longer-term Nato target of spending 3.5% on defence by 2035. Dhesi criticized the lack of clarity, stating that decisions should not be pushed off. Milei claimed that winds of change favourable to Argentina's sovereignty claim are blowing globally, pointing to signals from Donald Trump that American support for British sovereignty over the Falklands could be called into question in retaliation for the UK's lack of support for US actions regarding Iran. Milei also warned that companies developing oil behind the back of the Argentine government would be barred from doing business in the country. UK ministers condemned Milei's comments, affirming that the Falklands will remain British as long as residents want them to.
