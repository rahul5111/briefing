═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

In a late-night blog post last Friday, OpenAI announced it is winding down its partnership with the AI coding startup Cursor, walking away from a relationship projected to bring in more than $1 billion in annualized revenue by the spring of 2026. Cursor was one of OpenAI’s top five customers in terms of revenue at the start of 2026, paying fees to offer OpenAI models to developers. The decision follows SpaceX's recent acquisition of Cursor in a $60 billion deal. OpenAI stated it cannot trust Elon Musk, citing experiences with his companies violating contracts. We are making this choice because we cannot be confident that SpaceX will use our technology within our terms of service, based on our experience with Elon Musk's companies violating contracts, OpenAI said in the blog post. OpenAI also noted that earlier this year, Musk seemingly admitted during a deposition that xAI, now part of SpaceX, used OpenAI models to train its own models. Following the announcement, Cursor founder and CEO Michael Truell posted on X that OpenAI models serve only about 5 percent of Cursor user traffic. Thibault Sottiaux, OpenAI’s head of core products, responded that token usage is not a proxy for revenue or value created and asked Truell to share the math. Meanwhile, Anthropic cofounder Tom Brown stated that Anthropic will continue making Claude models available in Cursor.
