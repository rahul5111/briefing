═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

People smuggling gangs operating on France's northern shores are shifting to larger vessels, known as mega-dinghies, as increased policing limits the supply of small boats. A recent BBC investigation found that rival criminal groups are now forced to work together, loading more migrants onto fewer vessels, following a strategy that recently saw a 15-metre boat carry a record 230 migrants toward the UK. 

French police claim they are now intercepting two-thirds of small boats before they reach the coast, reducing crossing opportunities and driving up prices. Gangs are now quoting up to €2,000, or about £1,700, per person, compared to approximately €1,300 a year ago, with payments newly required in cash to ensure commitment. The heightened police presence has also altered tactics, with gangs increasingly launching operations in broad daylight using taxi boats arriving from distances as far away as Belgium. 

Emergency teams on both sides of the Channel have raised concerns over the increased risk to life, pointing to incidents such as 4 August, when 170 people had to be rescued after their vessel caught fire in the middle of the Channel. Despite French authorities intercepting vessels and clearing migrant camps, thousands of people continue to attempt the crossing each month, prompting the UK and France to announce measures to further increase police numbers on the French coast.
