═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

New Mexico’s largest reservoir, Elephant Butte Reservoir, plunged to just 1.4 percent capacity on July 27, 2026, reaching its lowest level since 1971. According to the NASA Earth Observatory, the severe decline was driven by years of extreme drought across the Rio Grande basin and worsened in 2026 by the earliest snowmelt on record in the southern Rocky Mountains. 

The drop exposed long-submerged debris and covered boat ramps with sediment at Elephant Butte Lake State Park. State officials reported that agricultural water releases concluded after July 28, allowing the reservoir to begin slowly rising again. The shrinking water supply underscores mounting pressure on Rio Grande resources relied upon by farms in New Mexico and Texas that produce crops including alfalfa, cotton, onions, pecans, and Hatch green chiles. 

To help managers navigate the limited supplies, initiatives backed by NASA's Western Water Action Office are utilizing satellite measurements, soil moisture data, and drone observations to track water availability, monitor groundwater pumping, and support resource allocation along the valley.
