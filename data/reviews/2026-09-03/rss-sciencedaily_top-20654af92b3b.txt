═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

NASA scientists have discovered a giant 10-sided atmospheric pattern circling Saturn's south pole, providing the planet with a surprising counterpart to its famous northern hexagon. Published in Science Advances on September 3, 2026, the findings reveal that the strange decagon is a vertically extended structure embedded within one of Saturn's powerful jet streams. 

Researchers reconstructed the development of the feature using Hubble Space Telescope observations dating back to 2023, alongside ground-based images from 2024 and 2025 submitted by amateur astronomers Trevor Barry and Jean-Paul Oger to the Planetary Virtual Observatory Laboratory. Study lead author Agustín Sánchez-Lavega of the University of the Basque Country and data from NASA's Cassini spacecraft confirmed that no similar long-lived formation existed in the southern hemisphere between 2004 and 2017. 

As Saturn's seasonal cycle made its south pole visible from Earth, the wave gradually strengthened from faint initial signs into a clear decagon. Amy Simon, study co-author and principal investigator for Hubble's Outer Planet Atmospheres Legacy program at NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center, noted the rarity of the event. As she stated, the northern hexagon has been present for over 40 years, but this feature is different because it appears to be strengthening, giving researchers the opportunity to watch a giant atmospheric pattern develop.

Scientists do not yet know what triggered the decagon or whether it will persist like its northern counterpart. Continued observations using Hubble and the James Webb Space Telescope, alongside computer modeling, will be used to determine its survival and what it reveals about atmospheric dynamics on giant planets.
