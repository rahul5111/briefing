═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The Trump administration has asked the Supreme Court to lift a block on a U.S. Postal Service plan that aims to restrict mail-in voting for the midterm elections. Key parts of the plan are currently frozen by a temporary restraining order issued by a federal judge in Boston. 

The plan, stemming from a March executive order by President Trump, would require state election officials to provide lists of mail-in voters' names and addresses to the Postal Service and include specific barcodes on ballot envelopes. Under the revised rule issued by USPS in August, the agency stated it would not deliver ballots to voters in states that fail to meet these requirements. 

Voting by mail remains available to all eligible voters for now, and USPS is expected to deliver ballots as usual while the legal battle continues. However, many election experts warn it is too late to implement the plan without causing major disruptions. A whistleblower report claims the agency has not properly tested the online portal system meant to collect voter information and has established a verification policy that could prevent many voters from receiving their ballots on time. 

Trump stated he issued the directives to prevent illegal voting by non-U.S. citizens, though studies and audits indicate that issue is extremely rare. Challengers argue the plan is illegal because the postal agency lacks the authority to control mail-in voting. The Supreme Court previously ruled on a procedural question regarding one of the lawsuits in August, but the justices have not yet ruled on the legality of the underlying order or the USPS plan. North Carolina is scheduled to begin mailing absentee ballots on Friday, officially launching the voting season for the midterms, with many other states following later this month.
