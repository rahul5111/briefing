═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Developers have released NeoMME, a family of 260M and 800M multilingual multimodal foundation encoders that process text tokens and raw image patches through a single bidirectional Transformer without relying on a separate pretrained vision tower or causal language model. Trained from scratch using a masked discrete-diffusion objective on about 524 billion packed input tokens, the models feature a 16,384-token context length and dynamic image resolution.

When fine-tuned for visual document retrieval using the page-image approach as NeoMME-Retriever, the models generate both dense and late-interaction embeddings in a single forward pass, bypassing OCR preprocessing steps. On an NVIDIA L40S GPU with a 2048 by 2048 image input size, the 260M model encodes about 51 pages per second, achieving roughly twice the throughput of ColModernVBERT. Combining hierarchical token pooling and asymmetric quantization reduces late-interaction index storage from roughly 1.5 megabytes to 6 kilobytes per page while retaining more than 95 percent of baseline nDCG@10. 

On the ViDoRe v3 benchmark, NeoMME-Retriever-260M scores 0.523 for nDCG@10, representing the highest score among evaluated models strictly below 800M parameters, while the 800M model scores 0.556. Omar Khattab, who introduced late-interaction in ColBERT, explains why the term is more precise than multi-vector: It describes the granularity and learnability of the scoring function, not simply the number of stored vectors. Model checkpoints are available in Hugging Face Transformers under the Apache 2.0 license.
