═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A nonprofit organization named the Fermi Explorer Mission announced it plans to launch a spacecraft to the Alpha Centauri star system by the end of 2029. The mission is expected to cost $15 million, funded by individual private donors, and carry at least one kilogram of cargo, including scientific payloads, messages, and a copy of the Golden Record. The spacecraft will follow a trajectory discovered by an artificial intelligence system called Get Physics Done, developed by Physical Superintelligence, an AI physics research lab that launched with $58 million in funding led by Bill Gates-backed Breakthrough Energy.

Alpha Centauri is 4.4 light-years, or about 25 trillion miles, away. The Fermi team spent a year trying to engineer a $15 million solar-powered spacecraft capable of reaching the star system before turning to PSI. Running mostly on its own for three days using a billion tokens, the AI system devised a novel trajectory that combined well-known orbital maneuvers in a way the human team had not considered. The plan involves the spacecraft slowing down so its orbit swings closer to the sun than Mercury, allowing it to fire its engine during close passes where solar panels receive four times the light to deliver a high-speed burst of thrust. Because the engine runs only near the sun, the solar panels and spacecraft remain light. If successful, the journey could take up to 80,000 years.

The project addresses Enrico Fermi's 1950 puzzle about why humanity has not seen signs of intelligent alien life despite the galaxy's age and the feasibility of slow interstellar travel. “We didn’t want to do another Breakthrough Starshot,” says Philip Johnston, the cofounder and president of the Fermi Explorer Mission, referencing Yuri Milner's unlaunched 2016 initiative. “We’re dead set on something actually launching.” Johnston adds that launching a probe means humanity has the capability and desire to reach another star, pointing toward unsettling possibilities about whether life is extremely rare or if intelligent civilizations tend to self-destruct upon hitting superintelligence.
