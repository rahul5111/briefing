═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

On September 3, 2026, website owner Neil Fraser announced that his internet presence of nearly twenty-five years will be terminated following a decision by domain registry operators. Fraser registered neil.fraser.name and later beverly.fraser.name, utilizing the domains for his website, personal email address, API servers, and Internet of Things devices. 

On April 15, 2026, Verisign proposed the destruction of the entire third level of the dot-name hierarchy to simplify its administration. Despite Fraser noting the proposal relied on numerous falsehoods, ICANN approved the action on July 28, 2026. Fraser learned of the decision through an email from his registrar. 

Unlike third-party operations managed by individual resellers, the dot-name hierarchy was established exclusively as a third-level operation featuring full whois records. Although Fraser initially chose the domain because it was operated by Global Name Registry rather than Verisign, Verisign subsequently acquired Global Name Registry. 

The termination means Fraser's website and email address will vanish in February, despite being registered and paid for until 2040, and IoT devices utilizing the domain will become unusable. Furthermore, once the third-level domains are terminated, the vacant second-level domains are expected to become available for registration. This creates a security risk where a third party could register fraser.name, recreate neil.fraser.name, hijack hundreds of linked accounts, commit code using Fraser's authentication, and seize control of connected IoT devices. 

Fraser is one of 22,000 people who will lose their domains under this administrative change and is preparing legal action.
