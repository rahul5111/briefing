═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Russia announced it will close Germany’s Goethe-Institut cultural centres in Moscow, Saint Petersburg and Yekaterinburg in response to Germany shutting down a Russian cultural centre in Berlin and a consulate in Bonn. The diplomatic escalation follows an incident on August 4, when an explosive-laden drone was found near a Ukrainian cargo plane at Leipzig/Halle airport and defused. German Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt accused Russia on Tuesday of being behind the hybrid attack, while Russian President Vladimir Putin claimed Germany planted evidence to blame Moscow. Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov said on Wednesday that Germany deliberately took the step, stating, “They deliberately took this step – they could not have failed to understand – which means the end of their cultural presence in Russia.” The Goethe-Institut in Moscow originally opened in 1992. Following the airport incident, EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas described it as having the hallmarks of state-sponsored terrorism. EU foreign ministers agreed to work on a proposal to impose sanctions on 1,600 individuals and entities linked to Russia’s military industry for potential adoption next month. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said France summoned Russia's envoy in Paris, and Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Alexander Grushko stated that Moscow would retaliate very soon for the closure of the Bonn consulate.
