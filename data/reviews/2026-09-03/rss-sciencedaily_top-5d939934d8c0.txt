September 2026 brings a busy lineup for skywatchers according to NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, featuring a dazzling Venus, a Harvest Moon near Saturn and Neptune, the seasonal equinox, and views of the Milky Way. Between September 14 and 20, the Moon serves as a guide to Antares, a bright reddish star that represents the heart of the constellation Scorpius, and the Teapot star pattern in Sagittarius. From dark locations, observers may glimpse the hazy center of the Milky Way galaxy rising like steam from the Teapot's spout. On September 18, Venus reaches peak brilliance during its current evening appearance, shining as an intensely bright point of light low in the west shortly after sunset. September 19 marks International Observe the Moon Night, encouraging people worldwide to observe the Moon and learn about lunar science. On September 22, the September equinox occurs as the Sun positions directly above Earth's equator, marking the beginning of fall in the Northern Hemisphere and spring in the Southern Hemisphere. The month concludes on September 26 when the Harvest Moon rises in the eastern sky shortly after sunset near Saturn and the much fainter Neptune. Saturn is visible to the unaided eye, while Neptune requires binoculars or a telescope at around magnitude 8.
