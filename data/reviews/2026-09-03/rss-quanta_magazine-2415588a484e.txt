During a live episode of The Joy of Why recorded on July 26, 2026, at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Philadelphia, hosts Janna Levin and Steven Strogatz discussed the accelerating impact of artificial intelligence on mathematics with panelists Akshay Venkatesh of the Institute for Advanced Study, Ravi Vakil of Stanford University, and Alex Kontorovich of Rutgers University. 

The conversation focused on recent milestones, including Google’s AlphaProof achieving a silver medal at the 2024 International Mathematical Olympiads, followed by a DeepThink model securing a gold medal in 2025, and recent AI solutions targeting problems in Tom Bloom’s database of Erdős problems. Kontorovich highlighted that an AI model autonomously constructed a counterexample disproving the conjectured value of the Erdős unit distance conjecture, which subsequently inspired human mathematicians to solve the unrelated sum-product problem using techniques exposed by the AI solution. 

The panel also addressed the challenges posed by the influx of machine-generated mathematics. Venkatesh and Vakil raised concerns that the proliferation of AI-generated proofs and low-quality submissions threatens to overwhelm mathematical journals and strain community peer-review systems. Vakil noted that mathematicians will increasingly face a flood of material, stating, I have in my inbox during these two hours, I’m not looking forward to seeing what kind of AI slop people are gonna send. 

The discussion underscored a broader philosophical shift in the field, as mathematicians grapple with whether the value of mathematics lies strictly in the final destination of a solved theorem or in the human journey of discovery and learning.
