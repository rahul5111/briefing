═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

An engineer has developed a battery-free NFC-powered business card featuring a microcontroller and twenty-one LEDs that execute an animation when tapped against a smartphone. The project utilizes an ATtiny816 microcontroller housed in a three by three millimeter QFN package and an NXP NTAG I2C Plus chip capable of harvesting energy from the magnetic field emitted by a phone. 

To power the hardware without exceeding the 220 nanofarad capacitance limit specified in the chip datasheet, the designer implemented a startup circuit using a ten microfarad capacitor paired with a resistor to limit inrush current and a P-channel MOSFET acting as a switch after 120 milliseconds. The LEDs are managed using Charlieplexing across six GPIO pins, and the custom rectangular-spiral antenna was generated using a KiPython script to achieve an inductance close to 2.75 microhenries, resonating at 13.56 megahertz. 

The listener ordered thirty fully RoHS-compliant, credit card-sized units manufactured with high-precision silkscreen printing and an ENIG finish through JLCPCB. Programming is executed via three exposed pads using a Pogo Pin Clip connected to an Adafruit UPDI Friend, while contact information and routing are handled by the NXP TagWriter app linking to connect.wilsonharper.net.

Hardware is hard is a phrase I’ve heard frequently, and this project made me realize how true it is.
