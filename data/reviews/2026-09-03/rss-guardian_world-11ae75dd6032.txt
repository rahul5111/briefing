═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez told parliament on Thursday that his government has found no evidence whatsoever that Morocco planned or facilitated a mass border breach into the north African territory of Ceuta. The address followed a media-quoted police report claiming Moroccan border forces aided about 70,000 people to cross in late July. Sánchez stated that no institution, including the diplomatic service and the European Commission, has provided solid evidence of Moroccan involvement, though he conceded Moroccan forces allowed crossings during a 10-hour period on 30 July. Moroccan officials have denied enabling the crossing. Sánchez also denied receiving any warning ahead of the influx. As many as 141 people are thought to have died trying to enter Ceuta during the event, which triggered a political and humanitarian crisis. On 21 August, Spain summoned Morocco's ambassador over unacceptable statements by two ministers regarding Ceuta and Melilla, including sovereignty claims by Moroccan justice minister Abdellatif Ouahbi. Sánchez appealed for European solidarity, noting that while 90 percent of crossers returned within 24 hours, several thousand remain, including at least 500 minors. Defending the treatment of migrants, Sánchez told MPs that under law migrants have a right to individual treatment, stating you cannot just grab someone by the arm and drag them across the frontier as though they were a sack of dirt. Opposition leaders criticized the government, with People's party leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo calling on Sánchez to resign for incompetence and complicity, and Vox leader Santiago Abascal telling Sánchez he is Morocco's ambassador. Sánchez concluded by noting the economic disparity between Ceuta and Morocco, stating that even with a 100-meter wall people would still attempt to cross.
