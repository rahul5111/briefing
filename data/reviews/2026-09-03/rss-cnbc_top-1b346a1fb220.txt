═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Vice President JD Vance said Thursday that the Federal Reserve should cut interest rates to help make homes more affordable, adding to pressure from President Donald Trump on the central bank to lower borrowing costs. Speaking at a White House press briefing in response to a question from CNBC reporter Eamon Javers, Vance called lower rates the proper and responsible response to recent United States inflation data. We are doing a lot of things to try to keep those interest rates down, but it would be nice to have some help from the Federal Reserve, Vance said. The remarks follow comments made less than a week earlier in Jackson Hole, Wyoming, by Trump's handpicked Fed chair, Kevin Warsh, who hinted at potentially hiking rates to address persistently high inflation and bring it down to the central bank's two percent target. Vance's statements arrive less than two weeks before the Federal Open Market Committee is scheduled to convene on September 15 and 16 to decide on rate adjustments. Traders are currently about evenly split on the likelihood of a rate hike at the upcoming meeting, according to the CME Group FedWatch gauge. On Tuesday, Fed Governor Michael Barr stated he would support a rate increase if inflation remains elevated, while Governor Christopher Waller indicated Thursday morning that he is more inclined to keep rates steady. Vance's comments may heighten concerns regarding the independence of the Federal Reserve under the Trump administration, which has previously pushed to slash rates and sought to fire Governor Lisa Cook.
