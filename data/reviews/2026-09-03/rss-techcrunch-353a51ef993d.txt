═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Palo Alto Networks acquired Console, a two-year-old startup specializing in AI agents for IT help desks, for $500 million in cash and stock, according to sources with knowledge of the transaction. The companies officially announced the deal on Tuesday without disclosing the financial terms. Founded in 2024 by Andrei Serban following the acquisition of his previous startup Fuzzbuzz by Rippling, Console had raised $29 million total, comprising a $6.2 million seed round led by Thrive Capital and a $23 million Series A co-led by DST Global and Thrive. Prior to the sale, PitchBook valued Console at $157 million, providing a swift return for backers including SV Angel, Abstract Ventures, and Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora, who had invested as an angel. Palo Alto Networks declined to comment on the purchase price. The cybersecurity company plans to integrate Console into its Cortex platform. According to Arora, Console’s agentic capabilities will give Cortex the arms and legs to deliver autonomous security outcomes across the entire enterprise by allowing security teams to investigate and resolve alerts using natural language. Console's technology previously automated routine tasks such as password resets and app access grants for customers like Ramp, Flock Safety, and Scale AI. The acquisition marks Palo Alto Networks' seventh buyout in 2026, following its $3.35 billion valuation acquisition of Chronosphere and its $400 million purchase of Koi.
