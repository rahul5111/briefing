═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The world has entered a danger zone of extreme weather as a supersized El Niño develops, potentially becoming the strongest in more than 70 years and lasting until at least February 2027. UN head António Guterres warned that the planet is in uncharted waters as the natural weather phenomenon combines with human-induced climate change to boost global temperatures. Surface temperatures in the Pacific's key monitoring region have been well over two degrees Celsius above average, with forecasts suggesting monthly sea surface temperatures could approach four degrees Celsius above average, surpassing any event since at least 1950. Sub-surface temperatures in parts of the Pacific are more than eight degrees Celsius above average. El Niño is changing global weather patterns by bringing drought to some regions and stronger typhoons and flooding to others. WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo stated that the phenomenon has the potential to deliver a massive blow to communities and economies worldwide. Governments and scientists are taking measures to reduce risks, including strengthened early warning systems and pre-emptive medical assistance. The impacts are already visible globally. In Panama, the number of vessels passing through the Panama Canal will be reduced by more than 10% due to anticipated lower rainfall, threatening a waterway that handles about 14,000 ships per year and brings in roughly three billion dollars annually. In Indonesia, wildfires have burned more than 107,000 hectares, prompting authorities to deploy cloud-seeding aircraft and arrest dozens over land-clearing allegations. Australia is bracing for severe marine heatwaves, particularly in the south. Meanwhile, China expects seven typhoons to make landfall this year, and Peru is facing risks of flash flooding and landslides, leading the United States to deploy the vessel USNS Comfort to the Peruvian coast with medical assistance and technical equipment.
