═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

India's GDP growth for the first quarter beat estimates at 7.8%, but the figure has sparked a heated debate over calculation methodologies and downward revisions of previous quarters. Former finance secretary Subhash Chandra Garg questioned the data, arguing that if previous numbers had not been revised down, nominal growth would be about 2.6% with real growth near zero. In February, the government shifted the GDP base year from 2011-2012 to 2022-23, revising the Q1 FY26 baseline from Rs 86.05 lakh crore down to Rs 80 lakh crore. The government maintains that old and new series figures cannot be directly compared and that the revisions incorporate improved methodologies, updated data sources, and the recent Household Consumption Expenditure Survey of 2022-23. Soumya Kanti Ghosh, member of the 16th Finance Commission and group chief economic adviser at SBI, criticized critics who mix old and new series numbers, stating, "This is completely unsolicited and a sure sign of intellectual dishonesty." Ghosh calculated that comparing the new Q1 FY27 nominal GDP of Rs 88.3 lakh crore against the updated Q1 FY26 base of Rs 80.4 lakh crore yields a 9.7% growth rate, translating to a real growth of 7.4%. Economists also addressed a puzzle surrounding the 2.5% implied GDP deflator, which sits below CPI inflation of 3.9%. Experts like DK Srivastava of EY India noted that the new series uses a double deflation method for selected sectors, separating output and intermediate goods price adjustments. This methodology caused manufacturing nominal GVA to grow 7.7% while real GVA grew 9.2%, resulting in a negative implicit GVA deflator because input prices rose faster than output prices amid the Middle East conflict and companies absorbed higher costs. Analysts emphasize that quarterly GDP revisions are routine, aligning economic indicators with updated price and production indices, though final figures for Q1 FY27 will not be locked in until February 2029.
