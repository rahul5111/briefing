═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The National Basketball Association has fined the Los Angeles Clippers thirty million dollars, stripped the franchise of five first-round draft picks from 2029 to 2033, and suspended multiple top executives following a year-long independent investigation into salary cap circumvention rules involving star forward Kawhi Leonard. The probe found a pattern of misconduct by the organization, including introducing Leonard to club sponsors for off-court income and endorsement deals, offering companies team business as inducements, and paying personal expenses for Leonard and his representatives. The businesses involved included Boingo Wireless, Daktronics, Lockton Insurance, and Aspiration Partners, the latter of which allegedly signed Leonard to a twenty-eight million dollar endorsement deal and held a twenty-three-year, three-hundred-million-dollar sponsorship with the Clippers. 

Owner Steve Ballmer received a one-year suspension from all league and team activities for seeking to help Leonard obtain off-court income, approving a business deal that was a precondition for Aspiration, and failing to enforce league rules. Team president of business operations Gillian Zucker and basketball operations president Lawrence Frank were suspended without pay for one year and six months, respectively, for their involvement in the arrangements, approving impermissible expenses, and in Zucker's case, providing false statements to investigators. Leonard, who is thirty-five years old, was personally fined seven hundred thousand dollars, while his uncle and former business manager Dennis Robertson was banned from league business for five years for failing to report improper solicitations. 

The Clippers issued a statement vehemently rejecting the findings as the result of a biased investigation and stated they intend to vigorously challenge the penalties through arbitration. Leonard stated through his agent Harrison Gaines that he had no knowledge of any intent to circumvent the salary cap and accepted responsibility for lapses in judgment by his inner circle. The penalties are final and binding under an agreement between the NBA and the National Basketball Players Association. Meanwhile, the Toronto Raptors had agreed in principle on June 30 to a trade to bring Leonard back, though the league statement did not clarify whether he has been cleared to join the team.
