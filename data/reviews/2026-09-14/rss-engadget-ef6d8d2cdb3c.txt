Standard Ethernet cables max out at 328 feet, which is the maximum length a rated connection speed can travel reliably while accounting for signal loss, interference, and timing issues. A typical setup utilizes 295 feet of solid-core cable plus up to 33 feet of flexible patch cables at either end. Within that 328-foot distance, solid Cat5e or Cat6 cables support 1 Gbps Ethernet. However, cable categories dictate specific performance limits. While Cat5e and Cat6 both support 1 Gbps up to the 328-foot limit, Cat6 can support 10 Gbps only up to about 180 feet. For a longer 10 Gbps run, Cat6a is the superior alternative because it sustains 10 Gbps up to the full 328 feet. Going beyond a cable's rated distance does not necessarily produce a gradual slowdown, but longer runs can cause errors and performance inconsistencies. For distances exceeding 328 feet, solutions include placing an Ethernet switch at the midpoint to create two separate copper runs or replacing the long portion with fiber optic cable using a media converter or fiber-capable switch. For Power over Ethernet, the 328-foot rule generally applies, but lengthy runs may fail to provide enough electrical power.
