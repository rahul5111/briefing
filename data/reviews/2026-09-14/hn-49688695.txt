The AI model Fable 5.1 has successfully solved Sir Thomas Urquhart’s 370-year-old Cyphral Distich, a 64-number cryptogram from the author's 1653 work Logopandecteision. Listed among historical-cipher researcher Klaus Schmeh’s Top 50 unsolved encrypted messages, the distich was decoded by Fable 5.1 in 44 minutes using 176,000 tokens. 

Rather than utilizing an external cipher alphabet, the model deduced that the key relied on the book itself. By taking the i-th number in each cipher line as a word index for the corresponding Proquiritation, and taking the first letter of that word, the distich yielded a rhyming royalist prayer: O GOD UPHOLD KING CHARLS THE SECOND AND MAKE HIM THE SUPREME RULER OF THIS LAND. 

Following this breakthrough, Fable 5.1 also decoded Urquhart’s larger, equally unsolved Cyphral Octastick from 1652’s The Jewel, which uses numbered pages instead of paragraphs to yield an ottava rima royalist prayer, though nine letters remain unreadable due to a minor printing anomaly or slip by the author.
