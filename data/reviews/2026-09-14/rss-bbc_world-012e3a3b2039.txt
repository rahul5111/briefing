Trump emphasized the geopolitical stakes, saying the United States is leading China on AI and that he wants to keep it that way because whoever wins AI wins. The comments follow warnings from industry figures, including former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon, who quit his job and told the BBC that staff developing the systems are genuinely frightened for humanity, adding that there is a strong chance everyone could die in the immediate future if current development continues. On Saturday, xAI owner Elon Musk and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman backed a call from Anthropic head Dario Amodei to slow development to reduce risks. In Washington, lawmakers are divided on the response. House Speaker Mike Johnson cautioned against rushing regulation on CNN's State of the Union, warning it would smother American innovation and cause the US to lose the race to China. Democratic House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries argued on ABC's This Week for decisive action to slow the pace of development and protect the public. David Sacks, a tech investor on Trump's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, told AI companies on X to police themselves rather than demand regulatory frameworks.
