═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Following Y Combinator’s Thursday Demo Day, early-stage venture capitalists flagged several standout startups skewing toward deep tech with more grounded valuations than recent cohorts. Investors described the batch as feeling like science fiction. Atomarine is building nuclear-powered data centers on sea barges to solve compute shortages and cooling needs, planning a gas-powered pilot for 2028 and floating nuclear ships in 2032, backed by over $4 billion in customer interest through letters of intent. Dipole Labs developed optical networking hardware that skips the conversion from light to electricity in AI data centers to prevent GPU compute delays. Isengard is producing jet-powered strike and counter-drones in allied countries, generating $10 million in revenue with one of the batch's loftiest valuations. Lamb Labs is creating custom inference chips called Model Processing Units by hardcoding AI model weights directly into silicon to eliminate memory bottlenecks. Other buzziest startups include an unnamed human data-collection firm for robot training, Nori with a $1,600 humanoid robot for everyday tasks boasting nearly half a million in sales after launching six weeks ago, and a heavy-duty robotics startup with $25 million in contracts through 2027 that aims to build a city on Mars with an exploratory mission by 2028. Additionally, Parasma is exploring human brain cells to power compute efficiently, while Waddle Labs offers an API layer using LLM agents to write robot control code in about twenty minutes.
