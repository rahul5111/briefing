═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The Iranian military announced Monday that its new advanced aerospace defense system intercepted and destroyed an American-manufactured MQ-1 drone over the Strait of Hormuz. The drone, produced by General Atomics and historically operated by the U.S. Air Force and the CIA, was downed amid ongoing exchanges between Tehran and Washington in a war now in its seventh month. 

Speaking Sunday at the Irish Open golf championship, President Donald Trump stated that the U.S. could continue its military campaign against Iran and take control of its oil, comparing the prospect to a deal struck with Venezuela in August where Washington gained majority control of more than 65 billion barrels of oil reserves in exchange for $209 billion to the Venezuelan treasury. Trump said he expects the conflict with Iran to end this year, possibly after the November midterm elections, adding that Tehran has been calling constantly for peace talks, a claim Iran has previously dismissed.

Diplomatic efforts face further delays as Omani Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi announced on X on Sunday that a planned Monday meeting in Oman between Iran and Gulf countries to discuss agreements and sign an Iran-Oman shipping route deal through the Strait of Hormuz has been postponed due to a need for consensus. 

Energy markets reacted sharply to the intensifying conflict. Oil prices surged past $100 a barrel for the first time since May on Monday after Saudi Arabia closed a key East-West energy pipeline following damage from Iraqi drones. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures rose 2.3 percent to $102.39 per barrel, while Brent crude climbed 2.4 percent to $107.11 a barrel.
