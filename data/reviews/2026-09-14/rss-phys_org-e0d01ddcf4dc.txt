═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A new study published in Frontiers in Space Technologies finds that ambitions to build large cities and heavy industry on the moon are constrained by a severe lack of sustainable water supplies. While impacts from asteroids created cold traps in craters at the lunar poles that have preserved water ice for about 4 billion years, and moon-orbiting missions since 2013 have mapped an estimated 1 billion tons of water, this supply will not support large populations long-term. Even under a best-case scenario assuming 98 percent efficient recycling—matching current International Space Station technology—a lunar city of 1 million people would exhaust the generous estimate of 1 billion tons of water in just over a century. Furthermore, current best estimates of actual lunar water reserves are roughly 30 times lower than 1 billion tons, meaning a small city would run dry in about a decade, though settlements of 1,000 to 10,000 people could remain sustainable for centuries. While power generation is viable through 3-gigawatt photovoltaic arrays placed on crater rims bathed in permanent sunlight, sustaining large populations will require either finding significantly more water beneath the surface rubble, drastically improving recycling efficiency, reducing usage through vertical farming, or importing water from asteroids.
