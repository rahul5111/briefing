═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Researchers in Austria are currently constructing a microscope powered by a quantum computer at TU Wien, as announced on September 13, 2026, in work detailed by the Universität Wien. The project brings together teams from TU Wien, the University of Vienna, JKU Linz, and the University of Innsbruck under the quantA Cluster of Excellence.

While conventional electron microscopes achieve atomic-scale resolution by counting electrons, they require a high electron dose that can destroy delicate samples like individual proteins. To bypass this limitation, the new system links free electrons in the microscope beam to a trapped-ion quantum computer developed by Philipp Schindler's team at the University of Innsbruck. As electrons travel through the microscope, they interact with ions held along the beam path, creating quantum entanglement that allows the electron and the ion to share a joint quantum state. 

By repeatedly executing quantum algorithms developed with Johannes Kofler's team at JKU Linz, the system combines information from multiple electrons that would normally be lost to random noise. As explained by Elias Pescoller, quantum physics allows researchers to overcome the statistical limits of conventional electron microscopes by extracting a maximum-strength signal from a relatively small number of particles. 

The consortium is funded by the Austrian Science Fund through the quantA Cluster of Excellence and the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation. Having demonstrated the advantages mathematically, the team is now moving to build the working experimental device at TU Wien's University Service Center for Transmission Electron Microscopy.
