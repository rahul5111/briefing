═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The largest global survey of physicists ever conducted, published September 13, 2026, by researchers at the University of Waterloo and Perimeter Institute in collaboration with the American Physical Society's Physics Magazine, has revealed a lack of consensus on fundamental questions about the universe. No majority of respondents backed the standard cosmological model, any leading explanation for dark matter, or any single theory of quantum gravity. 

Only two specific questions crossed a majority agreement threshold. Sixty-eight percent of the physicists surveyed said the Big Bang does not necessarily represent the beginning of time, but rather describes how the universe developed from an extremely hot and dense state. Additionally, 51% agreed that the early universe experienced an extremely rapid period of expansion known as cosmic inflation.

On dark matter, responses were heavily divided. Only 17% favored the idea that dark matter consists of undiscovered low-mass particles, 12% supported modifications to the theory of gravity, and 21% favored a combination of proposed explanations. Regarding quantum gravity, string theory received the most support at 19%, while 12% favored loop quantum gravity and 18% favored the possibility that gravity cannot be quantized at all. 

Niayesh Afshordi, an associate faculty member at Perimeter Institute and professor at the University of Waterloo who led the study with coauthor Phil Harper, stated, The most striking result is how few of the standard answers in fundamental physics command overwhelming support, with most falling short of a majority. The interesting point is not that physicists are confused. It is that the frontier is genuinely alive. Researchers noted that these divisions highlight areas where better data, sharper theory, or new connections between subfields may be needed to reshape our understanding of physics.
