═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A forty-year-old unvaccinated woman in Jefferson County, Pennsylvania has died of complications from measles. According to the county coroner, the death marks the third vaccine-preventable, measles-linked fatality in the United States this year, with all three occurring in Pennsylvania during an outbreak that has caused over 670 cases and 124 hospitalizations across 37 counties. Prior to these deaths, Pennsylvania had not reported a measles death in 35 years. In a news release dated September 12, Jefferson County Coroner Greg Furlong said the death was a heartbreaking loss for the family and an unfortunate reminder that measles can be a serious and potentially life-threatening disease. Chief Deputy Coroner Jason Stiver added that this tragedy highlights that measles should not be taken lightly, stating that many people associate measles with a childhood illness, but it can lead to severe complications in individuals of any age. A preliminary death certificate obtained by the New York Times indicated the woman had several chronic medical conditions, including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and required supplemental oxygen at all times. The certificate listed the immediate cause of death as respiratory failure due to suspected measles. Furlong stated that the woman's measles case had not yet been confirmed by testing, but she was unvaccinated and known to have been exposed to someone with the virus, and she exhibited symptoms including a rash at the time of her death. The coroner's office is working with the state health department, which stated it is aware of the case but has not yet added the death to the official state outbreak tally.
