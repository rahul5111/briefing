═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Apple MacBooks can be charged using either the proprietary MagSafe connector or the industry-standard USB-C port, with each option offering distinct advantages depending on user needs. MagSafe, currently in its third generation, attaches magnetically and disconnects easily if tripped over to prevent the laptop from being pulled to the floor. It also keeps the laptop's USB-C ports free for other accessories. 

When paired with Apple's ninety-nine dollar power adapter, the MagSafe cable can charge compatible models like the 2021 MacBook Pro and 2022 MacBook Air or later at up to 140 watts, achieving a fifty percent charge in thirty minutes. In contrast, standard USB-C cables typically deliver sixty watts of power, though special cables and specific hardware can reach higher limits. For MacBook Pros released after November 2023, USB-C can support charging at up to 240 watts when using the correct cable and power adapter. 

USB-C provides the convenience of a single cable that can power a Mac, iPhone, iPad, and other USB-C devices, simplifying travel by eliminating the need for multiple cords. Because different generations of MacBook MagSafe feature varying physical sizes and power capacities, they are not cross-compatible.
