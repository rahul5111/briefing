═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Collaborative Fund, a 15-year-old New York-based generalist venture firm with roughly $1 billion under management, is taking a stake in the soccer club D.C. United and its stadium, Audi Field. The deal is subject to Major League Soccer approval.

This makes Collaborative the latest and smallest firm to follow a path opened months ago by Joshua Kushner’s Thrive Capital, which turned venture money into pro sports ownership by launching Thrive Eternal, a vehicle built to hold iconic franchises for decades. While Thrive bought stakes in the San Francisco Giants and the Los Angeles Lakers, and individual tech billionaires like Vinod Khosla have made personal-wealth sports acquisitions, Collaborative is investing directly out of its early-stage fund used for seed and Series A checks.

In a memo shared with TechCrunch, Collaborative founder and managing partner Craig Shapiro framed the investment as an extension of the firm's existing work, writing that a franchise is the ultimate consumer product. Shapiro pointed to tailwinds in American soccer, including upcoming major events and rising youth participation, alongside D.C. United's ownership of Audi Field in Washington, D.C., a talent-development pipeline through Loudoun County, Virginia, and rights to a future Baltimore expansion team.

Rather than focusing solely on the appreciation of the sports team, Collaborative intends to use Audi Field as a living showcase and distribution channel for its own portfolio companies, such as integrating Whoop wearables activations and Olipop game-day concession drinks. At the same time, the investment benefits from soaring team valuations, with D.C. United’s valuation having climbed from $35 million in 2008 to $785 million today, inclusive of its ownership of Audi Field and surrounding real estate.
