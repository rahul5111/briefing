═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Prominent AI researcher Timnit Gebru argues that the current industry focus on artificial general intelligence and existential doom scenarios is a deliberate distraction from immediate, real-world harms. Her comments follow a week marked by controversy over OpenAI solving a million-dollar math problem and the high-profile resignation of an Anthropic researcher citing safety concerns.

Gebru, known for her departure from Google in 2020 after publishing a paper on the dangers of large language models, rejects safety frameworks that focus on hypothetical extinction events. Comparing AI models to inanimate infrastructure, Gebru stated, when a bridge collapses, you do not analyze whether the bridge was ethical or sentient. You ask, who is the person who built this bridge to be so flimsy?

According to Gebru, the narrative that advanced AI could wipe out humanity obscures concrete issues already underway. She points to autonomous weapons used in warfare, climate impacts exacerbated by data centers, labor displacement, and the potential creation of chemical and biological weapons. She also criticized how corporations use achievements in controlled fields like mathematics to manufacture narratives of superintelligence, bypassing traditional peer review and pressuring policymakers into hasty legislation ahead of company IPOs. Gebru's upcoming book, Deep Unlearning: The Rise of AI and the Radicalization of a Tech Idealist, is expected to ship in early 2025.
