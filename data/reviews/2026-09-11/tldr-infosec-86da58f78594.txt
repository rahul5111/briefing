═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

OpenAI may expand its manufacturing partnerships to include Samsung Foundry alongside TSMC for its next-generation AI processors, according to a statement made by Harrison Kim, General Manager of OpenAI Korea, at a press conference in Seoul reported by Reuters. Kim stated that one of the areas of most progress with Samsung Electronics is joint production and research on next-generation chips currently under development. However, an OpenAI spokesperson subsequently clarified to Tom's Hardware on Friday, September 11, that the reference to expanding collaboration with Samsung was made in the context of their existing relationship, including Samsung's adoption of ChatGPT and a letter of intent announced last October, adding that the company has nothing new to announce and did not announce plans to manufacture chips through Samsung.

OpenAI currently relies on Broadcom for design services and TSMC for wafer processing and advanced packaging for its first-generation inference AI accelerator, known as Jalapeño, which was produced in under 18 months. Because Jalapeño is already in mass production at TSMC, any dual-sourcing arrangement would likely apply to its successor chip, which is approaching tapeout. Broadcom and Samsung previously announced a strategic partnership valued at over $200 billion through 2030 to collaborate on advanced foundry, memory, and packaging technologies for AI infrastructure. Under its agreement with Broadcom to procure 10 gigawatts of custom AI accelerators, OpenAI could potentially utilize Samsung Foundry if it ports the design, helping to secure adequate silicon supply amid massive volume requirements and fully booked TSMC capacity.
