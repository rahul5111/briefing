═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Amid war, naval blockades, and tightening United States sanctions over the past six months, ordinary Iranians are facing a collapsing economy marked by skyrocketing inflation and widespread job losses. While the official unemployment rate sits at 9%, independent analysts state the true figure is much higher, underscored by a 50% spike in resume submissions on the portal Jobvision in late April alone. Alireza Mahjoob, head of Iran's Workers House, admitted on state television in May that joblessness is far worse than anticipated, stating that we do not know how much it will afflict our already fragile working class, leaving us all helpless and powerless. 

The economic fallout has devastated professionals like Rasoul, a 42-year-old Tehran engineer laid off in mid-February after government internet restrictions damaged his tech company's delivery services. His backup plan of driving for the local ride-sharing app Snapp failed due to fuel price spikes from the U.S. and Israeli naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, rising vehicle repair costs, and dangerous nighttime airstrikes. He ultimately relied on extended family and shelved plans to start a family before securing a new sales manager role in July. Similarly, Kayvan, a 42-year-old materials engineer, lost his job when maritime trade and the Strait of Hormuz closed due to escalating fighting, forcing his household to survive on his wife's income and dwindling savings amid unprecedented price hikes.
