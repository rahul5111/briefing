═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Researchers at Penn State have developed an artificial intelligence and camera system capable of forecasting individual turkey body weight up to three weeks into the future with approximately 93% accuracy. Published in Frontiers of Animal Science, the study addresses the challenges of traditional body weight monitoring, which requires extensive manual labor and frequent animal handling. As study senior author Enrico Casella noted, traditional approaches create both economic and animal welfare concerns, and in the poultry industry, weight information is essential for maximizing the value of each individual animal because the average weight of a flock determines equipment settings for processing operations.

The study, conducted at the Penn State Poultry Education and Research Center, observed 30 male turkeys housed together from days 37 to 133 of age. An overhead camera captured normal color images and depth images to reveal the three-dimensional shape and size of the birds. The AI used a technique called instance segmentation to distinguish individual turkeys from each other and the background. Researchers trained and tested the system by manually weighing the turkeys five times per week and employing a deep learning neural network called ResNet to analyze the image data. 

While previous computer vision systems were not capable of temporal forecasting over time, this new system's prediction of future body weight was nearly as accurate as estimating current weight. Casella stated that if farmers could reliably estimate weights using cameras, they would not need to catch and weigh large numbers of birds manually, which could help with monitoring growth, planning feed strategies, predicting market weight readiness, and reducing labor. However, the technology is not yet ready for every commercial farm because the experiment involved only 30 turkeys under controlled conditions, and larger studies are needed to test the model across different farms, environments, and much larger populations.
