═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Frontline activists have welcomed the launch of the Her Horizon Fund, an unprecedented $15.5m fund aimed at creating unstoppable momentum to end female genital mutilation worldwide. The practice has affected more than 230 million women and girls globally. The fund was officially launched earlier this month at a Justice for Girls global gathering in Kenya. It has been seeded with $14m from the US-based Wallace Global Fund, supplemented by a $1.5m contribution from an anonymous donor. The initiative's broader aim is to pool $100m from donors to reach a tipping point toward ending FGM within a generation. 

The launch coincides with a sharp decline in donor funding, particularly for work led by FGM survivors in local communities, at a time when women's rights are increasingly under attack. Last year, funding for the largest initiative to end the practice, a joint programme operated in 18 countries by UNFPA and Unicef, fell by 45% from $29.6m to $16.3m. The United Nations has set a goal of eliminating FGM by 2030, but progress is not on track as legal protections are challenged and funding declines. A 2025 study estimated that FGM causes approximately 44,000 deaths each year in the 15 countries examined. 

Susan Gibbs, the Wallace Global Fund's senior adviser on women's rights and FGM, stated that female genital mutilation is a global crisis, an extreme manifestation of gender-based violence and violence against children, and a public health emergency. She added that most frontline organisations have been absolutely starved for resources, and more direct funding would allow them to sustain and scale up their work. Ifrah Ahmed, a Somali-Irish activist and survivor who set up the Ifrah Foundation, noted that her organisation's UN funding worth $300,000 was cut by UNFPA last year. She warned that if they do not continue to raise money, the dream where this practice will end will have died for young girls.
