═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A new study investigates the impact of scaling model size and pre-training compute for video-based robot policies, utilizing Direct Video-Action models evaluated on a real industrial manipulation task. Researchers tested whether scaling pre-training on general web video measurably improves real-world task performance, holding everything else fixed. The evaluation task involved industrial unpacking, specifically handling boxes of bearings weighing 10 kilograms, which requires sorting packaging waste into cardboard, plastic, and paper bins across six scored sub-tasks within a 100-second time limit. The project required over 200 hours of real-robot evaluation time, conducting one hundred to a few hundred trials per policy. The findings indicate that larger pre-trained video models consistently yield better robot policies, a trend that holds at the largest size tested. Furthermore, increasing pre-training compute improves robot policies at every amount of robot demonstration data tested, with the largest gains appearing when robot data is scarce. Additionally, better prediction of held-out web video at a pre-training checkpoint correlates with better robot policies across all tested model sizes and compute budgets.
