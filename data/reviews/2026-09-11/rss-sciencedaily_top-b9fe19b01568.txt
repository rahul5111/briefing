═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Researchers at the Technical University of Denmark announced on September 11, 2026, the creation of an ultra-small nanolaser that could allow future computer chips to transmit information using light instead of electricity, potentially cutting computer energy use in half. The breakthrough was published in the scientific journal Science Advances in a study co-authored by DTU professor Jesper Mørk alongside Drs. Meng Xiong and Yi Yu from DTU Electro, with the light-trapping structure originally developed by Professor Ole Sigmund's group at DTU Construct. The device, built in the DTU Nanolab clean room facility, pushes beyond conventional size limits by using a nanocavity to trap and concentrate photons and electrons in a microscopic region, allowing the laser to function at room temperature with minimal energy. Thousands of these lasers could eventually be integrated onto a single microchip to make data centers, smartphones, and computers faster and cooler. According to Mørk, the nanolaser opens up the possibility of creating a new generation of components that combine high performance with minimal size, stating that the technology can reduce energy consumption in information technology or aid in the development of healthcare sensors where extreme light concentration delivers high-resolution images and ultrasensitive biosensors. While much of the internet already relies on light via fiber optic cables, internal computer data still moves through electronic circuits, which produces heat and limits speed. The next major challenge is making the nanolaser operate using electrical power rather than an external light beam, a hurdle researchers estimate could be solved within the next five to ten years.
