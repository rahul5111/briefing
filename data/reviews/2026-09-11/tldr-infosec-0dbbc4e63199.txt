═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

In September 2026, NVIDIA announced it is expanding its native GPU programming capabilities by maturing CUDA Rust into 2027 and beyond, bridging the gap for GPU kernels that previously required separate languages. 

The initiative offers two distinct tracks for developers. The SIMT track uses cuda-oxide, a custom rustc codegen backend that requires Linux, a GPU with compute capability 8.0 or later, a CUDA toolkit version 12.x or newer, clang with libclang headers, and a pinned nightly toolchain. It utilizes specialized types like DisjointSlice to enforce thread safety at compile time and requires launch contracts to validate configurations against live device limits. 

The second option is the Tile track, powered by cutile-rs, which operates at a higher level of abstraction by performing computations on tiles rather than scalars. Its requirements are lighter, needing a GPU with compute capability 8.0 or later, CUDA 13.3, stable Rust 1.89 or newer, and Linux, while utilizing CUDA Tile IR to JIT-compile kernels without requiring a custom nightly toolchain or local LLVM build. 

Both approaches prevent race conditions and memory aliasing errors at compile time by ensuring that tensor inputs are shared while outputs are exclusively owned by a single writer, addressing the unpredictable concurrency bugs that frequently pass initial testing before failing in production environments.
