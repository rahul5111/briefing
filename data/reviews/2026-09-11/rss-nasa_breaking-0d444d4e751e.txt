═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

NASA has launched a new citizen science project called Artifact InSPECtor, which invites volunteers of all ages to help train artificial intelligence tools to remove errors from space telescope data. The project relies on participants using smartphones, tablets, or computers to examine real data and learn how to recognize artifacts caused by cosmic rays, light glinting off telescope housing, or electronic quirks. The cleaned data will support missions like the European Space Agency Euclid space telescope and NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, which begins science operations in early 2027. Both observatories capture light from millions of distant galaxies using spectrographs to help scientists study the expansion of the universe and dark energy. Participants help improve the instructions guiding AI models that struggle to accurately distinguish invalid pixels from real astronomical objects. As nine-year-old participant Maeve F. noted after trying the platform, it is really cool that people can help teach computers new skills.
