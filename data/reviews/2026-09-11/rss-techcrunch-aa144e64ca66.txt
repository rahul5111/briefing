═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Khosla Ventures is opening its first-ever office outside of Menlo Park, California, establishing a new outpost on 14th Street in New York City that is expected to open this fall. Partner Keith Rabois confirmed the move Thursday night at TechCrunch’s StrictlyVC event in the West Village, noting that the firm does not even maintain an office in San Francisco. 

The new New York office will house a handful of Khosla investors, including Rabois, who recently relocated to the East Coast. It will also feature an executive briefing center designed to host groups of 10 to 12 portfolio companies four days a week to meet with Fortune 500 companies. Describing the initiative, Rabois said, “The portfolio companies love this. They get pilots and customers, and so it’s going to be a very vibrant office because of that.”

While Rabois expressed confidence in New York’s pipeline of junior individual contributor talent straight out of school, citing portfolio company Ramp as an example, he noted that recruiting senior technical talent and experienced executives presents structural challenges. Specifically, he pointed out that proven senior executives often live in commuter suburbs outside the city, making a mandatory five-day in-office culture difficult to sustain due to commute burdens and family housing costs. 

The expansion mirrors a broader shift highlighted in a CBRE report released last month, which found that New York has narrowly surpassed the San Francisco Bay Area in total tech talent headcount for the first time in the 13 years the real estate firm has tracked the data. Khosla’s new office places it alongside other major Bay Area venture firms like Sequoia Capital and Andreessen Horowitz that maintain a localized presence in New York.
