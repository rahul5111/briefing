═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Houthi forces captured the entirety of Yemen’s Red Sea coastline on Friday, reaching the Bab al-Mandeb shipping gateway and seizing the port city of Mocha on Thursday from the Saudi-backed Yemeni government. The offensive threatens Saudi oil exports, which increasingly rely on the East-West Crude Oil Pipeline to transport up to seven million barrels per day to the Red Sea following blockades of the Strait of Hormuz. Roughly 12 percent of global trade passes daily through the Bab al-Mandeb strait, where the Houthis previously declared a maritime blockade on Saudi-flagged ships in July. 

Proxy control of Bab al-Mandeb strengthens Iran’s position in its ongoing war with the US and Israel, which began in February. Alam Saleh, a professor of international relations at the Australian National University, stated that the development means Iran now controls the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab al-Mandeb corridor, giving Tehran leverage over global oil prices and energy security. 

Yemeni government forces number an estimated 300,000 compared with 250,000 Houthi fighters, but internal military fractures and reliance on Saudi air power have left the coalition fragmented. While some analysts suggest the Houthis are unlikely to sustain a long-term blockade due to poor governance capabilities, others note that the threat alone is enough to deter shipping companies. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman reportedly requested American airstrikes against the Houthis during two phone calls with US President Donald Trump on Thursday, but Trump declined.
