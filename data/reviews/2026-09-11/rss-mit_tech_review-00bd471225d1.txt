═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

MIT Technology Review has released its annual list of 35 Innovators Under 35, highlighting nine individuals transforming biotechnology. Five of these young scientists are leading distinct technological developments across medicine and research. In Tanzania, 28-year-old Paschal Kija developed the Mkanda Salama, a $70 device designed to treat postpartum hemorrhage, which contributes to approximately 29 percent of maternal deaths in the country. A study demonstrated that the device stopped postpartum bleeding in 73 percent of women within 20 minutes. Xiao Yang, 34, is creating ultra-small, flexible brain electrodes inspired by kirigami, the traditional Japanese art of paper cutting, featuring a honeycombed spiral basket structure intended to minimize tissue damage while studying brain cells. Sarah Grandinette, 26, was part of a team that developed a personalized gene-editing therapy for Kyle Muldoon Jr., a baby born in 2024 with a rare and potentially fatal genetic disorder. After screening approaches using cells with KJ’s specific genetic variant and testing in mice and monkeys, KJ received his first dose at about seven months old, responded well, and was discharged from the hospital. Grandinette notes that he is doing pretty great. Yuancheng Ryan Lu, 34, contributed to a 2020 study demonstrating that cell reprogramming therapy reversed vision loss in aged, blind mice. An almost identical version of that therapy is now being tested in humans by Life Biosciences, which dosed its first volunteer in June. Finally, 27-year-old Samuel King used a generative AI model last year to design new genetic blueprints for bacteriophages, which were then printed as DNA strands to test against bacteria, with the ultimate goal of creating AI-designed organisms to manufacture drugs or absorb pollution.
