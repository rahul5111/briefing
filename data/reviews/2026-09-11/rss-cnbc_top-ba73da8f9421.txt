Following a whirlwind 24-hour trip to Caracas alongside U.S. Secretary of Energy Chris Wright, the United States has signed a series of new oil agreements with Venezuela. The deals involve private operators including Italian energy giant ENI, Aspect Energy, and North American Blue Energy Partners, or NABEP, a private operating company run by investor Alejandro Betancourt in which the U.S. government is taking a stake in exchange for future oil production. 

Separately, Chevron is expanding its presence through a $7 billion investment spanning three different projects, building on more than a century of operations in the country. Venezuelan oil production peaked at about 3.5 million barrels per day in 1997 before collapsing under Hugo Chavez and Nicolas Maduro to under 1 million barrels per day for much of 2025, though it has recently climbed back above that threshold. 

U.S. Secretary of Energy Chris Wright expressed confidence during the visit that production can quickly scale up by a couple hundred thousand barrels per day. Supported by Chevron's expanded investments, officials harbor cautious optimism that Venezuela could return to producing 2 million barrels per day relatively soon. The framework is designed to ensure some proceeds and tax revenues flow back to the Venezuelan people rather than being siphoned off overseas.
