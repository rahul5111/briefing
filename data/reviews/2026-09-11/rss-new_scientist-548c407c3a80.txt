═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Satellite data indicates that dozens of temporary gas turbines in Mississippi powering SpaceXAI’s Colossus 2 data centre in Tennessee have caused a major spike in nitrogen oxide air pollution. The data centre, which came online in 2025 and is the largest operational data centre in the US, has been the subject of controversy and ongoing legal cases over its use of temporary gas turbines that lack catalytic converters to control emissions. 

Researchers led by Kevin Gauld at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology used observations from NASA’s TEMPO satellite to assess the impact on local air quality. The study tracked an increase in turbines from 27 in December 2025 to 69 by August 2026. Satellite analysis showed emissions averaged about 730 kilograms per hour between February and August 2026, peaking at 1180 kilograms per hour in August 2026. By comparison, monthly emissions from a nearby gas power plant in Mississippi averaged 21 kilograms per hour. Peter Zoogman at Minerva University noted that the calculated emissions mean each turbine produces roughly the same level of annual emissions as 50,000 cars. 

In July 2026, SpaceXAI promised to remove all 69 temporary turbines by July 2027, replacing them with 41 permanent gas turbines permitted by the Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality in March 2026. The company also stated that mobile turbines it manufactures in-house will feature pollution-control technology. SpaceXAI disputed the satellite findings using a court submission from a third-party air-quality expert, which claimed local monitoring stations have not observed notable pollution increases and that site emissions meet regulatory requirements.
