The National Institutes of Health has reached an agreement to use a portion of its budget to fund research at the Department of Defense. The Department of Defense released a copy of the agreement on Friday, September 4, 2026, confirming it was signed about a month prior. The deal is set to run for a decade and establishes a system for the NIH to transfer money to the Department of Defense to fund staff and projects meant to support the advanced development of medical countermeasures against pandemic influenza, chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear threats, and emerging infectious diseases. The funds will be drawn from the budget of the NIH's National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, which received an allocation of $6.6 billion from Congress in 2026. While the exact amount transferred remains unspecified, reporting by Nature indicates the Department of Defense initially sought up to a third of its total budget before being told to settle for roughly 10 percent. The agreement comes as the 2026 Defense budget, sitting at approximately $1 trillion, faces strain from the conflict with Iran, and as the NIH struggles to issue sufficient grants to spend its Congressional allocation.
