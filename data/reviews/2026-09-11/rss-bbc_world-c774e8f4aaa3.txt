═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Three Hong Kong activists who organised annual vigils commemorating the 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown have been sentenced to up to seven years in prison following convictions under the city's national security law. Lee Cheuk-yan, 69, and Chow Hang-tung, 41, were convicted last month of inciting others to subvert state power, while a third defendant, 74-year-old Albert Ho, pleaded guilty in January. On Friday, the court sentenced Lee to seven years in prison and Chow to seven years and three months. Ho was sentenced to five years in prison. The three were leaders of the now-disbanded Hong Kong Alliance, which was founded in May 1989 and organized yearly vigils before authorities banned the gatherings in 2020. A key part of the trial focused on the group's slogan of ending one-party dictatorship, which the court ruled incited people to undermine the Chinese Communist Party. On Friday, the court also imposed a HK$1.5 million fine, equivalent to $191,000 or £142,000, on the Hong Kong Alliance. Lee's wife, Elizabeth Tang, told Reuters that her husband intends to appeal his sentence.
