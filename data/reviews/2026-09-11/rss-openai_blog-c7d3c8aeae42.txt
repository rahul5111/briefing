═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

OpenAI members of technical staff Jon Lee, Chaomin Yu, and Ben Ries detailed how the organization adapted its online storage platform, Habitat, to manage rapid growth supporting ChatGPT products. Launched in mid-2024 as a Python client-side library connected to Azure Cosmos DB, Habitat has evolved into a distributed system handling more than 70 million requests every second across almost 40 geographic regions for over 1 billion weekly users, serving more than 500 petabytes of data. 

The platform grew more than 10 times year-over-year for three consecutive years. By mid-2025, the original client-side library approach reached its operational limits, prompting OpenAI to decouple the storage logic into a standalone service to establish a single point of control for deployments, observability, and data security. 

Despite the known performance overhead and network latency penalties of using Python for a high-throughput service, the team consciously accepted this technical debt to unblock product developers and achieve platform stability, betting that the advancement of internal coding models like Codex and GPT would eventually simplify a future migration. Running Habitat as a Python service required active management of tail latencies caused by the global interpreter lock and asyncio event loop scheduling delays. Engineers discovered that periodic JSON parsing of feature flag configurations via Statsig and uneven load balancing from default connection pooling behaviors contributed to high tail latencies and metastable failures. Mitigations included deploying smaller targeted configurations, adding jitter to background tasks, capping maximum connection reuse durations, and scaling out Python worker processes while keeping concurrent requests per process low.
