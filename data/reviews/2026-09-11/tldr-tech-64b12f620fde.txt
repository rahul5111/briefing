═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Beijing-based Caijing Magazine published a feature detailing leadership and operations at Unitree Robotics under founder, chairman, general manager, and chief technology officer Wang Xingxing. Following its August 19, 2026, listing on the STAR Market as China’s first humanoid robot stock, Unitree’s market capitalization briefly hit 440 billion yuan, making Wang the richest person in China born in the 1990s. As of August 28, its market capitalization stood at roughly 248.8 billion yuan.

Employees report that Wang maintains strict oversight, requiring his personal approval for any expense reimbursement over 100 yuan and offering rewards between 10,000 and 500,000 yuan for reporting leaked internal information. Unitree spent 145 million yuan on R&D in 2025, accounting for 8.53% of its revenue, compared to 31.39% in 2023 and 17.83% in 2024. The company has sustained multi-year profitability by driving down hardware costs through design choices rather than relying solely on production scale. According to its prospectus, 2025 sales reached 23,000 quadruped robots, up 222% year-on-year from 7,136 units in 2024, while bipedal humanoid sales reached 5,215 units, up 1,165% from 412 units in 2024. Gross margins on bipedal humanoids reached 63.18% in 2025, and quadruped gross margins rose to 56.72%.

Public visibility has been driven by three appearances on the Spring Festival Gala, including a 2025 performance featuring yangge folk dances in floral-print clothes that cost the company nothing and triggered a surge in orders and overtime. For the 2026 Gala, Unitree stationed nearly 50 engineers on site for over two months of tuning and rehearsals. Describing Unitree’s philosophy on pricing and engineering, Wang has publicly stated, Don’t try to beat us on cost — we can still go a lot lower.
