═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

OpenAI announced this week that its AI agents have solved a major two-hundred-year-old mathematical mystery concerning the Navier-Stokes equations, which describe fluid movement. The equations form one of the Millennium Prize Problems, carrying a one-million-dollar reward. Researchers learned of unpublished progress by a pair of mathematicians, prompting OpenAI to deploy thousands of AI agents on the problem. In just 88 hours, the agents determined that the equations do sometimes blow up, meaning a part of a fluid moves infinitely fast, proving the equations are imperfect rather than indicating real fluids will misbehave. 

While the breakthrough is among the biggest in decades, AI proofs differ from human ones. Artificial intelligence tends to produce convoluted arguments that are difficult to follow, lacking the insight traditionally prized by mathematicians. However, the logic can be verified through formalisation, a process turning proofs into code checked rigorously by computers. OpenAI has stated it has also made substantial progress on another Millennium Prize Problem. 

This development marks a new era where mathematics may yield more answers than explanations. While the transition raises challenges regarding access, usage, and the role of tech companies, the tool allows mathematicians to make progress faster than before.
