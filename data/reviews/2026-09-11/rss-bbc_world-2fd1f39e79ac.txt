═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement has claimed a major advance on the western Red Sea coast, seizing Perim Island in the Bab al-Mandab Strait and capturing the port city of Mokha from Saudi-backed pro-government forces. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Sarea stated that a large-scale military operation was launched on September 3, displacing Saudi enemy forces from six districts in Taiz and Hodeidah. The Houthis stated that maritime navigation remains safe for all companies except Saudi vessels. The escalation follows Thursday morning attacks on Saudi Arabia's east-west oil pipeline in the Riyadh and Medina regions, which the Saudi energy ministry said resulted in a number of injuries and prompted a precautionary closure of the pipeline. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman has personally pressed US President Donald Trump to take military action against the Houthis, though two sources familiar with the matter told CBS News that Trump has declined direct military involvement, offering intelligence and targeting support instead. A senior Trump administration official said the US is in continuous dialogue with Saudi Arabia and the Republic of Yemen government regarding regional stability. The International Organization for Migration reported that at least 46,000 people have been displaced in Yemen since fighting escalated, with IOM director general Amy Pope stating that families are being forced to flee for the second or third time with almost nothing left. Perim Island sits at the narrowest point of the Bab al-Mandab Strait, a crucial waterway used by Saudi Arabia to transport oil to Asia. The conflict escalation has contributed to a surge in global oil prices.
