═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Monitoring platform Checkly rewrote its high-throughput Results Daemon from Node.js to Go using Claude Code, shipping the service with zero incidents, a 70% reduction in running pods, and a lighter database load. The component processes approximately 92 million messages a day, roughly 40 million of which are check results and the rest WebSocket publishes. Platform growth had doubled check volumes over the previous year, turning the legacy JavaScript worker into a bottleneck.

Before writing code, the team built a black-box test harness using Playwright, Docker Compose, and Toxiproxy. Test cases were seeded by extracting 24 hours of account, group, and check configurations along with result outcomes into ClickHouse. An initial attempt using Opus was discarded, but an overnight run with Claude Code produced a deployable service of about 13,000 lines of application code staying within the daily limits of a $200 subscription. 

Production deployment revealed a critical gap in the harness: local queue topology assumed 3 queues per region, whereas production routing involved 18 possible queues. After updating the harness to match production and refactoring the retry module, Checkly migrated accounts in staged cohorts, moving from free accounts to paid monthly subscriptions and finally to enterprise accounts by flipping feature flags.
