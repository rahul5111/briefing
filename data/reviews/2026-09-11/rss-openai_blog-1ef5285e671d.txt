OpenAI and the U.S. General Services Administration have announced a multi-year agreement that expands artificial intelligence access and cyber defense tools to federal, state, local, and tribal governments. The 27-month agreement runs from October 1, 2026, through December 31, 2028, extending eligibility across a U.S. public-sector workforce of approximately 23 million people, building on existing agreements that already cover more than one million government employees. 

The deal eliminates the standard 15-dollar-per-user-per-month license fee, setting it to zero with no minimum commitment, while providing 50 percent off usage costs for eligible organizations. In addition, verified government entities will be approved for advanced cyber-defender Daybreak Blue access at 50 percent off standard commercial pricing, alongside scaled training and enablement. Defenders can also request access to Daybreak Red for advanced vulnerability research, exploit validation, and red teaming at standard commercial pricing. 

Practical implementations under previous access include the CDC accelerating public-health literature reviews to under 30 minutes, Georgia’s Department of Revenue reducing tax form digitization time from two weeks to 15 minutes, North Carolina's Department of State Treasurer identifying millions of dollars in potential unclaimed property, and Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory compressing months of fusion-research model development into hours. OpenAI President Greg Brockman stated that public servants should have access to the best AI tools available, noting that helping government work better by making services faster, easier, safer, and more reliable is a key way to bring the benefits of AI to everyone.
