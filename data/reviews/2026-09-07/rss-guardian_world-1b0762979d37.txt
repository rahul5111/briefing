═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

The United Nations General Assembly has voted to adopt a resolution officially phasing out the 16th-century Mercator map projection in favor of the 2018 Equal Earth design, which more accurately displays Africa's true scale. During the session at UN headquarters in New York, 164 countries voted in favor, six abstained, and only the United States voted no, with its representative calling the resolution superfluous. 

Togo spearheaded the campaign for the resolution on behalf of the African Union. Because UN resolutions are non-enforceable, countries and institutions are not mandated to use the new projection, but the outcome is expected to update classroom curriculums and everyday technology. 

Introduced in 1569 by Flemish cartographer Gerardus Mercator to help sailors navigate, the traditional map makes regions near the equator appear smaller while enlarging high-latitude areas, making Africa look roughly the same size as Greenland even though the continent is 14 times larger. Togo’s foreign affairs minister, Robert Dussey, said before the vote that the change is a scientific necessity, stating, "This discussion is not a political discussion, a scientific discussion, because there is scientific proof so we all have to accept the reality." 

France’s foreign minister, Jean-Noël Barrot, also spoke in support of the resolution prior to the vote, announcing that France will abandon the Mercator projection in favor of representations like the Eckert IV projection. Togo plans to host an event in Lomé with Unesco, the African Union, and tech brands including Google Maps during the first quarter of next year to outline the implementation of the resolution.
