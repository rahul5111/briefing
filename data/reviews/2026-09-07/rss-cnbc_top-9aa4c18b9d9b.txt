═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Oil prices rose to a six-week high on Monday following a weekend of military strikes between the United States and Iran, alongside reports of fresh attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure. Brent crude oil futures climbed 1.5% to $97.73 per barrel, reaching as high as $97.93, which marks the highest level since July 23. West Texas Intermediate, the U.S. oil benchmark, increased 1.8% to $93.10 a barrel, also hitting its highest mark since late July. The rising crude prices have driven up the costs of other fuels, pushing gasoline and diesel to record highs for a Labor Day weekend.

The escalation followed U.S. military strikes on Saturday targeting three Iranian oil tankers after Iran launched ballistic missiles at two U.S. Navy warships. U.S. Central Command stated the targeted ships belong to a shadow network funding the Iranian Revolutionary Guard and regional proxies. The Iranian Foreign Ministry denounced the vessel attacks as a war crime and an act of economic warfare. Tensions intensified further online when Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth posted that the U.S. will destroy and sink Iranian oil tankers if Iran fires on U.S. vessels. Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf responded on X on Monday, writing, "Strike our assets and you get struck."

Additionally, the Financial Times reported that Saudi Aramco oil facilities were hit in fresh attacks on Monday. Damage was still being assessed at the facility in Jizan, which houses a 400,000-barrel-per-day oil refinery, and responsibility for the attack was not immediately clear. The latest fighting follows about a month of relative calm in a conflict that surpassed the six-month mark in August.
