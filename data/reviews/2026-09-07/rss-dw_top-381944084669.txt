═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Eighty-four-year-old German filmmaker Werner Herzog has returned with Bucking Fastard, his first drama in seven years, which premiered at the Venice Film Festival to a warm audience reception and a divided critical response. The feature is loosely inspired by Freda and Greta Chaplin, real-life twin sisters who became a 1980s British tabloid sensation, and derives its title from an inadvertent simultaneous spoonerism the sisters made in court while describing a former lover. In Herzog's version, real-life siblings Kate and Rooney Mara star as Jean and Joan Holbrooke, sisters who share dreams, move in unison, and love an undeserving man played by Orlando Bloom. After being rejected by him, the sisters move to the wilds of Ireland to dig a tunnel in a cave, convinced they can reach the Orkney Islands where true love is possible. The film follows decades of provocative filmmaking characterized by extreme physical conditions, including his 1982 epic Fitzcarraldo, which involved hauling a 320-ton steamship over a Peruvian hill. Despite divided reviews calling his new feature confusing and nonsensical, Herzog maintains a large Gen Z following, driven by streaming availability and an Instagram account managed by his younger son. Herzog stated he is already deep in post-production on another new film, working on a Taschen photo book, and pursuing an ongoing ambition to film in space, noting that Elon Musk promised a spot on a Mars mission after NASA and a Japanese billionaire declined to take him. Reflecting on his career and characters striving to do the impossible, Herzog told DW, "Every grown-up man should move a ship over a mountain. Every grown-up woman should dig a tunnel through a mountain range. The message is very simple: Just go out and do it."
