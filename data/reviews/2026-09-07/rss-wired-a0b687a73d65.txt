═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Researchers from the University of Queensland and the University of New South Wales have developed cyborg insects called Paraborgs using giant burrowing cockroaches to search for disaster victims and administer first aid. As detailed in the journal Advanced Science, the team utilized northern Queensland cockroaches measuring up to 87 millimeters in length and weighing up to 40 grams. Because these insects can carry up to 1.5 times their body weight, the team built two types of Paraborgs: one with a camera and another featuring an automatic injection mechanism. The injection device uses a chemical reaction between citric acid and baking soda to create carbon dioxide, which pushes a syringe plunger. During testing, close-range injections within 150 millimeters of a target achieved an approximately 95 percent success rate, while the full sequence of tasks from departure to injection completion succeeded 72 percent of the time. Scientists remotely controlled the insects' direction and speed by applying electrical stimuli between 10 and 40 hertz to electrodes embedded in their antennae and cerci. As bio-robotics researcher Tan Vo Doan stated, cyborg insects have been designed for search and explore missions for decades, but the team wanted to take the next step. Vo Doan hopes that with secured resources, a rescue team of cyborg insects could be deployed at actual disaster sites within five to 10 years, though the current study did not replicate complex environmental conditions like uneven terrain and shifting landscapes.
