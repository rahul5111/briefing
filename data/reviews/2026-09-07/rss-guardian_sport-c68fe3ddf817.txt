The noise levels at the Billie Jean King National Tennis Center have become a prominent issue during this year's US Open. Spectators in the upper tiers of Arthur Ashe Stadium experience a persistent exterior hum from massive ventilation and cooling units, the Long Island Rail Road, forklifts servicing refrigerator trucks, and planes descending toward LaGuardia Airport. In the lower seating areas, the babble of spectators is incessant, featuring full-volume conversations on non-tennis topics like work, family, romance, and dinner that continue uninterrupted for the duration of entire matches. While the US Open has historically embraced brashness and noise as its signature distinguishing it from the Australian Open, Wimbledon, and the French Open, complaints about the volume of the New York crowd have persisted for decades, but the noise this year feels distinctly problematic.
