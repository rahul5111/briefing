═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A 135-minute video published by Gamers Nexus reveals that retail LG OLED television models, including the G5, continuously sweep home networks, map secondary devices, and log microphone audio even while appearing to be powered down in standby mode. Steve of Gamers Nexus conducted the testing alongside Level1Techs and independent security researchers. Network packet captures using Wireshark showed the televisions actively scanning local area networks for unrelated hardware such as phones and smartwatches, while also gathering internal IP addresses, neighboring Wi-Fi network names, signal strengths, and location data. This collected data is fed into LG Ad Solutions, the company targeted advertising arm. While LG claims roughly 216 million smart TV sales globally, the ad division states it has access to 363 million secondary addressable devices in the US alone by tracking other hardware on the same network. The sets also run Automated Content Recognition, sampling on-screen audio and video into digital fingerprints to log what users watch across inputs. During bench tests, researchers found the TV could capture clean microphone audio while the screen looked powered down, and when disconnected from Ethernet, the set continued saving voice input locally and uploaded the stored files once network access was restored. Additionally, the researchers documented remote code execution vulnerabilities in webOS that are moving through the responsible disclosure process. Because the televisions use broad network listeners and data sweeps out of the box, the team recommended disconnecting LG sets from the internet and using external streaming devices instead. LG has not commented on the findings.
