═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Recent astronomical observations are reviving the theory that dark energy and dark matter are physically intertwined rather than separate entities. In 2024, the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument research team found evidence that the strength of dark energy had lost its constancy, and a 2025 study based on more than twice as much data similarly concluded that dark energy was changing over time. The results indicate that dark energy reached a maximum value about 2 billion years ago and may have since begun to weaken, while potentially growing stronger in an earlier era. 

This apparent phantom behavior has prompted researchers to model interactions between dark energy and dark matter. A January 2025 study published in Physical Review D suggested dark matter may have transferred a portion of its energy to dark energy during a prior cosmic era, which could help resolve the Hubble tension, the 9 percent discrepancy between early and recent universe expansion rates. Furthermore, string theory models suggest both phenomena may share a link to a dark dimension. In a July 2025 paper, researchers including Harvard physicist Cumrun Vafa and University of Chicago physicist Georges Obied found that a scenario where gravitons leak into an enlarged dark dimension of roughly a micron in scale is consistent with DESI data. The model predicts that dark energy strength and dark matter mass will decrease over time at a rate too small to have been detected until now.
