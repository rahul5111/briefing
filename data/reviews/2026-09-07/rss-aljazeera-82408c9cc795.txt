═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Oil prices have surged to nearly a six-week high following an escalation of strikes between the United States and Iran in the Strait of Hormuz. On Monday, Brent oil futures rose to hover around $97 a barrel, marking a 9 percent increase over five days and approaching the highest point since July 24, when prices reached $97.93. US West Texas Intermediate crude rose to $92.27 a barrel. The price increases follow US strikes on three Iranian oil tankers on Saturday and corresponding Iranian strikes on three tankers and three US-linked vessels, alongside a strike on Saudi Aramco facilities in Jizan. Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has slowed to an average of 10 commodity ships per day over the past 10 days. The conflict has impacted US consumers, with the national average price for a gallon of petrol reaching $4.15 on Monday, up from $2.98 on February 28. US diesel prices topped $5.90 per gallon after hitting an all-time high of $5.85 last week. American households have spent an average of $764.59 on fuel since the war began, representing $418.82 more than usual, according to Brown University data. In Asia, China has insulated itself from the disruption by tapping its strategic petroleum reserve, relying on domestic resources, shifting toward alternative energy sources and electric vehicles, and sourcing oil from Russia. As Rachel Ziemba of the Center for a New American Security stated, this is a reflection of continued conflict and exchange of fire, noting that global supply deficits are persisting with little end to the shortages.
