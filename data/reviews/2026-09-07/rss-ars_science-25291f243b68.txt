Researchers at the Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology have developed a new algorithm named Spi-Fly that mimics the olfactory processing of fruit flies, which possess roughly 140,000 neurons in a brain smaller than a poppy seed. The work, led by Kevin Max and Yang Shen, is described in a paper published in the journal Neuromorphic Computing and Engineering. Current electronic noses on the market are expensive, narrow in what they can detect, and quickly forget older odors when learning new ones. In contrast, the new algorithm aims to replicate the ability of Drosophila to sort through a wide range of smells quickly and retain those memories over time.
