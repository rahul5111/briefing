═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Researchers at the John Paul II Catholic University of Lublin and the University of Eastern Finland have developed a new artificial intelligence governance model inspired by the teachings of Pope Leo XIV as outlined in his encyclical Magnifica Humanitas. The framework was created by Professor Stanisław Fel, Dr. Jarosław Kozak, and senior researcher Marta Choroszewicz, and is detailed in an article published in the journal AI and Ethics. Unlike existing models that focus primarily on whether governments or private companies should control the technology, the new hybrid model asks which principles should govern the distribution of power and responsibility to prevent a diffusion of accountability. The framework combines the participation of governments, tech companies, experts, civil society organizations, citizens, and international institutions under eight core principles: human dignity, the common good, the universal destination of goods, social justice, solidarity, subsidiarity, shared responsibility, and transparency and accountability. To demonstrate practical application, the authors use the example of AI systems used to assess eligibility for social benefits. Under the model, an algorithm may support an application review, but a specific human must remain responsible for the final decision. Applicants would receive an understandable explanation, retain the right to challenge the decision, and the system would face independent audits and oversight from representatives of affected groups. The study is among the first academic publications worldwide to ground an AI governance model in the encyclical Magnifica Humanitas.
