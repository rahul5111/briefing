In a comparative test published on September 4, 2026, OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra was given control of YAM arms under the Inspect Robots agent policy to evaluate its performance on robotic manipulation tasks against earlier models. Tested on two specific tasks, Astra completed the first task of picking up a red block and placing it inside a bowl in 19 of 20 trials, compared to 8 of 20 for Fable 5.1 and 1 of 20 for Fable 5. Astra averaged 2.5 minutes per trial at an estimated cost of $0.94 per run, while Fable 5.1 averaged 6.8 minutes at $2.12. On the second task of picking up a round blue puzzle piece by its center knob and placing it into a matching circular groove, Astra completed the insertion 2 times out of 20, matching Fable 5.1's score of 2 out of 20 while costing $1.36 per run compared to Fable 5.1's $2.18. A total of 120 trials were evaluated using a human grader rubric measuring the highest stage each run reached. Limitations noted in the testing include non-interleaved trial dates, the use of a different physical rig for the Fable bowl trials because rig-3 was unavailable, unblinded operator grading that leaves scores open to unconscious bias, manual resetting of objects between trials, a medium reasoning effort setting across all models, and cost calculations based on list prices where OpenAI automatically cached roughly one-fifth of Astra's inputs while Anthropic requests were sent without prompt caching.
