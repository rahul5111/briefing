═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Following deadly flash floods on the border of Nepal and China on August 26, questions are growing over Chinese reporting and restricted access to the disaster area in Tibet's Gyirong County. Nepalese authorities quickly released detailed information on missing foreigners, while Chinese authorities took five days to disclose that 261 missing foreigners come from 23 countries. Nepal has reported over 1,300 dead and nearly 5,000 missing, whereas China reports over 40 dead and more than 500 missing. The International Campaign for Tibet claims at least 300 homes were destroyed and that the actual casualty count could be much higher, noting that displaced Tibetans have been placed in tightly controlled holding areas with limited communication. Unlike Nepal, which provided immediate access to journalists and international aid agencies, China restricts foreign media in the region, granting only a carefully arranged visit for a small number of outlets after a week of requests. David Bandurski with the China Media Project noted that state outlets like Xinhua and CCTV maintain total information dominance, stating that controls have tightened steadily over the last decade to levels that are unprecedented. Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun denied that China is understating casualties, stating at a briefing in Beijing that China has been open, transparent, and timely in its information release, and adding that smear and disinformation is most unwelcome at a time of disaster response. State media coverage has focused heavily on rescue operations and government response rather than impacted villagers, while commercial media outlets have largely refrained from independent reporting and instead reprinted official Xinhua content.
