═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

Three years after the September 8, 2023 earthquake that struck Morocco’s Al Haouz region, killing over 2,900 people and injuring more than 5,500, survivors face starkly contrasting realities in their recovery efforts. 

In the High Atlas Mountain village of Ighermane, 72-year-old Mohamed Ait Belaid received 80,000 dirhams, roughly 7,300 euros, in partial state support to rebuild his home, adding 100,000 dirhams of his own. According to Omar Saeed of the Ighermane Association for Development and Solidarity, about 90% of the village is now rebuilt, with 114 of roughly 150 families receiving state aid. 

However, in nearby Azgour, 72-year-old Larbi Ait Jelloulat remains displaced in front of his destroyed home, unable to secure state assistance due to bureaucratic disputes over his residency status. "I want to know where my rights have gone," Ait Jelloulat said. "Who took what's rightfully mine? For three years, I've been going from one department to another, from one complaint to another, from one petition to another, just looking for an answer."

Nationwide, the High Atlas Development Agency stated in March that 54,425 homes have been built or rehabilitated, with over 3,000 still under construction. The government has distributed more than 7.2 billion dirhams, about 656 million euros, in reconstruction funds and emergency aid. Marrakech-based architect Mohamed Belmejjad defended the pace given geographical and logistical hurdles, noting that remote mountain locations, debris clearance, transport difficulties, and a lack of local labor complicated the work.

Despite this progress, the National Coordination for Al Haouz Earthquake Victims has received complaints from over 3,000 families excluded from state help due to issues like residency classification or tenant status. Victims continue to demand independent investigations and accountability, with upcoming general elections on September 23 prompting renewed political promises that many residents view with skepticism.
