═══ 01_DRAFT ═══

A new study published September 6, 2026, in the journal Aging-US has linked higher blood levels of the amino acid tyrosine to a shorter lifespan in men. Researchers from the University of Hong Kong and the University of Georgia analyzed data from more than 270,000 participants in the UK Biobank using observational data and Mendelian randomization, a genetic analysis technique. The findings indicate that elevated tyrosine levels could potentially reduce male life expectancy by nearly a year. No significant effect was detected in women, and men generally showed higher baseline levels of tyrosine than women. Phenylalanine, another amino acid examined in the research, showed no association with lifespan in either men or women after controlling for tyrosine. As stated by the study authors, phenylalanine showed no association with lifespan in either men or women after controlling for tyrosine. Tyrosine is found naturally in protein-rich foods and is widely sold as a dietary supplement to boost focus and mental performance. However, the researchers did not directly test tyrosine supplements, and the study does not show that taking a supplement shortens lifespan. Potential biological explanations for the findings include links to insulin resistance and stress-response hormones, though these mechanisms remain hypotheses requiring further investigation. The findings could eventually help scientists understand differences in aging and longevity between the sexes, but the authors emphasize that much more research is needed before the results can be translated into health or dietary recommendations.
